## 2026-05-29 — A board reviewer's feedback sheet may pre-date the current draft; diff against the live draft, not the sheet

**Källa:** K2C subcontracts — Mattias Wiking board-review sheet vs the post-2026-05-06 local drafts
**Kategori:** process + contract_review
**Taggar:** stale_review, board_feedback, draft_drift, K2C, verify_current_state

A board member's contract-review spreadsheet flagged six items across six subs (convenience termination missing, 45-day support, no AI clause, 30-day cure, etc.). Four of the six were **already fixed** in the local working drafts from an earlier flow-down pass — the reviewer had read the older uploaded Gdocs, not the current drafts. Treating the sheet as the spec would have meant "re-fixing" already-correct clauses and missing the items that genuinely were new (a retainer→hourly conversion, a court→arbitration switch, a signatory swap, a travel cap).

**How to apply:** when a reviewer's feedback sheet arrives, first grep the *current* draft for each flagged item and mark each cell already-satisfied / genuinely-open BEFORE editing. Present that reconciliation to the client ("most of this is already in; here's the true delta") — it's faster, avoids churn, and surfaces that the reviewer was looking at a stale copy (worth telling them so the next review is against the right version). Mirrors [[feedback_verify_live_sheet_vs_memo]] and the "live Gsheet beats local CSV" learning — same principle, applied to contract clauses.

## 2026-05-29 — UK counterparty: SCC arbitration clause shape (Expedited threshold + confidentiality undertaking)

**Källa:** K2C / Red Marmoset Studios Limited (UK 13367034) — court→arbitration switch per board feedback
**Kategori:** contract_review + swedish_corp
**Taggar:** arbitration, SCC, NYC_convention, UK_counterparty, enforceability, confidentiality, seat_stockholm

When AP (Swedish) contracts a UK Ltd and wants enforceability, swap the Stockholm-District-Court clause for SCC arbitration. The clause shape that worked here, keeping AP on home turf while staying NYC-Convention-enforceable against the UK entity:

- Governing (substantive) law: Sweden, "without regard to its conflict-of-laws rules."
- Forum: Arbitration Institute of the Stockholm Chamber of Commerce (SCC). **Rules for Expedited Arbitrations** below a threshold (used SEK 1,000,000), full **Arbitration Rules** above it or where the SCC so determines on complexity. The expedited tier keeps small disputes cheap — important for a ~700k SEK contract where full three-arbitrator SCC would be disproportionate.
- Seat: Stockholm. Language: English.
- **Confidentiality undertaking** on the proceedings — arbitration is private, which is the *affirmative reason* to prefer it here: a public court process could leak Upstream-publisher (RF) confidential material and put AP in breach upstream. Carve out disclosure required by law / to enforce rights / as needed to perform obligations to the upstream publisher.

Also update any belt-and-suspenders jurisdiction sentence in the preamble (Imi's draft had one pointing at the District Court) and confirm Section 15 is in the survival list so the arbitration clause survives termination.

**Why arbitration here and not for the Swedish subs:** for the five Swedish-AB counterparties, Stockholm District Court is fine and cheaper — a Swedish judgment is directly enforceable domestically. Arbitration's enforceability premium only pays off against the foreign (UK) entity. Don't blanket-convert; apply per counterparty domicile. (Extends 2026-05-21 K2C learning #4.)

## 2026-05-29 — Converting a flat retainer to pure hourly: three coupled edits, not one

**Källa:** K2C / Oskar Skokloster — Phase 2 retainer→hourly per Robert direction
**Kategori:** contract_review
**Taggar:** hourly_conversion, retainer, absence_clause, K2C, fee_structure

Switching a contractor from a flat monthly retainer to hourly-for-actual-hours is not just a rate line — three clauses move together or the contract contradicts itself:

1. **Compensation / Exhibit A:** rate + the fee total reframed from a fixed fee to a *reference estimate* ("billed for hours actually worked; total is a planning reference, not a fixed fee"); a fixed-amount monthly table becomes an *expected-hours* table (here: P&L monthly amounts ÷ hourly rate), with "hours materially above the monthly expectation need written pre-approval" as the spend control.
2. **Absence mechanics (Section 5):** the pro-rata-reduction clause (which only makes sense against a retainer) becomes N/A; the hourly carve-out clause is widened to govern the **whole Term**; state explicitly "Contractor is not paid for time not worked, including vacation, leave or illness" — that *is* the client's "if he's away he covers it himself" intent.
3. **Preamble + Duration:** any description of the engagement as "flat monthly retainer from August" must be reworded, or a reader hits a contradiction between the preamble and Exhibit A.

Run a grep for "retainer" across the whole draft after the edit — a Duration line still said "flat monthly retainer" after the Exhibit A table was already converted. Easy to miss because it's prose, not a numbered clause.

## 2026-05-29 — v3 review-copy pipeline: awk-strip changelog, upload --convert --html, rename via Drive PATCH

**Källa:** K2C — producing 6 fresh Gdoc review copies from edited local .md drafts
**Kategori:** tooling
**Taggar:** gdrive_upload, google_docs, changelog_strip, batch_upload, rename, K2C

End-to-end recipe that worked for turning edited local contract .md files into clean Gdoc review copies in a target folder, without leaking the HTML changelog comments:

1. Strip everything before the contract body: `awk 'found{print} /^# OUTSOURCING AGREEMENT/{found=1; print}' draft.md > clean.md` — robust because it drops the leading `<!-- changelog -->` comment(s) AND any markdown working-notes (Lost Hive had notes that weren't in a comment block).
2. Upload+convert: `node assistant/gdrive-upload.js clean.md <folderId> --convert --html` (the `--html` flag makes pipe-tables render as real GDoc tables). Capture the returned doc ID.
3. The created Gdoc keeps the ".md" in its title; rename via Drive API PATCH `https://www.googleapis.com/drive/v3/files/<id>?supportsAllDrives=true` body `{"name":"..."}`, OAuth token refreshed from `~/.claude/.gdrive-server-credentials.json` (refresh_token) + `~/.claude/gcp-oauth.keys.json` (client_id/secret). A small one-off node script does the whole batch.

Verified the conversion by reading one doc back with `mcp__gdrive__gdrive_read_file` — headings, the milestone pipe-table, mailto links and the new arbitration clause all rendered. One cosmetic wart: markdown `---` horizontal rules convert to a small "## ---" heading line; harmless for a review/signature doc but strip the `---` separators first if you want it pristine. Keep the original v2 Gdocs untouched as the baseline (Lawyer rule: never overwrite originals).

## 2026-05-29 — Harmonise a contract package to one uniform term with a single named exception, not a spectrum

**Källa:** K2C subcontracts — Robert: "no substantial diffs between the contracts except the Skokloster one on payment (5 working days after RF payment)"
**Kategori:** contract_review + process
**Taggar:** harmonisation, uniform_terms, pass_through, payment_terms, K2C, package_consistency

When a client says contracts in a package "should be the same except for X," the goal is **one canonical clause shared verbatim across the set, plus one explicitly-flagged exception** — not a near-miss spread (net 10 here, net 30 there, a 5-day fallback somewhere else). A package where each contract is *slightly* different on the same point is a due-diligence smell and an inconsistency a counterparty can play off against the others. Here the five pass-through subs were unified to "Client pays within 5 business days after receiving the corresponding RF pass-through payment," and Skokloster (hourly, RF-decoupled) is the one stated exception.

Two drafting wins from the unification: (1) AP never pays a sub ahead of RF funds (the board's float concern), and (2) the operative term is byte-identical across the five, so a single external-counsel review covers all five and the board can reason about the package as one shape. Replace the *primary* term, don't just leave the desired behaviour as a fallback clause — the earlier drafts already had "5 business days after receipt" but only as a fallback under a net-30 primary; the client wanted it to BE the term.

**How to apply:** when harmonising, (a) write the one canonical clause, (b) grep the whole package for the old variants (`net 30`, `net 10`, `net-payment term`) and confirm they survive ONLY in the named exception, (c) state the exception explicitly in each doc's changelog and the cover memo so nobody later "fixes" the intended difference. Re-generate all affected review copies together (delete the superseded uploads) so the folder never shows two live versions.

## 2026-05-29 — A paying-agent group entity can't invoice itself; its own fee is settled by set-off against the IC balance it owes the principal

**Källa:** K2C / Robert-CZP outsourcing agreement — CZP is AP's paying agent AND a subcontractor to AP
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + contract_review + swedish_tax
**Taggar:** paying_agent, betalningsombud, intercompany, set_off, kvittning, self_invoice, ABL_8_23, K2C, CZP

When a group entity acts as the **paying agent** that invoices the upstream publisher and collects the proceeds on the principal's behalf (here CZP invoicing Raw Fury for AP, because AP lacks F-skatt/VAT yet), and that *same* entity is also a **subcontractor** to the principal (CZP providing Robert's exec-producer services to AP for 880k), the agent's own fee **cannot** run through the normal "sub invoices principal, principal pays sub" mechanic — the agent would be invoicing itself for money it is already holding.

The correct mechanic: the agent holds the collected proceeds as an **intercompany payable owed to the principal** (the project P&L accrues as a debt the agent owes the principal at any given time). The agent's own fee is **deducted / set off (kvittning) against that IC balance**, milestone-by-milestone as each upstream payment lands — no invoice, no separate payment from principal to agent. The set-off discharges the principal's payment obligation *pro tanto*.

Drafting consequences in the agent's own sub agreement:
- Replace the pass-through "principal pays sub within N days" clause with a "settlement by set-off against the IC balance under the Paying-Agent Agreement" clause; state plainly "cannot invoice itself, no separate payment."
- Keep entitlement conditional on the agent having received the corresponding upstream payment (so fee doesn't accrue before proceeds exist).
- Point detailed mechanics to the Paying-Agent Agreement (where the ABL 8:23 self-dealing safeguard for the same-person-both-sides signature already lives — cross-ref the 2026-05-06 learning); the sub references it rather than reproducing it.
- This is a SECOND payment exception in a package otherwise harmonised on a single term — flag it explicitly in changelog + cover memo so it isn't "corrected" back to the uniform clause by a later editor.

**How to apply:** whenever the same group entity is both paying agent and a counterparty in the chain, check its own fee clause first — if it still says "Contractor invoices Client / Client pays Contractor," it's wrong. Convert to set-off against the IC balance. The external subs (who are genuinely paid by the agent on the principal's behalf) keep the normal pass-through term.

## 2026-05-21 — K2C subcontract package: back-to-back flow-down gaps in six-contractor review

**Källa:** K2C Sands of Duat (apb-009, Mattias Wiking review of 6 subs: Lost Hive, Red Marmoset, Oskar, Fredrik, Tim/Bright Gambit, Robert)
**Kategori:** contract_review + K2C
**Taggar:** back_to_back, flow_down, convenience_termination, AI_prohibition, travel_costs, payment_terms, signatory_governance

Mattias Wiking's board-level review spreadsheet identified **six material gaps** in the K2C sub package that must be closed before signature:

1. **Convenience termination missing across all six** — AP can be terminated by RF with 30d notice but cannot terminate subs. Direct margin exposure. Must add to all subs: "Company may terminate for any reason with 30d notice, paying for accepted work through notice date + wind-down."

2. **Support period 45 days vs RF master 3 months** — Gap of ~45 days uncompensated work per sub (~€5-10k aggregate). Trigger event also ambiguous (GM vs 1.0 vs revenue threshold). All six subs must align to 3 months from 1.0 launch.

3. **AI prohibition reps missing across all six** — RF 7.2.3 requires rep that no generative AI was used. Subs currently silent. Adds compliance risk if any sub used AI secretly. All six need identical rep language (with or without carve-out for incidental non-primary assets, depending on Robert's decision).

4. **Arbitration vs regular court for UK Ltd** — Red Marmoset (UK 13367034) has "vanlig domstol" (Swedish court). UK court judgments are difficult to enforce against UK-domiciled entities. Red Marmoset needs LCIA arbitration (seat London/Stockholm) to be enforceable under NYC Convention.

5. **Payment structure inconsistencies:**
   - Lost Hive: pass-through invoicing even if AP hasn't received RF funds (risk: AP carries float, owes Lost Hive on default).
   - Oskar: monthly retainer despite phase-1 being hourly (Mattias objects; missing clawback mechanism if Oskar underutilizes retainer).
   - Tim (Bright Gambit): NO UPPER LIMIT on travel/accommodation (open-ended cost exposure; Tim could book suites and AP pays all).
   - Robert: NET 10 vs all others NET 30 (inconsistency; should harmonize to NET 30 per RF schedule).

6. **Signatory governance failures (ABL 8:23 related-party safeguard):**
   - Tim contract: cannot be signed by Andreea (Tim is tied to Bright Gambit; independent directors Mattias/Alexander must sign).
   - Robert contract: cannot be signed by Andreea (Robert is the subject; independent directors must sign).

**How to apply:** When a board member surfaces a multi-contractor review spreadsheet flagging flow-down gaps, treat each column as a **systematic test against the master**. Use the four-clause checklist (termination cure/convenience, post-release support, AI reps, travel cost caps) as your baseline. When gaps exist across multiple subs at once, it often means the template drafts were based on an older or incomplete master reference — pull the signed master and re-baseline all drafts. For multi-contractor packages with governance issues (related-party signatories), confirm board approval minute will explicitly disclose the related-party transaction + independent-director sign-off per ABL 8:23.

**Cost of not addressing:** Each gap has direct margin loss or legal exposure: convenience termination gap = unwind exposure on RF termination; support period gap = 45 days uncompensated work; travel cap gap = unbounded cost overrun; signatory failures = board/fiduciary liability if a self-dealing transaction isn't disclosed.

## 2026-05-21 — Autonomous review "verification" claims need cross-checking against accessible evidence before accepting

**Källa:** Elias / Cold Pixel PR Agreement entity confusion (eli-029)
**Kategori:** process + contract_review
**Taggar:** autonomous_review, entity_verification, conflicting_evidence, escalation

An autonomous 4am agent claimed to have "reviewed Kristofer's suggestions" and "verified" the contracting entity as "Elias Software AB / 556959-4194" on 2026-05-20. A later session "corrected" this to "House of Elias AB / 559403-0156" claiming verification "against the signed BizDev Agreement." But the accessible BizDev Agreement DRAFT shows "Elias Software AB / 556959-4194", and the project README confirms that as the legal entity. The "signed BizDev Agreement" cited in the correction cannot be found.

**Two compounding problems:**
1. Autonomous agents **cannot read Google Doc suggestions** (lawyer_learnings 2026-05-15), so any autonomous review claiming to relay "Kristofer's corrections from the doc" has no reliable source.
2. When an autonomous review claims "verified against [document]" but that document contradicts the claim or cannot be found, the "verification" is unreliable.

**Red flags that should trigger entity re-verification before signature:**
- Conflicting org numbers across project files (README vs draft contract)
- A "correction" that references an unfindable signed precedent
- Brand name vs legal entity confusion ("House of Elias" vs "Elias Software AB")
- Autonomous review claiming verification against inaccessible sources (Google Doc comments, unfindable signed agreements)

**How to apply:** When an autonomous review memo or prior session claims entity verification, cross-check against ALL accessible evidence before accepting:
1. Project README org number
2. Any findable precedent agreements (BizDev, MNDA, prior contracts)
3. Gmail threads mentioning the counterparty's legal name
4. If evidence conflicts, escalate to `needs_input: true` and `taskType: critical` - using the wrong legal entity is a contract enforceability failure, not a typo.

Don't defer this check to CorpBot at sign-time - catch it at legal review. Wrong entity = potentially void contract.

## 2026-05-20 — Holdingbolag som tjänstekontraktspart: kolla verksamhetsföremålet först

**Källa:** BADASS / CZP Holding PM-kontrakt
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + contract_review
**Taggar:** verksamhetsföremål, bolagsordning, holdingbolag, ABL 8:42, konsulttjänster

When Robert asks to use a named AB as the contracting party for a *services* contract, check that company's **verksamhetsföremål** in the bolagsordning before drafting. Creation Zero Point Holding AB's registered objects are holding-company-only: "äga och förvalta aktier och andelar i dotter- och intressebolag samt kapitalförvaltning." Providing PM/consulting services sits **outside** that.

It does not void the contract toward a good-faith counterparty (ABL 8:42 protects the third party), but it is a governance + tax-tidiness problem: a holding company invoicing operating/consulting income is exactly what a tax auditor or a later konkursförvaltare queries.

**How to apply:** Pull the registreringsbevis, read the Verksamhet line. If the entity is a pure holding company and the contract is for operating services, flag it and recommend a bolagsstämma resolution broadening the verksamhetsföremål + Bolagsverket filing (CorpBot territory) before signature. Don't silently draft around it.

## 2026-05-20 — A bankrupt entity's contract cannot be mirror-swapped; the new one must stand alone

**Källa:** BADASS / APDS konkurs
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + contract_review
**Taggar:** konkurs, konkursbo, novation, standalone contract, APDS

When the contracting entity of an existing agreement is in konkurs (here APDS AB), that agreement is property of the konkursbo. You cannot novate it or "swap the party name" to a live entity unilaterally - that needs the konkursförvaltare. The correct move is a **genuinely new, standalone contract** with the live entity that explicitly does NOT continue, assume, or amend the bankrupt entity's agreement (put that disclaimer in the Entire Agreement clause). Mirror the *terms*, never the *continuity*. Also confirm: which entity invoiced/was paid for prior work, and whether the new contract's retroactive coverage conflicts with that paper trail.

**How to apply:** On "create a new contract mirroring the old one but with a different contractor," first establish whether the old contractor is solvent. If bankrupt: standalone fresh contract, explicit clean-break clause, and the prior-work billing history must be checked before any retroactive-coverage clause. Real-lawyer review warranted for claw-back risk on work performed near the konkurs window.

## 2026-05-20 — Producing a revised Google Doc working copy: upload-convert-rename pipeline

**Källa:** Elias / Cold Pixel PR & Marketing Agreement v2 revision
**Kategori:** tooling
**Taggar:** gdrive, google_docs, working_copy, redline, gdrive-upload

The gdrive MCP has no Google Docs *write* tool — only `gdrive_read_file` / `gdrive_search` / `gsheets_*`. It also returns the document **body only, not inline comments or suggestions** (reinforces the 2026-05-15 suggestions-inaccessible learning). So when a task says "update this Google Doc," the Lawyer agent cannot edit the live doc or read the counterparty's comments directly.

The working path to deliver a revised copy:
1. Edit the contract as a local `.md` in the project's `contracts/` folder (a corrected local `.md` often already exists and is *ahead* of the live Doc — check it first).
2. Find the target Drive folder: `node assistant/gdrive-upload.js --info <originalFileId>` gives the `Parents` folder ID.
3. Upload + convert: `node assistant/gdrive-upload.js <file>.md <folderId> --convert --html` → creates a native Google Doc. `--html` pre-renders markdown so `**bold**` headers render properly.
4. The new Doc inherits the source filename **including the `.md` extension** in its title. gdrive-upload.js has no `--rename`. Fix with a Drive API PATCH reusing the script's OAuth creds (`~/.claude/.gdrive-server-credentials.json`, refresh against `gcp-oauth.keys.json`): `PATCH https://www.googleapis.com/drive/v3/files/<id>?supportsAllDrives=true` body `{"name":"..."}`.

This keeps the original Doc untouched (Lawyer rule: never overwrite originals) and lands a clean working copy in the project's `legal/` Drive folder. When the counterparty's comments can't be read, ask Robert to paraphrase them — don't guess.

## 2026-05-20 — Multi-payer time-allocation clause: cap the contracting client, route the rest to a named separate agreement

**Källa:** Elias / Cold Pixel PR & Marketing Agreement — Kristofer Eng wanted 30h split 20h Client / 10h CZP Holding
**Kategori:** contract_review
**Taggar:** time_allocation, third_party_payer, separate_agreement, broker_deal, verksamhetsforemal, CZP

When a services contract's hours are funded partly by the contracting client and partly by a third party, draft it as a **cap on the contracting client**, not as a tripartite payment clause. Clean four-touch pattern:

1. **New clause** stating the total scoped hours and the split — e.g. "Services scoped at 30h; up to 20h payable by the Company; the remaining 10h payable by [third party] under a separate agreement between [third party] and Service Provider, to which the Company is not a party."
2. **Cap the client:** "The Company's payment obligation under this Agreement is limited to the hours allocated to the Company."
3. **Qualify the invoicing clause** so the client is invoiced only for client-allocated hours; third-party hours invoiced under the separate agreement. Otherwise a generic "invoice Company for hours worked" clause contradicts the cap.
4. **Mirror in the Schedule** and require the time report to split hours by payer.

Keep the third party a *non-party* to the contract being drafted — it's still a broker/two-party deal; the third party's funding lives in its own agreement.

**Two flags whenever the third-party payer is a holding company (here CZP Holding AB):** (a) the separate agreement must actually exist or be drafted — a dangling reference is a defect; (b) a registered holding company paying for operating services sits outside its verksamhetsföremål (cross-ref the 2026-05-20 BADASS PM learning) — flag it for the *separate* agreement, not the one being drafted.

**Real lawyer should review:** the interaction between the capped-client clause and the (not-yet-drafted) separate CZP↔Cold Pixel agreement, to confirm no gap where hours 21–30 are worked but unfunded.

## 2026-05-20 — Verify the contracting entity against the signed precedent, not an autonomous review memo

## 2026-05-13 — Insolvency termination clauses create hidden revenue-forfeiture exposure in publishing deals

**Källa:** IndieCade Climate Jam publishing agreement review (db-126)
**Kategori:** contract_review + swedish_corp
**Taggar:** insolvency, termination_for_cause, revenue_forfeiture, publishing_deals, publishing_risk

Publisher-side publishing agreements (where the publisher is receiving revenue share) increasingly include a new clause: unilateral termination right if the publisher becomes insolvent or enters bankruptcy. The risk is that **this interacts dangerously with "termination for cause" forfeiture clauses**.

**The pattern:**
- Section 10.4 (or similar): "Either party may terminate immediately upon written notice if the other party becomes insolvent, unable to pay debts, files bankruptcy (not dismissed within 60d), or has receiver appointed."
- Section 10.2 (termination for cause): "If Client terminates for cause, Publisher forfeits all rights to revenue share, deferred payments, and unpaid compensation."
- **The gap:** 10.4 does NOT explicitly state whether insolvency termination is "cause" or "convenience." A court would likely treat it as cause (since it's unilateral, immediate, no cure period), which means the terminating party gets to exit with zero compensation exposure.

**Practical exposure for the Publisher (AP's perspective):**
- If AP hits any financial distress (temporary payroll issue, delayed funding, balance-sheet spike), the Client can terminate immediately
- AP loses all rights to *future* revenue share, even for work already completed and accepted
- This is a hidden free-exit option Client gets the moment Publisher looks weak

**Why this matters:** Publishing contracts run 2+ years. A 30-person studio financing itself on advance + revenue share is at risk from this language because **any temporary cash shortage could trigger it**, even if the studio is fundamentally viable.

**How to fix it:**
1. Make 10.4 insolvency termination = "termination for convenience" (AP retains revenue rights for all completed work)
2. Add cure period and notice requirement (e.g., "30d written notice + 30d cure opportunity before termination effective")
3. Tighten insolvency trigger (require actual bankruptcy filing/not dismissed, or receiver appointed; remove "unable to pay debts as they become due" — too vague)
4. Add explicit carve-out: "Termination under 10.4 shall not trigger the forfeiture provisions of 10.2"

**How to apply:** When reviewing publisher-side publishing agreements, always check: (a) does the contract have an insolvency-termination clause? (b) how does it interact with termination-for-cause forfeiture? (c) what counts as "cause"? If the interaction is unclear, propose a fix at review stage, not at crisis stage.

## 2026-05-13 — Approval/consultation rights without tiers create operational gridlock in publishing

**Källa:** IndieCade Climate Jam agreement, Sections 1.2 & 3.2
**Kategori:** contract_review + process
**Taggar:** approval_rights, governance, publishing, decision_authority, operational_risk

Publishing agreements increasingly give the Client approval or consultation rights over "publishing services" (Section 1.2) and specific decisions like pricing/platform negotiations (Section 3.2). The risk is **vague scope + no decision tiers = operational gridlock**.

**The pattern in the IndieCade agreement:**
- 1.2: AP must perform services "subject to the approval rights of Indiecade as found herein" — but the contract doesn't define what those approval rights are. Vague.
- 3.2: AP can adjust pricing and enter platform deals "in consultation with Client" and "after reasonable input and consultation with Client" — two different standards, not synced.
- No response timelines, no escalation rule, no guidance on when "consultation" becomes "approval veto."

**Why this matters:**
- AP can't move quickly on seasonal promotions, price adjustments, platform launches, or marketing campaigns without Client feedback
- If Client is slow or unresponsive, decisions get bottlenecked
- If Client and AP disagree, there's no tiebreaker rule — potential deadlock
- The vagueness means different people at Client might interpret "consultation" differently

**Operational impact:**
- Example: Steam seasonal sale window opens in 48h. AP wants 15% discount. AP sends message to Client for "consultation." Client takes 5 days to respond. Steam window closes. Missed opportunity.
- Example: GOG expresses interest in exclusive window for 30 days. AP wants to negotiate. "After reasonable input and consultation" — but Client doesn't respond in time. Deal falls through.

**How to fix it:**
Propose a governance matrix:
1. **Routine decisions** (notification after, no approval needed):
   - Pricing adjustments within ±10%
   - Social media, community management
   - Standard campaigns within quarterly budget plan
2. **Material decisions** (30d notice, Client input considered, AP retains final decision):
   - Platform launches (console, mobile, new storefronts)
   - Marketing campaigns >25% over quarterly budget
   - Pricing outside ±10% band
   - Major partnerships
3. **Strategic decisions** (mutual written agreement):
   - Game scope changes (DLC, expansions)
   - License assignment
   - Termination of primary distribution

**Add response rule:** "Client shall respond to material-decision notifications within 10 business days with approval or substantive objection. Absence of timely response = approval. If Client objects on reasonable grounds, the Parties shall confer; AP retains final decision authority."

**How to apply:** When reviewing approval/consultation clauses, always ask: "What decisions does Client actually care about?" Then tier them. Routine notifications should be fast, material decisions should have a response deadline, strategic decisions should require true agreement. The goal is to protect Client's interests without paralyzing Publisher's ability to execute. Most Clients, once you tier their concerns, will accept that they get real input on major decisions but not veto on everything.

**Cost if not negotiated:** MODERATE. Not a dealbreaker, but it slows decision cycles. AP will either move slow (less competitive), pre-coordinate everything (more communication overhead), or operate with low visibility (risks surprises). A tiered approach is usually acceptable to Clients once you frame it as "we want your input on the things that matter."

## 2026-05-06 — UK SME inbound mutual NDA: three-tell pattern for re-skinned one-way templates

**Källa:** Formula Drone Ltd NDA review (db-091, AP AB)
**Kategori:** contract_review
**Taggar:** NDA, MNDA, UK_law, signing_entity, indemnity, term_length, founder_grade

A UK founder-/SME-grade "mutual NDA" sent ahead of fundraising disclosure is a high-frequency artifact in biz-dev. Three tells consistently flag a doc that was *originally drafted as a one-way NDA and reskinned* — and these are the only things worth pushing on in a light review:

1. **Asymmetric indemnity clause.** Body uses "Discloser/Recipient" generically (looks mutual) but the indemnity clause names "Party 1" specifically. The doc-author search-and-replaced the operative obligations but left one or two clauses on the original side. Fix: replace with "the Discloser" or strike — most market mutual NDAs carry no indemnity at all.
2. **Personal-name signing block on the receiving side.** Counterparty drafts often name the human at the AP-side address (e.g. "Robert Bäckström of Aurora Punks at Timmermansgatan 43") rather than the legal entity. This binds the human personally. Always re-state as the AB with org. nr., signed by the human as authorised signatory. *This is the biggest one — it's not negotiable, even on a "light" review.*
3. **Term length 5 years post-termination.** UK SME default; market norm for fundraising-stage finance/deck NDA is 2-3 years. Trade-secret-grade info survives indefinitely under Sweden's FHL (`lag om företagshemligheter` 2018:558) regardless of contractual term, so shortening costs nothing.

**Things that are fine and not worth raising on a UK inbound MNDA:**
- English law + exclusive jurisdiction of England & Wales courts (Clause 20-equivalent). Standard. Push for non-exclusive only when it's the underlying commercial contract, not the NDA.
- "No licence granted, no IP rights transferred" clause as the first numbered term. This is the *correct* shape — protects AP IP. Don't strike it; counterparty drafts that lack it are worse.
- Standard carve-outs (public domain / prior knowledge / independent development / compelled disclosure). Confirm all four are present in the definition + a separate compelled-disclosure-with-notice clause.
- "Confidential information remains the sole property of the Discloser." Standard ownership-of-info clause; doesn't transfer IP.

**How to apply:** For inbound mutual NDAs from UK SME counterparties, run the three-tell scan first (asymmetric indemnity, personal-name signing block, 5-year term). If only those three issues exist, send a single short reply with the three fixes — don't redline. If a fourth issue surfaces (residuals carve-out, non-solicit, exclusivity, IP-grant clause), escalate to a proper redline pass.

## 2026-05-06 — Sweden has no work-for-hire; "no licence granted" clauses in NDAs are protective, not restrictive

**Källa:** Formula Drone NDA review, restatement of swedish_ip principle
**Kategori:** swedish_ip
**Taggar:** upphovsrätt, NDA, IP_grant, sv_vs_uk_us

Worth re-stating because it comes up every NDA review: Swedish copyright law (`URL` 1960:729) has no true work-for-hire doctrine. ## 2026-05-04 — Paying-agent (betalningsombud) is the lightest of three intra-Swedish IC bridge structures

**Källa:** K2C Sands of Duat / k2c-003 (AP signs RF, CZP bills)
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + swedish_tax + contract_review
**Taggar:** ABL 17:1, IL 14:19, IL 16:1, IL 36, ABL 8:23, paying-agent, betalningsombud, kommissionärsbolag, intercompany

When a Swedish AB group needs Entity A to hold a contract while Entity B fronts the billing (typical when A's VAT/F-skatt registration is pending), three frameworks apply:

1. **Paying-agent / disclosed agency (betalningsombud).** Lightest. A is contractual party end-to-end; B is authorised by written IC agreement + notice to upstream counterparty to invoice / receive / disburse on A's behalf. B holds **no economic interest** (book inflows as IC payable, not revenue). No fee unless explicitly agreed (and any fee must satisfy ABL 17:1 business motivation). **Default choice** when the upstream contract permits invoicing through "affiliated companies or subcontractors."
2. **Kommissionärsförhållande (IL 36 kap. 1–4 §§).** Six conditions; commission AB's profit taxed at kommittent. Rejects cleanly when commission AB has substantial non-commission activity (fails condition 5).
3. **Sub-license / sub-contract.** Two priced contracts, creates an internprissättning surface even within Sweden (ABL 17:1 + IL 16:1 scrutiny on the AP↔B price). Heaviest.

**Why paying-agent is specifically tax-clean within Sweden:** IL 14:19 international arm's-length principle does not bind two Swedish AB. ABL 17:1 olovlig värdeöverföring still applies but is satisfied by the stated business motivation in the IC agreement (registration-bridge). IL 16:1 avdragsrätt is unaffected because there is **no priced transaction** under disclosed agency — only an IC payable booked at agent AB. Skatteverket has no comparator to challenge.

**Practical implementation (k2c-003 template, 2026-05-04):**
- 2-3 page AP↔CZP Paying-Agent Agreement (8 clauses): appointment, scope/limits, cash flow + reconciliation, no-fee + ABL 17:1 motivation, termination, board approval / self-dealing safeguard, governing law, notices. Schedule of covered sub agreements.
- Notice letter from principal AB to upstream counterparty naming the agent + invoicing/payment/performance/notices/revocation. Reference the upstream contract's affiliated-companies clause.
- Section 2.4 paying-agent clause in every downstream sub agreement (already in K2C subs since CorpBot 2026-04-22).
- Self-dealing safeguard (ABL 8:23): when same person signs both sides, principal-side signature is by other directors and dual-role person abstains from principal-board vote.

**Templates live at:** `umbrella/k2c_sands_of_duat/Legal/` (memo + agreement draft + notice letter draft). Promote to `czp_legal/templates/PayingAgent/` once instance count ≥ 3.

## 2026-05-04 — Personlig borgen för dotterbolag — varken sysslomansregress eller arbetsrätt ger automatisk indemnification mot CZP/holdingbolag

**Källa:** APDS-konkursen / Roberts personliga borgen (umbrella/aurora_punks/legal/apds_borgen_recourse/)
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + swedish_employment + swedish_tax
**Taggar:** borgen, regress, HB 18:4, ABL 17:1, 3:12, värdeöverföring, koncernintern, VD-konsult

När Robert som VD/firmatecknare för ett dotterbolag (APDS) tecknat **personlig borgen** för bolagets krediter, och sedan tvingats betala kreditgivarna när dotterbolaget gått i konkurs — kan han **inte** automatiskt vältra över förlusten på sitt eget holdingbolag (CZP) som hyrde ut honom som VD-konsult. 1. **Avtalsenlig indemnification finns inte i standardstrukturen.** CZP↔dotterbolag-konsultavtalet (mall: WLBS 2022-06-01, gdrive `1Ubp-pzJn9VB_OIHeub81-10uqAO3lrETWFZKtyBYlp4`) har bara D&O-försäkringskrav (pkt 4) och en ansvarsfriskrivning som *skyddar* CZP, inte tvärtom. 2. **HB 18 kap. 4 § (sysslomansregress)** fungerar inte konstruktivt. Borgen tecknades till förmån för dotterbolaget = huvudman. Roberts regress *materialiseras redan* som fordran på konkursboet. Att kläm in CZP som mellanled saknar stöd i borgensavtalets ordalydelse.

3. **Skadeståndslagen 3:1 (principalansvar)** avser arbetsgivarens ansvar för skada anställd vållar tredje man — inte ersättning för anställds *egna* finansiella åtaganden.

**How to apply:** Vid VD-konsulter/uthyrningar i koncern, granska konsultavtalet för indemnification-klausul vid första granskning. ## 2026-05-04 — Privat avdrag för regressfordran efter infriad borgen — IL 48:24, 70 %, RÅ 2001:57 styr

**Källa:** APDS-konkursen / verifierad mot HFD-praxis och Skatteverkets rättsliga vägledning
**Kategori:** swedish_tax
**Taggar:** correction, IL 48:24, IL 44:8, regressfordran, borgen, kapitalförlust, RÅ 2001:57, RÅ 2003:12

**Korrigering av tidigare felcitering:** Initialt citerade jag "IL 54 kap. 6 §" för kapitalförlust på fordran — det är fel kapitel (utländsk valuta). Korrekt regelkedja för **privatpersons kapitalförlust på regressfordran efter infriad borgen** är:

- `IL 41 kap. 1 §` — kapitalförlust i inkomstslaget kapital
- `IL 44 kap. 8 §` — fordran avyttrad när definitivt värdelös → vid bolagets **konkursbeslut** (inte vid slutlig utdelning)
- `IL 48 kap. 24 §` — **70 % avdrag** för icke marknadsnoterad fordringsrätt
- Omkostnadsbelopp = **infriat borgensbelopp** (inte fordrans marknadsvärde) — `RÅ 2001 ref. 57`
- Efterföljande konkursutdelning = tilläggsersättning år den erhålls

**Avgörande gränsdragning (affärsmässig grund vs personlig levnadskostnad):**

- `RÅ 2001 ref. 57` (borgen för **eget bolag**) → **avdrag medgivet**, 70 % kvotering. Delägarskap räcker som affärsmässig grund.
- `RÅ 2003 ref. 12` (borgen för **närstående persons** bolag) → **avslag**, personlig levnadskostnad. Presumtion mot avdrag vid borgen till närstående.

**How to apply:** När klient infriat personlig borgen för bolag och regressfordran är värdelös pga konkurs — pröva först om bolaget är (a) klientens eget/gruppbolag = sannolik avdragsrätt, (b) närstående persons bolag = svår avdragsrätt. För (a): yrka avdrag i deklarationen för det inkomstår konkursbeslutet fattats; bilägg dokumentation för affärsmässig grund (ägarandel, roll, kreditgivarens krav). Saknad borgensavgift försvagar men diskvalificerar inte. För framtida liknande åtaganden — föreslå klient att prissätta borgensavgift för att stärka affärsmässighet och skapa skattepliktig inkomst för borgensman + avdragsgill kostnad för bolaget.

**Lärdom om citeringsdisciplin:** När jag är osäker på vilket IL-kapitel som styr — verifiera mot Skatteverkets rättsliga vägledning *innan* jag citerar, inte efteråt. Felaktig lagrumshänvisning i ett första-skissmemo undergräver klientens förtroende när det måste rättas.

**Tonen i wordingen är dock viktig.** Felaktig formulering kan signalera "inte mitt bord" och provocera motparten. Rätt formulering: "har internt överlämnats till bolagets VD som driver dagliga verksamheten, för åtgärd". Det visar internal handover, inte distansering.

**Alternativt:** dubbelsignering kan användas vid extra känsliga ärenden — ordförande för legalsubstansen, VD för det operativa åtagandet. Lämpligt när motparten kan tänkas läsa "ordförande står inte bakom åtgärden" i en singel-signering.

**How to apply:** I legalbrev från klient som inte är operativt aktiv i bolaget (vanligt vid styrelseordförande-roller eller vid utlånade VD-konsulter), beskriv operativa åtaganden som "internt överlämnat" snarare än "vi kommer göra X" om det inte är klienten själv som gör X. Rekommendera dubbelsignering ordförande + VD om motparten är en jurist eller fackförbund — det stänger en angreppsyta.

## 2026-05-03 — Ideell rätt går inte att överlåta blankt — URL 3 § 3 st

**Källa:** Aurora Punks / Robot Lord Rising IP-dispyt (Scen & Film propå 2026-04-22)
**Kategori:** swedish_ip
**Taggar:** URL 3, ideell rätt, namngivelse, anställningsavtal, fackförbund

URL 3 § 3 st: *"Med bindande verkan kan upphovsmannen eftergiva sin rätt enligt första eller andra stycket endast såvitt angår en till art och omfattning begränsad användning av verket."*

En bred IP-överlåtelseklausul i ett anställningsavtal (typ "alla rättigheter, inklusive copyright, är bolagets exklusiva egendom") kan giltigt överlåta **ekonomisk rätt** men kan **inte** giltigt eftergiva ideell rätt generellt. Eftergift av ideell rätt måste vara *specifik till art och omfattning*. Anställd som skapat verk under anställning behåller rätten att namnges, även om all ekonomisk rätt giltigt överlåtits.

**Praktisk implikation:** I tvister från fackförbund (Scen & Film, Sveriges Författarförbund, KLYS-medlemmar) som åberopar ideell rätt — anställningsavtalets IP-klausul är **inte** ett försvar. Strategi blir efterlevnad inom scope, inte stridsläge.

## 2026-05-03 — Skyldighet enligt URL 3 § ligger hos den som framställer/tillgängliggör — inte hos IP-ägaren

**Källa:** RLR Scen & Film-ärendet
**Kategori:** swedish_ip
**Taggar:** URL 3, namngivelse, ansvarsfördelning, licensgivare

URL 3 § knyter namngivelseplikten till den som *framställer exemplar* eller *gör verket tillgängligt för allmänheten*. Det betyder att i en kedja IP-ägare → licensgivare → förlag → återförsäljare så ligger den primära skyldigheten på **förlaget** (för exemplar och förlagets sajter) och **återförsäljaren** (för deras sajter).

IP-ägaren (här: AP AB som spel-IP-ägare) är **inte** primärt ansvarig för seriealbumets exemplarframställning eller tillgängliggörande. AP:s ansvar är **kontraktuellt** mot sina licenstagare att de följer URL — inte direkt URL-mässigt mot upphovspersonen för seriealbumet.

**How to apply:** Vid namngivelse-anspråk i en publishing-kedja, kartlägg vem som faktiskt utför handlingen. Adressera svar och åtgärder mot rätt motpart per kanal. Avgränsa upstream IP-ägaren från frågor som gäller downstream tillgängliggörande.

## 2026-05-03 — Proportionalitet under "god sed" är ett legitimt försvar mot allt-eller-inget-yrkanden

**Källa:** RLR Scen & Film-ärendet
**Kategori:** swedish_ip
**Taggar:** URL 3, god sed, proportionalitet, namngivelse

URL 3 § skriver uttryckligen: *"i den omfattning och på det sätt som god sed kräver"*. Det är en proportionalitetsstandard inskriven i lagrum.

God sed för namngivelse varierar mellan kanaler:
- Bok kolofon: obligatoriskt
- Förlag/utgivare egna produktsidor: obligatoriskt
- Återförsäljarsajter: förlagsansvar att förmedla korrekt metadata
- Marknadsföringsinlägg på sociala medier: hänvisning till verket räcker, inte fullständig credits-lista
- Trailers/ads: end-credits ja, mellan-cuts nej
- Pressreleaser: huvudcredits

Vid yrkande om "namngivelse vid varje exemplar/tillgängliggörande" → svaret kan med fog avgränsa enligt god sed-standard utan att bestrida URL 3 § principiellt.

**How to apply:** När fackförbund eller upphovsperson yrkar maximal namngivelse, hänvisa till URL 3 § "god sed" och diff:a kanaler. Det är legitimt försvar; inte en flykt.
