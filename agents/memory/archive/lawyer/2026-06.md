## 2026-06-17 — Licensing user-tied consumer data to AI buyers: purpose-limitation is the master gate, anonymisation is the only clean exit

**Källa:** RankOne (rko) — data-rights scoping memo for the AI-data pivot (license ~101k users' curated game-preference data + 413k reviews to AI labs / data buyers)
**Kategori:** gdpr
**Taggar:** [RankOne rko, 2026-06-17], purpose_limitation, Art_5_1_b, anonymisation, Recital_26, EDPB_28_2024, legitimate_interest, consent, Chapter_V, SCC, DPF, AI_Act, UGC_copyright, URL

Recurring pattern whenever a client wants to monetise an existing consumer dataset by licensing it to third parties (AI buyers, data marketplaces). The analysis is the same every time:

1. **Purpose limitation (Art. 5(1)(b)) is the master gate, not lawful basis.** Data collected for purpose X (build a profile / run the app) cannot be repurposed to an *incompatible* new purpose (license to external AI). Two cures only: **fresh consent** for the new purpose, or **true anonymisation**. There is NO "we updated the privacy policy" cure — a unilateral policy edit does not retroactively legitimise data collected under the old purpose. State this first; clients reflexively assume "we'll just change the ToS."

2. **Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) fails the reasonable-expectations limb** for third-party licensing of identifiable consumer data. Per **EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (17 Dec 2024)**, LI can cover AI processing only after a documented balancing test AND only within data-subject expectations — external licensing of identifiable taste/preference data is outside expectations. LI is fine for the client's *own internal* analytics, never for external licensing. Don't let a client build the deal on LI.

3. **Anonymisation is the only route out of GDPR scope; pseudonymisation is NOT** (Recital 26 explicit — pseudonymised data is still personal data). The "anonymous" bar is the **"means reasonably likely to be used"** test (Recital 26) and per EDPB 28/2024 is **case-by-case** — aggregation/name-stripping is NOT automatically anonymous. Two killer traps in any rich consumer dataset: (a) **high-dimensional uniqueness / singling-out** (a longitudinal multi-attribute per-user vector is usually unique — Netflix Prize / AOL re-identification failure mode); (b) **free-text fields** (reviews/comments carry self-identifying content in the body — stripping the author ID does nothing). Anonymous product = aggregates protected by k-anonymity / differential privacy + a documented re-identification-risk assessment (that assessment IS the EDPB-expected evidence).

4. **Two paths, decide early — it's a product decision with legal consequences:** **Path A anonymised-aggregate** (exits GDPR entirely, no consent campaign, sidesteps Chapter V transfer, but less granular) vs **Path B identifiable/pseudonymous** (richer per-user product, but needs unbundled **opt-in** consent re-paper — opt-out is invalid — granular toggle, easy withdrawal, updated Arts 13-14; withdrawal-fragile and a buyer who trained on withdrawn data has an unfixable problem). Default-recommend Path A. Model Path B corpus at realistic opt-in attrition (20-40%), not 100%.

5. **Non-EU buyers (US AI labs) = Chapter V (Arts 44-49):** adequacy via **EU-US DPF** (verify buyer is DPF-certified) or **SCCs (Art 46) + transfer impact assessment** (post-Schrems II). DPF has stability questions; SCCs are the durable fallback. **All of this vanishes if the data is genuinely anonymous** — second strong reason to favour Path A.

6. **AI Act (Reg (EU) 2024/1689):** the data *supplier* is not an AI "provider," so direct obligations sit with the buyer — BUT GPAI training-data transparency duties (live since 2 Aug 2025; full applicability + Commission enforcement 2 Aug 2026) mean buyers will **diligence the rights chain hard** and demand provenance + rights warranties. Clean rights = a sales asset, not just compliance cost. (Verified 2026-06-17 via EC digital-strategy + artificialintelligenceact.eu.)

7. **UGC text (reviews) is a DOUBLE gate — copyright AND GDPR, independently.** Under URL (1960:729) Sweden has no work-for-hire; copyright vests in the user-author. The platform can sublicense review text onward ONLY if the ToS licence grant is **broad + sublicensable** ("perpetual, worldwide, sublicensable") — a narrow "to operate the service" grant doesn't reach licensing. A perfect copyright sublicence still does NOT solve GDPR (the text is also personal data). Moral rights (URL 3 §, ideell rätt) can't be assigned blanket. **Always check the ToS licence-grant wording before assuming the platform can resell UGC.**

**How to apply:** For any "license our user data to AI / data buyers" brief: (1) the single highest-priority step is **read the actual live ToS + privacy policy** — stated purposes, any existing third-party/partners disclosure, the UGC licence-grant breadth/sublicensability, and what users actively tick vs passively accept. Treat the project as legally unscoped until that text is read. (2) Frame the verdict as VIABLE-WITH-CONDITIONS and force the Path-A-vs-B decision early. (3) Gate any buyer LOI on rights posture existing first. (4) Escalate to a licensed dataskyddsjurist/advokat for sign-off on the anonymisation methodology (A) or consent mechanics + buyer licence drafting (B) before any binding commitment. The memo template lives at `projects/rankone/drafts/rankone_data_rights_assessment.md` — reusable shape for the next data-licensing question.

**Also flag the user-trust collision** (non-legal but co-load it): "we sell your data to AI" can spook the engaged users who ARE the dataset. The anonymised-aggregate framing is both the legally cleaner and the trust-cleaner story — they align, so lead with it.

## 2026-06-17 — Harmonising a clause across a sub package: copy the gold-standard wording byte-for-byte, vary only the slot word

**Källa:** K2C Sands of Duat — Andreea board-redline pass on the 6 subcontractor source drafts
**Kategori:** contract_review + K2C + process
**Taggar:** harmonisation, change_of_scope, paying_agent, currency, uniformity_check

When a board member (here Andreea Chifu) flags a clause as "unclear / risks charging for normal milestone work," the fix is usually not bespoke per-sub rewording — it's **lift one already-correct formulation and replicate it verbatim**, varying only the one slot that must differ (here the discipline word: production/design/programming/art "work"). The Lost Hive "Change of scope" Special Term was the gold standard: *milestone delivery + bug-fixing + RF-requested rework within RF-agreed scope is included at no charge; only genuinely new scope >5 working days triggers a written amendment.*

**Two process wins worth keeping:**
1. **Verify uniformity by hash, not by eye.** `grep` the operative sentence out of each file, `sed` out the known-varying word, `md5sum` — identical hashes prove the wording is truly uniform modulo the intended variation. Caught two stale artifacts the prior pass left: a duplicated "Change of scope: Change of scope:" label prefix in two subs, and an unbolded label in a third. The substance was fine; the uniformity check surfaced the cosmetic drift that a contract reviewer would read as sloppiness.
2. **A clause that *looks* like the flagged one may be a different artifact — don't auto-extend the edit.** Andreea's "AP cannot invoice" objection targeted the CZP self-referential set-off rationale ("because CZP cannot invoice itself"). The four EXTERNAL subs carry a milder "while AP's VAT/banking are being finalised (autumn 2026)" framing in their §2.4 paying-agent *designation* — same surface words ("VAT", "finalised"), different function (it's the operative designation + signals temporariness to the external contractor). I trimmed the CZP draft per the scoped instruction and **flagged** the external-sub framing as a Robert decision rather than silently extending. When a redline instruction names one doc, check whether the "same" clause elsewhere is actually the same legal animal before broadening.

**How to apply:** For any multi-contract harmonisation: (a) identify the canonical wording, (b) replicate verbatim varying only the necessary slot, (c) hash-verify uniformity as the last step, (d) when the flagged clause has look-alikes in sibling contracts, confirm they serve the same function before extending the edit — otherwise flag, don't assume.

## 2026-06-17 — "Substitute vs add" is the right shape for a fixed-fee named-resource clause

**Källa:** K2C — Imi/Red Marmoset (draft_05) additional-resources clause, Robert's default
**Kategori:** contract_review
**Taggar:** fixed_fee, named_resource, substitution, additional_services, K2C

A fixed-fee sub priced on a named individual (here Imi as Art Lead, full-time) needs the additional-resources clause to distinguish two cases that the prior draft collapsed into "all additional personnel at no charge":
- **Substituting** the named resource (or a previously approved one) with an equivalent resource → **no additional charge** (the fee already bought that capacity; who fills the seat is the contractor's discretion).
- **Adding** resources *beyond* the named scope → **Additional Services**, additional fee agreed in writing before work commences (this is genuinely more capacity than the fee bought).

The earlier "any additional personnel at no charge" wording was a giveaway — it let the contractor scale up headcount for free, which is never what a fixed fee priced on one FTE intends. The substitute/add split is the market-correct default; tie the "add" branch to the existing Additional Services / Amendment clause (here §2.2) rather than inventing a parallel mechanic.

**How to apply:** Whenever a fixed-fee sub is priced on a named person but the body lets the contractor "bring in additional staff," check the cost treatment splits substitution (free) from net additions (priced). Flag the default as confirmable — Robert's was the substitute-free/add-priced shape, but the client may want all-inclusive for a flat-rate creative engagement.

## 2026-06-17 — Don't bake a temporary operational rationale into a long-lived contract

**Källa:** K2C — CZP/Robert (draft_01) paying-agent editorial trim, Andreea feedback
**Kategori:** contract_review + swedish_corp
**Taggar:** paying_agent, set_off, ABL_8_23, drafting_hygiene, editorializing

Andreea's note on the CZP outsourcing agreement: keep the operative paying-agent / set-off **mechanism**, but strip the explanatory editorialising — "because CZP cannot invoice itself" and "until AP's VAT registration and banking infrastructure are finalised (expected completion autumn 2026)." Her point is sound drafting hygiene: a contract may outlive the operational reason it was set up a particular way. If the autumn-2026 VAT activation slips, or the structure persists for other reasons, a recital that says "this exists because X is temporarily true" becomes either stale or an admission a counterparty can exploit. State the mechanism plainly and operatively ("CZP shall not invoice AP for its fee; the fee is settled by set-off against the IC balance...") without the causal justification.

**How to apply:** When reviewing intra-group or bridge-structure clauses, separate (1) the operative mechanism — keep, and (2) the "we do this because [temporary condition]" rationale — cut or move to the internal memo / board minute, not the contract face. The board's approval rationale (ABL 17:1 business motivation, ABL 8:23 self-dealing safeguard) lives in the per-capsulam minute + the Paying-Agent Agreement, not in every downstream sub.

## 2026-06-17 — perl in-place exact-string replace is the clean tool for a repeated-string fix across many contract drafts; it invalidates the Edit-tool read cache

**Källa:** K2C — AP address fix (Bondegatan 32 → c/o Bäckström, Bondegatan 31) across 8 files
**Kategori:** tooling + process
**Taggar:** perl, bulk_edit, address_fix, edit_cache

For a mechanical exact-string replace repeated across many files (here the AP registered address, identical string in preamble + notices of 7 drafts + the paying-agent agreement), `perl -i -pe 's/OLD/NEW/g' file...` in one Bash call is faster and less error-prone than per-file Edit calls — but **it modifies files out-of-band**, so any file you'd Read earlier now fails the Edit-tool "file modified since read" guard and must be re-Read before further Edit. Sequence the work: do the bulk perl pass FIRST, then Read + Edit the files that still need substantive (non-mechanical) changes. Verify with `grep -rn OLD` (should only hit change-log notes documenting the fix) + `grep -rl NEW` (should list every in-scope file).

**Address-correction gotcha:** the prior pass (2026-06-15) had "corrected" AP to Bondegatan **32**; the actual correct address was Bondegatan **31** with a **c/o Bäckström** prefix. A second redline pass re-correcting a field the previous pass already touched is a real pattern — don't assume the last value is right just because it was recently changed. And ⚠️ always carry the registreringsbevis-verification flag: the 2024-12 Bolagsverket cert still showed the OLD Timmermansgatan/säte Kramfors, so the new address is per-Robert, not yet registry-confirmed.

## 2026-06-17 — Convenience termination in a back-to-back sub: replace bare "for any or no reason" with the upstream-cancellation trigger

**Källa:** K2C Sands of Duat — Tim Browne's APPROVED §14.2.2 redline applied to all 6 subs (draft_01–06)
**Kategori:** contract_review + K2C
**Taggar:** back_to_back, convenience_termination, flow_down, upstream_agreement, RF, uniform_clause

When a back-to-back sub gives the Client (AP) a convenience-termination right "for any or no reason," a sophisticated counterparty (here Tim, on his own contract) will push back: a convenience-out is fine but it needs a **substantive reason** behind it, not bare convenience. The clean fix is **not** to delete the right — it's to re-anchor it to the actual back-to-back risk being managed: **the upstream publisher (RF) terminating or materially descoping the Upstream Agreement** such that AP no longer needs the sub's services. This both satisfies the contractor's objection AND is the more accurate clause — the only reason AP would ever pull a sub for "convenience" is that RF pulled or cut the master.

**The wording that worked (uniform across all 6 K2C subs, 2026-06-17):**
> 2. By Client on cancellation or descoping of the Upstream Agreement. Client may terminate this Agreement and/or any Schedule hereto, in whole or in part, by providing thirty (30) days written notice to Contractor (...), in any of the following circumstances: (a) Raw Fury AB terminates the Upstream Agreement, or terminates the corresponding milestone, scope or work package thereunder, such that Client no longer requires the Services or the affected part of the Services; (b) Raw Fury AB materially reduces, descopes or suspends the milestone, scope or work package under the Upstream Agreement to which the Services relate; or (c) the title is cancelled, shelved or indefinitely suspended by Raw Fury AB. In such cases, Client will pay Contractor for work completed and accepted up to the termination date on a pro-rata basis. Contractor has no corresponding right of termination for convenience.

**Three things to preserve when doing this conversion (don't lose them in the rewrite):** (1) the notice period (30 days here), (2) pro-rata pay for work completed AND accepted to the termination date, (3) Client-only direction — the contractor gets no reciprocal convenience right. Keep it as a companion to, not a replacement for, the §14.2.1 for-cause clause (which stays untouched). Add "in whole or in part" so a partial RF descope can cascade to a partial sub termination, not an all-or-nothing.

**Anchor to the draft's existing defined term.** Each K2C sub already defined the RF master as the "**Upstream Agreement**" in its back-to-back recital; the new ground must reference that exact term, not invent a parallel one. Check the recital before drafting the trigger.

**How to apply:** When asked to harmonise an approved convenience-termination redline across a package of back-to-back subs: (1) confirm the old clause is byte-identical across all drafts first (`md5sum` on the clause line) — if it is, the replacement can be a single uniform string; (2) apply the same new clause to every draft; (3) verify with a single md5 of the new clause line across all files (one hash = uniform) + a `grep -L 'for any or no reason'` remnant check; (4) flag any draft whose clause was structured differently (none here — all six were identical). Note the downstream consequence in the report: re-render every rendered Gdoc AND void-and-resend any already-circulated eSignature batch (the redline invalidates in-flight PDFs).

**Process note (file-state guard):** a `replace_all` Edit only works within one file; for the same string across N files you still need one Edit call per file, and each target file must have been Read in-context first (the first draft_01/03/04/05/06 Edits failed with "File has not been read yet" until Read). Reading just the ~5-line clause region per file is enough to satisfy the guard cheaply.

## 2026-06-15 — Game-audio buy-out: "no payment to any collecting society" overclaims; use a STIM/NCB/SAMI non-registration warranty + direct sync licence instead

**Källa:** K2C Sands of Duat — Carolina Foghammar sound-design sub (draft_07 + Route-B Exhibit B draft_07b)
**Kategori:** swedish_ip + contract_review
**Taggar:** music, audio, buyout, STIM, NCB, SAMI, sync_licence, collecting_society, work_for_hire, URL, K2C

A PM-drafted music/audio sub assigned IP to AP and added "...such that Client and Raw Fury may exploit the audio without further payment to Contractor or **to any performing-rights or collecting society**." That last clause is legally false under Swedish law and should always be cut:

1. A composer/performer who is a **STIM** (performing + mechanical via NCB) or **SAMI** (neighbouring/performer rights) member has already granted those societies a separate administration mandate. A bilateral game contract **cannot** override or terminate that mandate — the society relationship is independent, and the composer's public-performance royalty share is collected and paid by the society directly, regardless of any buy-out.
2. The enforceable mechanism for a full game-audio buy-out is three-part:
   - (a) **Full assignment** of economic copyright + neighbouring rights in compositions, master recordings, stems, multitracks, SFX (define them as "Audio Works"), plus — to the extent assignable — sync + mechanical reproduction rights.
   - (b) A **non-registration warranty**: composer warrants the cues are not and will not be registered with / placed under the administration of any CMO (STIM/NCB/SAMI/equivalent) in a way that would let the CMO claim payment from, or impose a licence on, the client/publisher/platform for the in-game + marketing sync/reproduction. This is what actually protects AP/RF from a society claim against the game's exploitation.
   - (c) A belt-and-braces **perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, exclusive, royalty-free, paid-up, sublicensable synchronisation licence** for any right that can't be assigned outright.
   - Plus an express carve-out acknowledging that any **third-party** public-performance remuneration a society separately collects (e.g. from a broadcaster) and pays the composer is a society↔composer matter, not a client cost. This keeps the clause honest and avoids a composer/STIM dispute later reading back onto AP.

**How to apply:** Any music/audio/composer sub in Sweden — strike "no payment to any collecting society" and drop in the assignment + non-registration warranty + sync licence triad. Define "Audio Works" once and reference it. The non-registration warranty is the load-bearing clause for a games buy-out; the assignment alone doesn't stop a STIM-registered cue from generating sync/performance claims against the game.

## 2026-06-15 — Route-B "Exhibit to existing master" beats a standalone sub when the resource is an employee of an already-contracted studio

**Källa:** K2C — Carolina routed via Ark Island Studio AB (Fredrik's company), appended as Exhibit B to draft_03 rather than a new agreement
**Kategori:** contract_review + swedish_ip
**Taggar:** exhibit, master_agreement, assigned_resource, work_for_hire, URL, IP_chain, side_letter, K2C

When a new contributor is an **employee/assigned resource of a studio AP already has a master with**, the clean structure is an **additional Exhibit on that master**, not a fresh sub. The master's "additional Exhibits may be incorporated" clause + a precedence clause (Exhibits prevail in alphabetical order) means the new Exhibit inherits all general terms / RF flow-downs / governing law and only has to vary scope + model + fee. No new self-dealing analysis, no duplicated boilerplate.

**The one latent gap to flag every time (URL no-work-for-hire):** the master warrants the studio's *employees* do the work and the studio *owns* the IP. But Sweden (URL 1960:729) has no work-for-hire — authorship vests in the natural-person creator and must be **expressly assigned** to the studio before the studio can pass clean title up to AP (and onward to the publisher). So confirm the resource's own employment/engagement terms with the studio carry a present written IP assignment (+ moral-rights waiver + CMO non-registration). If unknown, add a one-line **side-letter** confirmation from the studio that it holds the assigned IP "sufficient to perform its assignment obligations under Section 3." Without it the upstream assignment chain has a hole that only surfaces in DD.

## 2026-06-15 — Junior/intern contractor without F-skatt = AP withholding + reclassification exposure; prefer employ-via-studio route

**Källa:** K2C — Carolina came in as an AP Academy LIA intern, likely no company
**Kategori:** swedish_tax + swedish_employment + contract_review
**Taggar:** F-skatt, A-skatt, arbetsgivaravgifter, enskild_firma, contractor_vs_employee, LAS, junior, K2C

Contracting a **private individual without F-skatt** as a "subcontractor" is a trap: Skatteverket treats the payment as salary-like — the payer must withhold A-skatt and pay arbetsgivaravgifter (~31.42%), and a single-principal, principal-directed, principal-tooled engagement scores several contractor-vs-employee factors toward **employment** (LAS exposure), especially for a junior on a first gig. The 80k fee would balloon ~+31% in employer cost.

Decision tree for "individual, maybe no company":
- Has own AB, or **enskild firma WITH F-skatt** → standalone sub, pay gross vs invoice, clean.
- No F-skatt → either (a) she registers enskild firma + F-skatt first (offer Route A only after F-skatt confirmed), or (b) **route her through a studio that employs her** (here Ark Island) and contract the studio — materially cleaner and the recommended default for a junior.

**How to apply:** Before drafting any "contractor" agreement with an individual, ask: company or F-skatt? If neither, don't paper it as a sub — recommend employment-via-an-existing-studio-counterparty or insist on F-skatt registration first. Same check applies to any first-time/junior contributor.

## 2026-06-15 — Fixed-price deliverables sub needs a deemed-acceptance backstop the FTE template lacks

**Källa:** K2C — adapting Fredrik's FTE master (draft_03) into a fixed-price audio package (draft_07)
**Kategori:** contract_review
**Taggar:** fixed_price, deliverables, deemed_acceptance, payment_trigger, back_to_back, K2C

When converting an FTE/retainer sub template to a **fixed-price deliverables** model, removing the FTE availability/absence/pro-rata clauses (Sections 5.1–5.4) is correct — but a pure pass-through payment trigger ("invoice on Client's confirmation of RF acceptance") can leave the contractor's payment stranded if the Client never gets around to relaying acceptance. Add a **deemed-acceptance** clause: if the Client neither relays a rejection nor a revision request within N business days **after the Client has itself received RF's upstream acceptance**, the deliverable is deemed accepted *as between the Parties* for invoicing. Anchor it to *upstream* acceptance so it never shortens or front-runs the RF timetable — it only stops indefinite downstream deferral. Proportionality/good-faith under Swedish contract law supports this and it costs AP nothing it isn't already on the hook for upstream.

**How to apply:** Every fixed-price deliverables sub gets a deemed-acceptance backstop tied to upstream acceptance. FTE/retainer subs don't need it (payment is time-based, not deliverable-gated).

## 2026-06-15 — Change-of-scope trigger must be anchored to upstream-agreed scope, not "days added to a milestone"

**Källa:** K2C subcontracts — Andreea Chifu board feedback
**Kategori:** contract_review
**Taggar:** change_of_scope, back_to_back, RF_master, AP_protective, milestone, K2C

The K2C sub drafts had a change-of-scope clause reading "*Any request adding more than five (5) working days of [X] work to a milestone triggers a written amendment.*" Andreea flagged the real defect: as written it lets a contractor treat **milestone completion and bug-fixing** (i.e. work already inside the RF-agreed scope) as billable extra, because *any* >5-day request trips the amendment. The fix is to anchor the trigger to **work outside the scope agreed with the upstream publisher**: in-scope delivery, RF-requested revisions, and rework to bring a milestone to acceptance are included at no extra charge; only a genuine addition of *new* scope >5 working days triggers an amendment + agreed fee.

**How to apply:** In any back-to-back sub, the extra-fee/change trigger must reference the upstream-agreed scope document, not "days added to a milestone." Otherwise the clause inverts the back-to-back logic and exposes the prime (AP) to paying twice for getting one milestone accepted. When you find this clause in one sub, it is almost certainly copy-pasted across the package — fix it in all subs that carry it (here: Robert/Tim/Fredrik/Lost Hive; Imi + the hourly Oskar didn't have it). Anchor phrase used: "*within the scope agreed between Client and Raw Fury … including bug-fixing, revisions, and rework requested by Raw Fury to bring a milestone to acceptance.*"

## 2026-06-10 — Förvaltarberättelse-svar: gäldenär-kanal är smal, bevara den hedgade tonen

**Källa:** APDS-konkursen, förvaltarberättelse K 4429-25 (Carler/Nils Åberg)
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + process
**Taggar:** konkurs, förvaltarberättelse, gäldenär, återvinning, KBR, intressekonflikt, samarbetsvilja

När konkursförvaltaren ger styrelseledamoten i konkursgäldenären chans att kommentera förvaltarberättelsens **utkast** innan inlämning till tingsrätt, är den rätta tonen **samarbetsvillig förtydligande av faktauppgifter, inte partsargumentation**. Förvaltaren rapporterar till Tillsynsmyndigheten (TSM) och kan vid misstanke hänskjuta frågor till åklagare; en stridslysten eller överambitiös gäldenärsinlaga riskerar att väcka fel uppmärksamhet.

**Vad som hör hemma i gäldenär-svaret:**
1. Sakrättelser i skuld-/tillgångssammanställning (belopp, motpart, datum)
2. Erbjudande om kompletterande underlag (avräkningshistorik, avtal, kvitton) som förvaltaren saknat
3. Påpekande av redan dokumenterade motfakta som förvaltaren noterat själv (t.ex. "ingen verkställighet", "löpande skattebetalningar") — utan att bestrida förvaltarens bedömningsutrymme
4. Förmildrande omständigheter som är objektivt verifierbara (t.ex. "CZP lämnade merparten av sin fordran obetald", "närstående konsultavtal signerat av oberoende styrelseledamot")

**Vad som INTE hör hemma i gäldenär-svaret:**
1. Återvinningsförsvar i sak (t.ex. "betalningarna till CZP var inte otillbörliga enligt KL 4:5 eftersom...") — det är en separat process mellan boet och motparten; motparten kan ha egen rådgivare
2. KBR-försvarsargumentation enligt ABL 25:14 (t.ex. "immateriella tillgångar hade verkligt värde X") — dubbeleggat mot boets tillgångsjakt och RLR/IP-tvisten
3. Skadestånds-/ABL 29:1-motargument — det är en separat, potentiellt adversarial process
4. Personliga borgensrecourse-argument — rättsförhållandet klient↔borgensman↔boet är separata frågor

**Den hedgade tonen i utkastet är en tillgång, inte ett problem.** Åberg skrev "kan antas", "sannolikt", "går inte att utesluta", "utredningen pågår" — mjukt, villkorat språk. Det är förvaltarens skyldighet att rapportera misstankar, men han är inte åklagare. Målet är att de mjuka formuleringarna **behålls** och att förmildrande omständigheter som redan finns i rapporten **står kvar och syns**. Att försöka "vinna striden" i gäldenärskanalen kan göra mer skada än nytta.

**Intressekonflikten.** När gäldenärsföreträdaren också är (via sitt holdingbolag) återvinningsmotpart OCH personligt exponerad för KBR-/skadeståndsansvar, kan hen inte använda gäldenärskanalen för att driva sin egen försvarsposition. De tre rollerna företräds av olika kanaler. Återvinnings- och skadeståndsförsvaret kräver oberoende obeståndsadvokat — inte som "kvalitetssäkring" utan för att det är skarp personlig exponering med miljonbelopp i spel.

**Dokumentsamlingsprocess.** Förvaltarens "underlag saknas"-noteringar är ofta en inbjudan till samarbete snarare än en anklagelse. Att snabbt inkomma med underlag (avräkningshistorik konto 1675, konsultavtal, borgensspecifikationer) kan omklassificera poster från "misstänkt närståendebetalning" till "ordinär låneåterbetalning" — konkret substansförbättring. Deadline-driven dokumentinhämtning (här: 2 dagar) kräver tydlig checklista + IPC-alert om dokument saknas och hämtas utanför agentens räckvidd (Fortnox-utdrag, fysiska avtal).

**How to apply:** Vid framtida förvaltarberättelse-utkast: (1) läs för mjuka hedgade formuleringar — behåll dem, (2) identifiera sakrättelser + underlagserbjudanden (det är gäldenärs-safe zone), (3) flagga återvinnings-/KBR-/skadeståndsargument som OUT OF SCOPE för gäldenärskanalen, (4) kartlägg intressekonflikter tidigt (gäldenär vs. motpart vs. personligt ansvar), (5) skicka aldrig något utan att oberoende obeståndsadvokat granskat de känsliga delarna först.

## 2026-06-09 — Förvaltarberättelse-utkast: gäldenärens synpunktsfönster ≠ partsprocess, och dubbelrollen är den verkliga risken

**Källa:** APDS-konkursen / förvaltarberättelse-utkast K 4429-25 (Nils Åberg, Carler), Robert ensam APDS-styrelseledamot 2025
**Kategori:** swedish_corp + process
**Taggar:** konkurs, förvaltarberättelse, KL 7:15, obestånd, kontrollbalansräkning, ABL 25:13, ABL 25:14, ABL 25:18, ABL 29:1, KL 4:5, KL 4:10, återvinning, närstående, intressekonflikt, KBR

En förvaltarberättelse (7 kap. 15 § KL) är **förvaltarens eget** dokument till tingsrätt + TSM. Gäldenären "invänder" inte mot den som mot ett beslut. När förvaltaren delar ett **utkast** är det ett courtesy-fönster att (1) rätta sakfel och (2) se till att hedgade formuleringar behålls - **inte** att driva partsargument. Förvaltaren rapporterar till TSM och kan hänskjuta misstanke till åklagare; en stridslysten gäldenärsinlaga väcker fel uppmärksamhet, särskilt när ställföreträdaren redan bekräftat bouppteckningen på heder och samvete (11 kap. BrB).

**Förvaltaren binder sig sällan.** Åberg skrev uttryckligen att KBR-tidpunkten "kan antas", "utgör ett antagande", och att det "ankommer på varje enskild borgenär att vidare utreda och styrka". Obestånd: "sannolikt ... kan inte uteslutas". Detta mjuka språk är guld - målet blir att bevara det, inte att slåss.

**Den verkliga risken var dubbelrollen, inte berättelsen.** När klienten är (a) gäldenärsföreträdare, (b) återvinningsmotpart via eget bolag, och (c) potentiellt KBR/skadeståndssvarande - kan inte alla tre företrädas via samma kanal. Återvinningsförsvar (CZP) och KBR/skadeståndsförsvar (privat) ska köras separat, helst med oberoende rådgivare (inte boets byrå).

**Obeståndsförsvar - motindikationer som ofta redan står i förvaltarens egen text:** frånvaro av betalningsföreläggande (enskilt mål), frånvaro av verkställighet (UB 4 kap.), löpande löne- och skattebetalningar, och - viktigt - om obeståndsankaret är en **närståendefaktura** (att inte betala sin egen ägares holdingbolag i tid ≠ oförmåga att betala externa borgenärer). Förstärk dessa; kräv inte ett bestämt senare datum (= partsinlaga).

**KBR / ABL 25:18 - det dubbeleggade 25:14-argumentet:** en KBR räknas inte på bokfört EK utan på verkligt värde enligt ABL 25:14. Bokförda immateriella tillgångar (här spel-licenser 3,27 MSEK netto, konto 1030) kan göra att en korrekt KBR visat EK intakt → ingen materiell 25:18-brist trots formell underlåtenhet. MEN: att hävda IP-värde drar åt motsatt håll om (a) konkursbouppteckningen tar upp tillgångar till ~noll (inbjuder boet att jaga IP:t) och (b) det finns en parallell IP-ägandetvist (RLR). Kör 25:14/IP-strategin med advokat innan något sägs - lyft den ALDRIG oförsiktigt i gäldenärskanalen.

**Närstående-återvinning, tidsgränser:** KL 4:5 (otillbörlighet) har **ingen bakre tidsgräns** för närstående (4:3+4:5); KL 4:10 (betalning av skuld) = **2 år** för närstående. Sen betalning av normal faktura i normala pengar träffas inte självklart av 4:10 - ett faktiskt försvar på fakturabetalningar. Bästa konkreta åtgärden mot "underlag saknas" på avräkningsposter (konto 1675): ta fram avräkningshistoriken → omklassar "otillbörlig närståendebetalning" till "ordinär låneåterbetalning". Samarbetsvilligt, vilket är rätt ton mot förvaltaren.

**How to apply:** Vid förvaltarberättelse-utkast till en klient som var ställföreträdare: (1) kartlägg klientens samtliga roller i konkursen (företrädare/återvinningsmotpart/ansvarssubjekt) och flagga konfliktern först; (2) skilj sakrättelser (säker gäldenärskanal) från partsförsvar (separat, advokat); (3) leta motindikationer i förvaltarens egen text och be att de vägs in + att hedgningen behålls; (4) håll svaret till förvaltaren kort, samarbetsvilligt, icke-stridande; (5) eskalera till oberoende obeståndsadvokat när personligt 25:18/29:1-ansvar och intressekonflikt mot boet föreligger - det är inte en "kvalitetssäkring", det är skarp exponering.
