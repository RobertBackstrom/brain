## 2026-04-30 — WMAY/Shosha porting subcontract: AP-signs-via-CZP-billing pattern (re-use)

**Learning (re-use the K2C Section 2.4 paying-agent clause for AP-side contracts where billing flows through CZP):** When Robert's instruction is "AP signs the contract, CZP bills," the cleanest implementation is to lift the K2C Oskar Master Agreement Section 2.4 ("Payment via AP's designated paying agent") nearly verbatim. Adjust three things only: (i) the project context line in the preamble (here: WMAY co-pub vs K2C upstream RF agreement), (ii) the parties' roles in the orientation (in K2C Oskar, AP is Client and the clause designates CZP as AP's paying agent on the *outbound* side; in WMAY this contract, AP is Service Provider and the clause designates CZP as AP's paying agent on the *inbound* side — receiving payment from Shosha rather than paying out to a sub), (iii) the timing trigger ("while AP's VAT registration is being finalised" still applies). The clause's structural feature — "valid discharge if Client pays the Paying Agent" + "Service Provider remains contractually liable" + "5 business days' notice on revocation" — is reusable across both directions of payment flow without modification.

**Learning (IP direction in publisher-vs-developer contracts depends on who owns the underlying IP, not who's hiring whom):** In WMAY, AP is the publisher and Shosha is the developer/IP-owner per the Apr 25 2025 co-pub. Even when AP is providing porting *services* to Shosha (i.e. AP is the contractor), Work Product flows **to Shosha** (the IP owner), not to AP. Don't reflexively assign Work Product to the contracting party that's "in the AP family." The original 2026-04-14 draft had this wrong — it cited a "Dec 6 2024" co-pub (which doesn't exist; actual signed co-pub is Apr 25 2025) and treated AP as the underlying IP holder via "Developer Property" pass-through, which mis-identified Shosha (the actual Developer) as a third-party. Always verify the underlying-IP-ownership clause in the upstream contract before drafting IP assignment in any service subcontract.

**Learning (scope-toggle architecture: default scope + reduced-scope amendment path, not two parallel options in one contract):** When Robert says "porting scope with Switch = 6 months / without = 4 months," resist the urge to write two parallel scope options into the contract — that creates ambiguity at signing. Instead: lock the *default* scope (full = with Switch in this case) into the operative provisions (Term, Total Fee, Exhibit A), and add a single scope-reduction clause (here: Section 2.3) that permits mutual written amendment to drop a platform with pro-rata Term + Fee reduction. Then provide both milestone tables in Exhibit A — default and reduced — so the amendment doesn't require re-drafting the SOW, just an amendment letter pointing at the reduced-scope table. Keeps the signed contract unambiguous while preserving operational flexibility.

**Learning (when "follow K2C setup" is ambiguous, choose the deliverable-tracking shape and surface the alternative in NOTES):** Robert's instruction "milestone breakdown per platform, follow the same setup as for K2C project plan" has two readings: (a) the K2C-style %-payment-mapped milestone ladder (15/15/10/10/15/20/15) with milestone-gated payment, or (b) the K2C `k2c_milestones.csv` deliverable-tracking shape — chronological milestones with rich per-platform deliverables in each row, decoupled from monthly flat payment. WMAY porting is a flat €7K/mo retainer (not milestone-gated), so reading (b) fits — same shape Oskar's own K2C subcontract uses for its monthly-flat structure. Default to (b) and flag (a) as a NOTES item for Robert to confirm. Don't try to retrofit %-payment milestones onto a flat-monthly engagement.

## 2026-04-29 — Rename + reparent tickets when scope drifts from prefix

When a ticket's project/scope no longer matches its prefix (e.g. `czp-000-epic` titled "CZP Finances — Aurora Punks P&L" was doing AP-board P&L work, not CZP corporate ops), don't let it drift. Rename and reparent under the correct epic.

**Pattern:**
1. Spot the mismatch (Robert's framing question is often the trigger: "should this be sorted under X?")
2. Propose concrete restructure (new ticket ID, parent, what folds into what, what stays separate)
3. Confirm fold-vs-keep-child for related tickets and ask whether to trim repetitive activity log entries
4. Execute: write new ticket, rewrite old one as proper umbrella, update parent epic's child list, delete folded ticket
5. Update memory project_*.md files + MEMORY.md index pointers

**Activity log trimming:** keep substantive entries (creation, scope changes, blocker discoveries, data syncs) and drop repetitive "no work available" 4am sweep entries — they can stretch to 9+ consecutive duplicates and bury the real timeline. The DB ticket history isn't a forensic archive; it's working context.

**Routing fields:** if the old ticket had `routed_agent` / `routed_model` for daily sweeps, carry those onto the new ticket so the cron continues to run on the new home.

**Why:** Tickets are the durable unit of context for cross-session work. A misnamed/misfiled ticket means future sessions (and 4am sweep agents) ground in the wrong project. The admin_learnings, project memory, and RAG search all amplify the wrong scoping if the source ticket sits in the wrong place.

**How to apply:** Whenever Robert questions a ticket's parent/scope ("should this be under X?", "this isn't really Y"), treat it as a restructure signal, not just a label question. Draft the move concretely before executing.

## 2026-04-29 — Owner time allocation: 170h × 80/20 framework for multi-entity P&Ls

When an owner-director (Robert) has billable time spread across multiple entities (CZP project work + AP admin), the cleanest allocation is a fixed monthly capacity (170h/mo) split by percentage with explicit hourly rates per channel.

**Pattern (from K2C/AP, 2026-04-29):**
- Total monthly capacity: 170h
- 80% → CZP-side project (K2C): correlates to budgeted owner cost on the project P&L (~100K/mo flat sub-equivalent)
- 20% → AP admin: 34h × 1000 SEK/h = 34,000 SEK/mo on the AP P&L

**Why this is cleaner than ad-hoc lines:**
- Removes "BD push" / "extra hours" patches (each was a separate line with its own rationale; collapses into one rule)
- One percentage knob to dial up/down if scope shifts
- Hour rate per channel is explicit (AP admin at 1000 SEK/h is a different number from the implicit K2C rate ~735 SEK/h — they're different work types with different billing relationships)
- Pre-project months (e.g. Mar before K2C contract starts) stay at baseline (25h × 1000 = 25K) — the 80/20 only kicks in once the project is active

**How to apply:**
- Whenever an owner is the bottleneck on multiple billing entities, push for a `Xh × Y%` framework rather than itemized hour blocks
- State the framework in a CSV note row so the board sees the rule, not just the number
- For board-facing P&Ls, label the line `Robert AP admin (170h × 20% × 1000)` so the formula is visible — no hidden assumptions

**Why:** Boards and tax authorities both want to see the rule, not just the result. An itemized "25h base + 25h BD push" looks ad-hoc; a "170h × 20%" framework looks deliberate and survives audit/scrutiny.

## 2026-04-29 — File-ID confusion: csv snapshots vs live Sheet

When a project folder has both a static CSV upload and the corresponding live Google Sheet, the file IDs look similar and our docs/tickets can drift to the wrong one — the CSV is what the upload script produced first, but the live Sheet (different ID) is what the board actually edits and views.

**Pattern (apb-002, 2026-04-29):**
- Ticket referenced `1cvidQBkyrnJ43rCUnBq15wX4M60aCTIE` as the "target sheet" — that was a `text/csv` snapshot from Apr 1
- Live Google Sheet `1ml7BaJaVDTZwDp-CKFd6LPPaJ0HzaQsLQm96rt0-yiU` (mime `application/vnd.google-apps.spreadsheet`) was the actual board version
- For 5+ weeks the 4am sweeps reported "GDrive permissions blocker" because gsheets_read failed on the .csv ID (you can't gsheets_read a .csv, only a Sheet)
- Three stale .csv copies existed across multiple folders, including one inside the board-shared Shared Drive folder, creating real downstream confusion risk for board members

**How to apply:**
- When a ticket has a file ID, check the mime via `drive_search` or `drive get` before treating it as the live target
- For board sheets specifically: live Sheet mimeType must be `application/vnd.google-apps.spreadsheet`. If it's `text/csv`, you're looking at an export snapshot, not the source of truth
- After uploading via `gdrive-upload.js --convert`, capture both IDs in the ticket — the original `.csv` (if kept) AND the resulting Sheet — and explicitly mark which is the live one
- "GDrive permissions blocker" reports on a board file should always be sanity-checked: maybe the file exists but is the wrong type for the operation

**Replacing live Sheet contents in place:**
- Per-cell `gsheets_update_cell` is too chatty for full rewrites (~150+ calls)
- Best path: `assistant/gdrive-replace-sheet.js <local-csv> <fileId>` — Drive API multipart PATCH with `supportsAllDrives=true`, mime override to `application/vnd.google-apps.spreadsheet` so the CSV auto-converts to native Sheet format, file ID preserved (board members keep the same link, no re-share needed)
- Shared Drives require `supportsAllDrives=true` in every Drive API call — without it, file ops 404 even when the file exists and you have access

**Why:** Boards operate on link permanence. If a file ID changes, every email + Confluence page + Slack DM linking the old ID breaks. In-place replace via files.update keeps continuity.

## 2026-04-29 — Sheet formula recovery via batchUpdate (when Robert manually restores from Drive history)

When a Sheet's formulas are damaged (e.g. by CSV bulk-replace), the recovery flow is:

1. **Robert** restores from Drive UI Version History (only humans can pick a specific revision for native Sheets — Drive API doesn't expose revision content for read).
2. **CorpBot** reads with `valueRenderOption=FORMULA` to find what's still missing — Robert typically restores easy patterns (col N `=SUM(B<row>:M<row>)`) and leaves the cascade rows (subtotals, derived metrics, payout block) for the agent.
3. **CorpBot** sends ONE atomic `spreadsheets.values:batchUpdate` POST with `valueInputOption=USER_ENTERED` and all formula updates as separate `data[]` entries (one per range). This is much faster than per-cell `gsheets_update_cell` and atomic.
4. **Verify** by reading with `UNFORMATTED_VALUE` and comparing computed totals against expectations. Differences usually indicate intentional input edits Robert made during his restore (e.g. zeroing a contractor line, adding a new cost row) — those should propagate, not be reverted.
5. **Clean up stale leftover values** (e.g. col N total cells that got values from a flattened range earlier) with `values:clear` to avoid orphan numbers.

**Pattern for AP P&L formulas (reusable for board sheets):**
- Subtotal rows: monthly = `=B<input1>+B<input2>+...` per col, then col N `=SUM(B<row>:M<row>)`
- Cumulative rows (Accrued P&L): B = `=B<source>`, C onwards = `=<prevcol>37+<thiscol><source>`
- Payout chain: Available = `=MAX(0, Accrued - Buffer - Prior)`; Prior cumulative = `=<prevcol>50+<prevcol>52`; PAYOUT = `=Available`
- End-of-year totals: `=M<source>` for buffer-style, `=N<source>` for full-year totals

**Drive API curl call for batchUpdate:**
```
curl -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d @payload.json \
  "https://sheets.googleapis.com/v4/spreadsheets/<id>/values:batchUpdate"
```
Payload shape: `{"valueInputOption":"USER_ENTERED","data":[{"range":"<sheet>!<a1>","values":[[<formulas>]]}, ...]}`

**Why:** `gsheets_update_cell` is one cell per call — too chatty for ~100+ formula restorations. The batchUpdate endpoint takes the same inputs, runs atomically, and returns total cell counts for confirmation.

**Source:** Aurora Punks (apb-002 P&L formula recovery, 2026-04-29)
**Tags:** Sheets-API, batchUpdate, formulas, recovery, AP, P&L, Drive-revisions

## 2026-04-27 — Switching default signing tool: DocuSeal → Google Drive eSignature
**Category:** tooling / cost / workflow
**Source:** Eternal Minds AB (em-001), CorpBot infrastructure refresh

**Decision:** Native Google Drive eSignature is now CorpBot's default signing tool. DocuSeal is fallback only. Rationale: Drive eSignature is included in Workspace Business Standard and above (which the AP tenant has) at zero marginal cost, while DocuSeal API access requires a paid console tier Robert isn't carrying.

**Capability tradeoffs (verified 2026-04-27 web search + Workspace docs):**
- ✅ Native Drive eSignature signs Google Docs and PDFs in Drive, up to 10 signers, sequential or parallel, audit-trail PDF auto-generated, signer doesn't need a Google account
- ❌ **No public API for programmatic sending.** Drive eSignature is UI-only — the send step is always Robert (or another Workspace user) clicking Tools → eSignature in the doc. No webhook for completion either.
- ⚠️ Wizard UI is English even when the doc body is Swedish — fine for technical signers, may need a heads-up note for less tech-fluent counterparties.

**How to apply:** For any one-off legal signing (Bolagsverket filings, NDAs, MNDAs, samtycken, sub-contractor agreements), default to Drive eSignature. CorpBot prepares the doc end-to-end, posts a one-click "Tools → eSignature → add signers X/Y/Z → Send" instruction to the DB ticket activity log, and watches the Drive folder + Gmail for completion. Reserve DocuSeal for the case when (a) Robert activates a paid plan and (b) we have a high-volume templated flow that justifies webhook automation.

## 2026-04-27 — 3-layer template storage (skill / .md / Gdoc) — recommendation accepted
**Category:** storage / templates / knowledge-architecture
**Source:** Robert ask 2026-04-27 ("template could possibly be better used stored in the Wiki")

**Learning:** Legal templates live in three coordinated layers, each serving a distinct purpose:
- **Layer 1: Knowledge skill** (`skills/legal_template_<type>.md`) — clause patterns, when-to-use, jurisdiction notes. RAG-indexed, agent-readable. Survives template churn because it captures the *reasoning*.
- **Layer 2: Renderable source** (`assistant/legal_templates/<type>.md` — todo, not yet populated) — `{{PLACEHOLDER}}`-marked .md that md-to-docx renders. Source-of-truth for instantiation.
- **Layer 3: Browse-only Gdoc** (`G:\Shared drives\CZP\Projects_2\FTG_RM\`) — auto-rendered Gdoc copy, read-only, for human "what does this look like" browsing without rendering.

**Promotion rule:** A template moves from Layer 1+2 to all-three only after 3+ successful agreements with no clause changes between iterations. Stops the FTG_RM Gdoc folder from filling up with stale templates while patterns are still evolving in admin_learnings.

**Current registry state (2026-04-27):** Only the K2C-derived `Sub-Contractor Agreement_FTG_AuroraPunks` Gdoc lives in FTG_RM. Layer 1 + 2 backfill is a future task — extract from K2C contracts and admin_learnings. Other doc types (MNDA, samtycke, stämmoprotokoll per capsulum, konstituerande styrelseprotokoll, paying-agent agreement) have working examples in project folders but no formal template extraction yet.

**Why three layers, not one:** The wiki (skills) is right for **knowledge** — patterns, reasoning, why-this-clause. The .md is right for **renderable text** — agents instantiate via the doc-generation pipeline. The Gdoc is right for **human browsing** — Robert can peek at a stable template without invoking any agent. Conflating them (e.g. just keeping templates in Drive) loses the searchable knowledge and the auto-styling. Conflating into just skills loses the "click to see" affordance.

**How to apply:** When authoring a new legal template, start with Layer 1 (skill file capturing the patterns). When the template is concrete enough to instantiate, extract Layer 2 (.md). Only promote to Layer 3 (Gdoc in FTG_RM) once stable.

**Tags:** templates, storage, skills, knowledge-architecture, FTG_RM, layers

## 2026-04-27 — Patch Gdoc text via Docs API replaceAllText (no URL churn)
**Category:** tooling / gdrive
**Source:** Eternal Minds AB doc 07 address fix

**Learning:** When a reviewer leaves a Gdoc comment requesting a small text change (here: Robin's postaddress), the cleanest fix is `documents.batchUpdate` with a `replaceAllText` request — no need to delete + re-upload, which would change the Gdoc URL and break links the reviewer (and other shareholders) have already opened.

**Recipe (works with the existing GDrive OAuth token at `~/.claude/.gdrive-server-credentials.json`):**
1. Refresh the access token via `oauth2.googleapis.com/token` (use node since the VPS has no `jq` for bash one-liners)
2. POST to `https://docs.googleapis.com/v1/documents/<docId>:batchUpdate` with body:
   ```json
   { "requests": [ { "replaceAllText": { "containsText": { "text": "OLD STRING", "matchCase": true }, "replaceText": "NEW STRING" } } ] }
   ```
3. Response includes `replies[0].replaceAllText.occurrencesChanged` — verify it matches your expectation

The existing `drive` OAuth scope covers Docs API (parent scope), no separate `documents` scope needed.

**When to use vs delete-and-reupload:** Use `replaceAllText` for surgical text changes (typos, address updates, name corrections, version-string bumps). Delete-and-reupload is right when (a) styling needs to change too, or (b) the new content is so different that a regeneration is cleaner. Always preserve URL when there's an active comment thread or share.

**Caveat:** `matchCase: true` is the safer default for legal-doc edits — case-insensitive replace can fire on unintended occurrences. Always grep the source `.md` first to confirm the target string is unique.

**Tags:** gdrive, docs-api, replaceAllText, surgical-edit, oauth, no-jq

## 2026-04-27 — Direct .md-to-Gdoc upload skips canonical legal styling
**Category:** tooling / styling / pipeline
**Source:** Eternal Minds AB bolagsverket pack styling review

**Learning:** Uploading a `.md` file directly via `gdrive-upload.js --convert` produces a Gdoc with **Google's default styling** (Arial body, symmetric default margins, plain table borders) — NOT the canonical legal-doc styling baked into `md-to-docx.js` (EB Garamond body / Calibri bold headings / A4 with asymmetric 1440-left / 1080-right margins / bordered tables with #e8e8e8 header shading).

To get canonical styling, the path is **always** `md-to-docx.sh <src>.md /tmp/x.docx --title ...` → `gdrive-upload.js /tmp/x.docx <folder> --convert`. The intermediate `.docx` carries the styling through the docx→Gdoc conversion. Skipping the `md-to-docx` step (i.e. uploading the `.md` directly with `--convert`) loses all of it.

**Why this matters:** For internal Bolagsverket pack uploads (one-shot, getting PDF-exported anyway) the loss is cosmetic and tolerable. For external-counterparty contracts the styling delta is visible and signals "house standard" vs "ad-hoc" — important for negotiation drafts where presentation conveys care.

**How to apply:** CorpBot's [[contract_workflow]] DRAFT step always runs through `md-to-docx.sh` first. If a doc was uploaded as raw `.md` and the styling matters, regenerate via the pipeline (accepting URL churn). If the doc is already shared and signing is imminent, accept the cosmetic delta and apply canonical styling from the next pack onward — don't churn URLs for purely visual gain.

## 2026-04-27 — Direct .md upload also drops numbered-list semantics (silent demotion to bullets)
**Category:** tooling / pipeline / formatting
**Source:** Eternal Minds AB doc 01 + doc 05 patch round

**Learning:** Compounding with the styling-loss issue from earlier today, `gdrive-upload.js --convert` on a `.md` file **silently demotes numbered lists to plain bullets**. Source markdown using `1. 2. 3.` syntax renders in the Gdoc as a `[bullet]` list with no number glyphs. This is invisible in the markdown re-export (the export reconstructs `1. 2. 3.` from the bullet list because that's how MD lists work) — you only see it by inspecting the live Gdoc structure or fetching via Docs API and reading `paragraph.bullet.glyphFormat` (empty for plain bullets, `"%0."` for `NUMBERED_DECIMAL_NESTED`, etc.).

**Why it matters:** For legal docs, numbered points have **legal weight** that bulleted points don't. "Vi bekräftar att 1. ..., 2. ..., 3. ..." is a numbered enumeration of binding statements; the same text rendered as bullets reads as informal listing. Reviewers (Bibbi flagged this on the EM pack) notice instantly.

Both requests use the same `{startIndex, endIndex}` range. Bullet-format requests don't shift text indices, so they can be batched together with insertText requests in a single `documents.batchUpdate` (apply bullet ops first, then insertText ops in descending position order).

**Docs API preset cheatsheet for numbered lists:**
- `NUMBERED_DECIMAL_NESTED` — 1., 2., 3. (matches markdown `1.` syntax)
- `NUMBERED_DECIMAL_PARENS_NESTED` — 1), 2), 3)
- `NUMBERED_UPPERLETTER_NESTED` — A., B., C.
- `NUMBERED_UPPERROMAN_UPPERALPHA_DECIMAL` — I., A., 1. (legal-style nested)

**How to apply:** When a doc uploaded via raw `.md --convert` is reviewed and the lists "look wrong," check via Docs API for missing `glyphFormat` on bulleted paragraphs. Patch surgically as above. **Better long-term fix:** push CorpBot's [[contract_workflow]] DRAFT step to always go through `md-to-docx.sh` so numbered lists carry through correctly the first time.

3. Unplanned absence — Contractor risk, no sick-pay top-up, 1-business-day notification, counts toward threshold.
4. Threshold + pro-rata reduction — daily equivalent = 1/20 of monthly reference fee; or milestone slip.
5. Extended absence (10 consecutive business days) — special termination right bypassing the 30-day cure period. This is the clause that makes the whole section useful; without it you're stuck waiting out a 30-day cure when your lead has disappeared.
6. Hourly engagement carve-out — for capped-hourly deals (like Oskar porting), planned/threshold subsections don't apply; hour log self-regulates. Extended-absence still applies.

**Key drafting note:** Availability itself MUST NOT survive termination. Remove it from the survival list. Otherwise you've attached a vacation-policy to a terminated contract which is nonsense.

**Renumbering pattern when inserting a new top-level section mid-document:**
- Work descending (N → N+1 for N from max down to insertion point) to avoid collisions.
- Heading regex: `^## \d+ ` and `^### \d+\.\d+`.
- After renumbering, hand-fix the inserted section's heading (it'll have been double-bumped if the renumber script ran after the heading was temporarily written at its old position). My workflow:
  1. Edit: replace "## 5 OLD" with the whole new Section 5 block + "## 6 OLD" at end.
  2. Run descending renumber.
  3. Replace the accidentally-bumped "## 7 OLD" back to "## 6 OLD".
  Cleaner alternative: insert the new section AFTER the renumber pass.
- Cross-refs to hunt for: `Section N`, `this Section N`, `Section N.x`, `Subsection N.x`, and the survival list. Skip refs to sections that didn't move (in K2C case, Sections 1–4 were stable).
- Imi's preamble referenced "Section 12.6" for governing-law — caught because grep for `Section \d+\.\d+` flagged it. Moral: grep all cross-refs before declaring renumber done.

## 2026-04-14 — Swedish commercial office lease structure

**Learning (legal framework):** Swedish commercial leases (hyresavtal för lokal) are governed by **Jordabalken chapter 12** (Land Code 12 kap.). Key differences from residential leases:
- **No automatic tenant protection** — commercial tenants lack direct occupancy protection but may claim indirect protection after 9 consecutive months (can trigger compensation rights if lease terminates)
- **Notice periods differ by contract type:**
  - Indefinite term (tillsvidareavtal): landlord needs **9 months** notice, tenant needs **3 months** notice
  - Fixed term over 3 months: **3 months** notice unless otherwise agreed
  - Fixed term ends automatically unless renewed
- **Indexation restrictions:** Rent indexation (KPI-based) only permitted for fixed-term contracts **exceeding 3 years** (12 kap. 58 § Jordabalken)
- **Contractual freedom:** Parties have wide latitude to negotiate terms, unlike residential leases which are more regulated

**Learning (standard clauses):** Essential elements for a commercial office lease:
1. Parties (landlord/tenant with org numbers)
2. Premises description (address, area in sqm, floor, unit)
3. Purpose/permitted use (must be non-residential)
4. Rent amount + what's included (utilities, internet, cleaning, etc.)
5. Payment terms (monthly in advance is standard)
6. Contract period (fixed or indefinite) + start date
7. Termination/notice provisions
8. Deposit amount (typically 1 month's rent, held in separate account)
9. Maintenance responsibilities split (tenant: daily upkeep; landlord: structural/major systems)
10. Alterations clause (written approval required for changes)
11. Insurance requirements (tenant: property + liability)
12. Condition at move-in/move-out + inspection protocol
13. Assignment/subletting restrictions (landlord approval required)
14. Breach remedies (immediate termination for material breach)
15. Force majeure
16. Governing law (Swedish law, specific court jurisdiction)

**Learning (deposit mechanics):** Swedish commercial lease deposits should be:
- Held in **separate account** (not commingled with landlord's funds)
- Returned after lease ends IF: rent paid in full, premises returned in good condition (normal wear excepted), no damages
- Standard amount: **1 month's rent** (can be higher by negotiation)
- Alternative: bank guarantee (bankgaranti) instead of cash deposit

**Learning (resources - Swedish lease templates):** Reliable sources for commercial lease templates:
- **Företagarna** (Swedish Business Federation): Free templates for standard + short-term commercial leases (Word format)
- **Enkla Juridik**: Office room lease template + legal guidance
- **Wonder.legal**: Office lease template (Word + PDF) with English option
- **Itkett AB**: Legally reviewed templates for various commercial premises

**Learning (context discovery pattern):** When tasked with "set up rental agreement" with minimal context:
1. Check project P&L for office cost allocations (timing + amount clues)
2. Check company registered office address (could be the rental location)
3. Search Google Drive for existing agreements or correspondence
4. Check due dates vs current date (was this already set up informally?)
5. Before drafting, identify: landlord, premises details, rent, term, start date
6. Create comprehensive template with [BRACKETED] fields for missing data
7. Flag all required inputs with context clues rather than blocking immediately

**Why:** Rental agreements for office space are common across Aurora Punks, CZP, Runatyr. Standardizing the template structure and knowing Swedish legal requirements prevents drafting errors and ensures compliance with Jordabalken. For coworking spaces, the "landlord" may be the operator and terms may differ (shorter notice, all-inclusive pricing).

**How to apply:** When Robert asks to "set up hyreskontrakt" or similar, use this structure as the starting template. Always confirm premises type (traditional lease vs coworking) before assuming standard terms.
