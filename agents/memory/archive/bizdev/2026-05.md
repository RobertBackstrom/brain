### Peer / competitor audio shops — pick the service-side lead, not the tool-builder CEO

When a target studio is itself an audio service shop that ships its own middleware (e.g. Sonigon → Sonity), the "CEO" is usually the person who *built* the competing tool and is the worst possible contact — they have direct skin in their own product. The right contact is the **service-side lead** who picks middleware per-project on client work. Same person, two hats: as a contractor they're tool-agnostic; as a CEO of the tool side they're defensive.

For Sonigon (2026-05-06): Victor Engström is CEO + sole developer of Sonity. Wrong contact. Jacob Akpinar Olsson runs the audio service side — he's tool-agnostic on consulting projects, Martin at Elias already did a demo with him a year ago, and he's a *referral path* into the Swedish indie/Skövde audio scene if he respects Elias enough to recommend it on projects where Sonity isn't the right fit.

Don't try to "convert" the tool-builder. Treat shops like Sonigon as **referral network** entries (alongside Djuric, ranGa Illeperuma, Wahlund) — peer respect, no pitch on first touch, "want to see what's new since last demo?" framing. Move them to Audio Referral Network table in the tracker, not Wave 1. [Elias, 2026-05-06, pipeline strategy]

## GIN (Games Industry Network) Platform

GIN (`gamesindustry.network`) is Nordic Game's B2B matchmaking + networking platform (LinkedIn + matchmaking + Discord-like event/community servers). Learned setting up Erik Brattlöf's NGC meeting availability via Robert's GIN account (Elias, 2026-05-21):

- **No native Google Calendar sync.** Settings only covers email/subscription. Collision-avoidance with an external calendar = manual **"Blocked Slots"** on `/my-calendar` ("Block Timeslots" tab of the slot dialog): title + date + start + end.
- **API** (far more reliable than driving the FullCalendar UI — call via `fetch()` in the page context, auth rides on the session): `POST /gin-backend/public/api/block_meeting_slots` body `{title,start_date,end_date}` with datetimes `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` in **UTC**; `GET /meetingsFromTo?start=&end=`; `DELETE /delete_block_meeting_slot/{id}`. Headers: `X-XSRF-TOKEN` (decode the `XSRF-TOKEN` cookie), `X-Requested-With: XMLHttpRequest`, `X-Selected-Event: gin`, JSON content-type.
- **Overlap-check bug:** POST returns **406 "A meeting is already occurring..."** when a new block's START coincides with an existing block's END. END-touching an existing START is fine. To create a contiguous chain of blocks (e.g. a full day split into chunks), **create them in reverse-chronological order** — every touch becomes an allowed end-touch and all succeed. Forward order fails ~half of them with false 406s.
- **UTC date filtering:** blocks store in UTC; `meetingsFromTo?start=X` filters by UTC date, so a block starting 00:00 local (= 22:00 UTC the prior day) drops out of that week's query. Read with a wide date range, and trust the API JSON over the rendered FullCalendar grid for ground truth. The block form silently rejects a 24h span — keep chunks <=12h.
- **When Robert books GIN meetings "for" a client contact (e.g. Erik at Elias), the guiding calendar for GIN availability is that contact's calendar, not Robert's.** Robert's GIN account is a scheduling proxy. [Elias, 2026-05-21, tooling]
- **A GIN meeting-request invite that books a client contact's slot needs an explicit hat-disclaimer up front.** Robert's GIN profile foregrounds Aurora Punks; when he sends a meeting request to set up a slot with Erik Brattlöf (Elias), the recipient sees "Robert / Aurora Punks" and assumes an AP conversation. The invite must open by naming the Elias hat ("reaching out wearing my Elias Audio hat - I do biz-dev for them; you might know me from Aurora Punks but this isn't an AP conversation") so the recipient knows who they're meeting and why. Pair the one-clause capability mention (raytraced acoustics + GPU spatial audio, post-Metasound, for the Elias 4.7 line — Robert's pick 2026-05-22) with a consultative ask ("not a pitch - Erik wants to hear how your team approaches audio and where the friction is"), and add the Luma mixer as the secondary low-commitment CTA. 30-min slot length is accurate — that's Erik's GIN appointment-page slot size. [Elias GIN scheduling, 2026-05-22, message-drafting]

## Wiki Backfill Heuristics

- **Hybrid backfill (named-only) reveals which prospects deserve a wiki presence vs which are noise.** eli-025 (2026-05-18): Robert chose to backfill only rows with named contacts, skip TBD-only rows. This filter naturally separates real prospects (someone has a path in) from pipeline filler (a name on a portfolio list with no entry vector). For future backfill passes on any project: don't auto-create deal pages for every studio on a portfolio list — gate on "do we have a named contact OR a specific reason to make them findable in RAG". Empty stub pages add lint debt without compounding value. **Why:** wiki pages have maintenance cost (lint, broken-link risk, status drift); deal pages without an entry vector go stale immediately and pollute "stale > 30d" reports. **How to apply:** before creating a deal page during any backfill pass, check: (a) is there a named contact, OR (b) is there a specific public reason this deal could re-open (named acquisition, key hire, event accept, etc.). If neither, skip — log to project memory as "candidate not promoted to wiki" instead. [eli-025, 2026-05-18, wiki-shape]

- **Wave 2 "Draft Ready, pending send" deals live as `status: New` in the wiki, not `Contacted`.** Convention surfaced via eli-025 backfill of the 5 Polish DD targets (CDPR/Techland/Bloober/11 bit/PCF): drafts existed in eli-008, send-tickets eli-019–023 spawned, but nothing left Robert's outbox. eli-019's own activity log says "On send: Status: New → Contacted" — so the contract is: New until message physically goes out, Contacted after. The project pipeline page "Draft Ready (pending send)" section is a sub-bucket of New, not a separate status. **Why:** otherwise the dashboard inflates Contacted count with deals that haven't actually been contacted, and "Hot This Week" misrepresents what actually moved. **How to apply:** all draft-ready entries get `status: New` in frontmatter regardless of which section heading they live under on the pipeline page; only update to Contacted after Robert confirms send. [eli-025, 2026-05-18, wiki-shape]

- **Behold Summit + Behold Tier B = pre-pitch in-person beats cold DM.** Winterkeep, Oddiko, Playwood Project are all small Behold portfolio cos where genre fit for Elias is moderate (strategy / co-op management / diorama strategy = lower audio complexity than action/horror). For these, the right first touch is Behold Summit walk-up (2026-05-25 Malmö), not a cold DM. The warm-portfolio peer framing is the asset; burning it on a cold message before the in-person opportunity wastes it. Reserve cold-DM cadence for Tier A (Dead Astronauts, Sunscorched) where genre fit warrants the channel. **Why:** sub-10-person studios in non-audio-defining genres respond worse to cold middleware pitches than to peer-to-peer event introductions where the relationship can develop first. **How to apply:** when categorizing portfolio prospects, tag the next-touch channel explicitly (cold-DM / event-walk-up / warm-intro-only). Don't default to "draft a DM" for every named contact. [eli-025, 2026-05-18, channel-selection]

- **`named-contact-pending` tag is a first-class signal.** When a deal page is worth creating (real studio, real audio-context wedge) but the actual decision-maker contact isn't surfaced yet, tag the deal `named-contact-pending` in frontmatter rather than skipping the page or fabricating a contact. Examples from eli-025: Bloober (game director is public face but not audio lead), People Can Fly (Game On audio team contact not surfaced). [eli-025, 2026-05-18, wiki-shape]

- **Wikilink path bug surfaced: `wiki/deals/projects/<slug>.md` pages use `../../umbrella/...` which doesn't resolve from `wiki/deals/projects/` under strict-path semantics.** Found during eli-025 lint pass: project pipeline pages have ~20+ wikilinks of form `[[../../umbrella/elias_bizdev/...]]` which from `wiki/deals/projects/elias.md` resolves to `wiki/umbrella/...` — non-existent. Correct strict-path would be `../../../umbrella/...`. The wiki appears to be relying on Obsidian's name-only resolution (where prefix path is decorative and the link resolves by filename match). My new deal pages at `wiki/deals/deals/<slug>.md` use `../../../assistant/followups/...` which resolves correctly under both conventions, so I left the pre-existing project-page links alone (changing them is out-of-scope for backfill work and risks Obsidian users). **Why:** silent path bugs are a permanent compounding cost; either decide on convention and enforce. **How to apply:** propose to Robert (when surfacing): pick a convention (strict-path vs Obsidian-name) and lint-fix all pages to match; future page authors use the chosen convention. New deal/contact pages should use strict-path (3-up from `wiki/deals/deals/<slug>.md` → project root). [eli-025, 2026-05-18, wiki-tooling]

## 2026-05-22 — GIN blocks MUST be in the event scope (X-Selected-Event), not the global gin scope (Elias NGC)

**Updates the "GIN Platform" section above.** The 2026-05-21 GIN block recipe placed 11 "Erik unavailable" blocks with `X-Selected-Event: gin` — they appeared correctly on the user's Global calendar but **did NOT propagate to NGC matchmaking**, which uses the `nordicgame` event scope. NGC attendees could request Erik any time on any day, despite the visible blocks. Cosmetically correct, functionally invisible to the event.

**Why:** GIN scopes meetings and blocks per event via the `X-Selected-Event` header. The global `gin` scope is not a superset of event scopes — event-only entities ("Chief Rebel - Johan Fläckman" blocks, "https://elias.audio/" meeting) only return when the event scope is requested.

**How to apply:** For any event-scoped availability mirroring on GIN, use `X-Selected-Event: <event-slug>` (e.g. `nordicgame`) on the `POST /gin-backend/public/api/block_meeting_slots` call. Verify by polling the event-scoped `meetingsFromTo` after creation and confirming the new blocks are returned. Global-scope blocks are at best harmless decoration for event purposes. Same scope rule applies on reads. [Elias NGC, 2026-05-22, GIN Platform]

## Declining Event / Showcase Spots

Learned drafting DevGAMM Gdańsk 2026 decline emails for Tears of Adria + Knives & Gutters (2026-05-21):

- **Check whether the spot was already CONFIRMED before drafting the decline — the reply shape is completely different.** A spot never answered = clean decline ("thanks, won't make it"). A spot already confirmed = a graceful walk-back of your own prior commitment, opened with a light "sorry for the turn" line. K&G's acceptance email was never answered (clean decline); ToA's was confirmed by Robert on 2026-05-04 ("we'll be there in person", Valeria replied "see you there!"). Read the live Gmail thread, not just the acceptance email — the acceptance email alone wouldn't show ToA was already confirmed. [ToA + K&G / DevGAMM, 2026-05-21, message-drafting]
- **One event submission can hold multiple placements with different cost profiles — scope the decline to what actually carries the cost.** DevGAMM Gdańsk bundled an on-site Game Showcase booth (needs travel + ticket) AND a Baltic Showcase on Steam (a digital Steam-page feature, zero travel). "We're not attending" only kills the travel-dependent booth — the digital placement is pure upside and should be kept by default. Don't withdraw everything; confirm scope with Robert, decline the on-site part only, and say so explicitly in the email so the organizer doesn't drop the digital placement too. Separate threads = separate decline scope. [K&G / DevGAMM, 2026-05-21, event-logistics]
- **Decline emails for showcase spots should explicitly free the spot.** DevGAMM acceptance emails ask you to tell them right away if you can't come "so we can reassign your spot to someone from the waiting list." The decline should say plainly "please pass the booth to the waiting list, no need to hold it" — gives the organizer an immediate action and reads as considerate, not just a no. [DevGAMM, 2026-05-21, event-logistics]
- **Keep event-organizer declines warm and door-open.** Valeria Reznikova is Developer Relations Manager at DevGAMM (per her email signature), a warm recurring relationship across AP / Ark Island titles, and Robert is also a returning DevGAMM Awards judge. Decline emails to a relationship like this end with "hope to catch a future edition" — the no is about this June, not the relationship. [DevGAMM, 2026-05-21, voice]
- **One event = multiple separate acceptance emails, one per activity track. Sweep `from:<organizer-domain>` for ALL of them before declaring an event-wide decline done.** DevGAMM sends a distinct "[ACTION NEEDED] accepted to ..." email per activity (Game Showcase, Public Pitch, Baltic Showcase on Steam, Digital Vikings Awards, Game Roast) — each with its own P.S. saying "this message only concerns X, other activities are handled separately." Drafting declines off the two Game Showcase threads missed a K&G **Public Pitch** acceptance (a separate on-site activity that the not-attending decision also kills); it only surfaced during /close. The deal wiki page didn't mention Public Pitch either, so a RAG search alone would not have caught it. **How to apply:** when declining or confirming event participation, first run `from:<organizer-domain>` in Gmail and enumerate every acceptance/activity thread for the relevant titles, and cross-check against the DB tickets (kng-002 was literally titled "Public Pitch"). Don't trust the task prompt's framing or the deal wiki to be the complete set. [ToA + K&G / DevGAMM, 2026-05-21, process]
