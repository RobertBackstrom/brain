# Heist Royale 2: Design and Audience Due Diligence

Prepared for the Aurora Punks co-development pitch to Starbreeze (Tobias Remmers, Matt Dixon). Research date 2026-08-17. Sources: RankOne audience panel, SteamCharts, Krafton and Starbreeze earnings statements, PUBG Studios and Starbreeze developer interviews, community channels. Every number carries its source and a confidence level. Where we could not verify something, we say so.

Naming: "Heist Royale" (HR1/HR2) below refers to what shipped publicly as "New Mode: PAYDAY" in PUBG: Battlegrounds, internally Project Irons.

## 1. What this changes about our pitch

1. The evidence supports Tobias on more than it contradicts him. His avoid-list for stealth systems, the progression rework, and the restart and partial escape work are all backed by data or by clear precedent. We should say that first.
2. The one place the evidence pushes back is aggregate complexity. HR1's own success formula, stated by Tobias himself in the 80.lv developer interview, was arcade-like, digestible, minimum hurdles. HR2 stacks bigger heists, a stealth ingredient expansion, a progression rework, a new difficulty and a new escape system simultaneously. That is a scope and identity risk, not any single feature.
3. The reachable audience is the PUBG player, and that player is measurably neutral on stealth and strong on co-op PvE. The stealth expansion should be scoped and sequenced accordingly, not cut.
4. HR1 produced no measurable Steam user growth for PUBG. The business case for HR2 is engagement and monetization of existing PUBG players plus a franchise halo for PAYDAY, and scope should be priced against that, not against an acquisition story.
5. Our sharpest single ask: get HR1 mode-level telemetry from Krafton (adoption, stealth-vs-loud split, completion, repeat play, event pass attach) before locking HR2 scope. Nobody public has this data, Krafton does, and half the open bets in the plan resolve instantly with it.

## 2. Audience and overlap, with numbers and provenance

### PUBG: Battlegrounds, the host audience

1. Steam PC concurrent players, monthly averages 2026: March 327k, April 353k, May 322k, June 317k, July 327k. Source: SteamCharts, measured, high confidence. Steam only, excludes console and Korean PC (Kakao).
2. Krafton states peak concurrent users rose from roughly 800k to 1M after the April 2026 launch of its own co-op PvE mode (reported as XenoPoint or Genopoint, translation of the same mode varies by outlet), and that more than one third of battle royale players tried that mode. Source: Krafton via digitaltoday.co.kr, company-stated, medium confidence.
3. PUBG franchise revenue passed 1 trillion KRW per quarter in Q1 2026, but 702.7B of that is PUBG Mobile against 363.9B for PC. Source: Krafton Q1 2026 via thelec.net, company-stated, high confidence. PUBG Mobile is a separate title. The mode cannot reach the mobile audience at all.
4. RankOne panel: 3,109 measured PUBG: Battlegrounds profiles, stated reach 5.8M players. Behavioral fingerprint: tactical 1.57x over-index, massively multiplayer 1.78x, esports 1.9x, pre-release testing participation 2.2x, loot box receptivity 2.2x, Russia the top regional over-index at 1.59x. Source: RankOne, panel-measured counts with modeled over-index ratios, medium confidence on ratios, low confidence on any absolute share.
5. Neither RankOne nor public sources gave us age or gender splits we would stand behind. We could not verify demographics and are not inventing them.

### PAYDAY, the guest audience

1. PAYDAY 2 Steam concurrents, monthly averages 2026: March 21.3k, April 19.4k, May 27.4k, June 28.8k, July 30.0k. Source: SteamCharts, measured, high confidence.
2. PAYDAY 3 Steam concurrents, monthly averages 2026: March 467, April 410, May 691, June 671, July 914. All-time peak 69k in September 2023. Source: SteamCharts, measured, high confidence.
3. The load-bearing fact: the living PAYDAY community is PAYDAY 2's, at roughly 30 to 40 times PAYDAY 3's active scale. Any "PAYDAY authenticity" argument is really an argument about PAYDAY 2 players.
4. RankOne panel: PAYDAY 2 2,137 profiles (stated reach 2.33M), PAYDAY 3 only 382 profiles. 43.98% of the PAYDAY 3 panel owns PAYDAY 2. Source: RankOne, panel-measured, medium confidence, and low for PAYDAY 3 given the small n.

### Overlap, both directions

1. 20.4% of the PUBG panel also plays PAYDAY 2, at a 4.06x affinity over baseline. Only 2.25% plays PAYDAY 3, at 1.25x. Source: RankOne, described by the tool as measured co-play within its panel, medium confidence on the ratios, low on the absolute percentages (enthusiast panels overstate multi-game ownership).
2. 27.7% of the combined PAYDAY panel plays PUBG, at 4.05x affinity. Same source and caveat.
3. Reading: the affinity is real and strong in both directions, roughly 4x either way, and it runs almost entirely through PAYDAY 2, not PAYDAY 3. The four HR2 heists are adapted from PAYDAY 3, which the crossover-relevant PAYDAY audience largely has not played. That is not a problem (the heists are new content to everyone) but it removes "recognition" as a design argument.
4. No public source provides an independent overlap figure, so we cannot cross-check RankOne here. We flag that these numbers stand alone.

### Genre affinity inside the PUBG base

1. Co-op PvE: 40.1% of the PUBG panel shows affinity, over-index 1.63x. Source: RankOne, panel-measured, medium confidence.
2. Stealth: 12.1% engage with stealth themes, over-index 1.00x, exactly neutral. Solo-puzzle stealth under-indexes (Hitman at 0.93x). Stealth paired with tactical military framing does better (MGSV 1.54x). Source: RankOne, medium confidence.
3. Heist as an explicit keyword: 0.81% of the panel, 1.42x. SWAT-style tactical team play: 2.53x, one of the strongest signals in the whole fingerprint. Extraction shooter keyword: 0.34% but 1.82x. Source: RankOne, medium confidence.
4. High-affinity comparables among PUBG players: PAYDAY 2 (4.06x, 20.4% reach), Left 4 Dead 2 (2.46x, 23.0%), Hunt: Showdown (2.41x, 10.4%), Escape from Tarkov (2.09x), Deep Rock Galactic (1.95x). Source: RankOne, medium confidence.
5. Reading: the PUBG player's appetite is loud, cooperative, objective-based tactical play. Stealth is tolerated, not sought. This is the single most decision-relevant audience finding in this memo.
6. Krafton's own measured behavior points the same way: their co-op PvE roguelite (loud, arcade) got a third of the battle royale base to try it and lifted peak CCU by roughly 25%. Company-stated, medium confidence.

## 3. Reaching the PAYDAY community: frictions and what reduces them

1. Client and account. A PAYDAY player must install PUBG (roughly 40GB), sit inside a battle royale client, and find the mode in a BR-first lobby. PUBG is free to play on PC and console, so there is no purchase barrier, but there is a discovery and identity barrier. Reduction: PAYDAY-branded entry points, direct store-page and launch options during the event window, cross-promotion inside PAYDAY 2 as well as PAYDAY 3 (PAYDAY 2 is where the audience is).
2. Feel, 3C and TTK. The mode keeps PUBG gunplay and movement by design, and HR2 explicitly will not do a full gunplay overhaul. PUBG's hard recoil, weapon weight and optional third person feel foreign to a PAYDAY player. This friction is structural and will not be removed, only softened by the planned weapon feel and balance pass. We found no measured data on how PAYDAY players experienced HR1's gunplay, only the design fact.
3. Monetization. F2P client, 3.99 USD event pass for cosmetics in HR1, a panel that over-indexes 2.2x on loot box receptivity. Fine for PUBG players, mildly alien to a premium-game community. Low severity.
4. Platforms. PUBG covers PC, PS4/PS5 and Xbox; RankOne shows the PAYDAY 2 audience at 98% PC concentration, so platform coverage is not a real friction. PUBG Mobile players are unreachable, which caps the ceiling but is not a PAYDAY-side friction.
5. Time windows. HR1 ran about five weeks per platform. A PAYDAY player who heard about it late missed it. Krafton's CEO has publicly favored the model of running PvE modes for a period and re-releasing them upgraded, which is exactly what HR2 is. Reduction: announce the return cadence, so lapsed and late players know the content is not lost. Company-stated, medium confidence.
6. Goodwill. The PAYDAY community's dominant reaction to the crossover has been resentment about resources leaving PAYDAY 3 (see section 4). Marketing HR2 to that community as a love letter will underdeliver while PAYDAY 3 remains in its current state. The cosmetics import from PAYDAY 3, the masks and the music are the cheap and effective authenticity carriers; deep systems are not, because the systems are PUBG's either way.
7. Which HR2 features serve whom. Serving the PAYDAY player: loadout freedom and skill selection (the most PAYDAY-authentic feature in the plan), masks and cosmetics import, difficulty ladder, heist variety. Serving the PUBG player: PUBG gunplay retained, arcade session structure, restart and partial escape, new weapons, shield enemy. Pulling in opposite directions: stealth depth (PAYDAY 2 fans find ingredient-stealth shallow, PUBG players are neutral on stealth entirely) and the overall complexity level (PAYDAY veterans want depth, the PUBG walk-up player was the stated reason HR1 stripped it).

## 4. Heist Royale 1: sentiment and retention, with sources

### Measured signal

1. PUBG Steam concurrents did not rise during the mode's window: April 353k average before, 322k and 317k during May and June, 327k in July after it ended. The mode did not acquire users on Steam PC, and at minimum did not stem the seasonal dip. Source: SteamCharts, measured, high confidence. Console and Kakao numbers are not public.
2. Krafton nonetheless named "Payday" among the modes and collaborations that drove PC engagement and revenue in H1 2026. Note what is missing: for its own Genopoint mode, Krafton volunteered a CCU lift, a trial rate and players requesting permanent availability. For the PAYDAY mode it volunteered no equivalent stat. Absence of a boast in an earnings deck is weak evidence, but it points the same direction as the SteamCharts data. Source: Krafton H1 2026 via thelec.net and digitaltoday.co.kr, company-stated, medium confidence on the asymmetry reading.
3. PAYDAY 3 Steam concurrents rose from 410 average in April to 691 in May and 671 in June, roughly a 65% relative lift but only about 260 players in absolute terms. PAYDAY 2 rose from 19.4k to 27.4k and 28.8k over the same window, though a concurrent Steam sale ("PUBG x PAYDAY Celebration Sale") and PAYDAY 2 update activity confound that. The halo to the PAYDAY titles was real and small. Source: SteamCharts, measured, high confidence on the numbers, medium on attribution.
4. Starbreeze financials: the Krafton work was Starbreeze's single largest revenue source in Q2 2025 (SEK 19.6M of SEK 53.8M net sales), which was development revenue recognized before launch, not live performance. CEO commentary called the project "ahead of expectations". Roughly a quarter of Starbreeze's development staff was on it for a year. The first post-launch quarter (Q2 2026) report was not yet published at research time; when it lands it is the first hard read on live economics. Source: Starbreeze interim reports via gamedeveloper.com and pubglooker.com, company-stated, high confidence.

### Community signal, mostly anecdotal and labeled as such

1. PAYDAY community, pre-launch and launch: consistently negative, centered on resource diversion, not on the mode's design. A PAYDAY 3 Steam forum thread on the announcement ran overwhelmingly negative across 19 replies with zero positive responses, themes being "fix PAYDAY 3 first", mismanagement, and wrong-partner complaints. Source: steamcommunity.com PAYDAY 3 forums, anecdote, but unanimous in direction.
2. PAYDAY community YouTube: TheKknowley (largest PAYDAY commentary channel, 210k subscribers) titled his launch video "The Payday X PUBG Crossover is Absolutely Criminal" (42k views) after an announcement video called "Payday in PUBG is Tone Deaf Madness". Smaller channels: "The Payday Crossover No One Wanted" (MrBullseye), and "PUBG meets PAYDAY 3 for a cute but forgettable event" (fresh cut flower), whose description says the event left "PAYDAY fans incredibly unsatisfied" while granting it put the name back in front of a new audience. One reviewer summarized the full event as "a huge disappointment". Source: YouTube titles, descriptions and view counts collected directly, anecdote, medium confidence that it represents the PAYDAY-community mood, given consistency with the forum signal.
3. PUBG community, Steam forums: we pulled every PUBG Steam forum thread (app 578080) mentioning payday from the event window, roughly 10 threads with around 30 participants, a small sample and flagged as such. Direction among people who actually played: positive on the mode itself, negative on specific execution details. "Event is good, but there're too many team saboteurs that won't leave when all the objs are done and intentionally fail the mission" (June 6). One player in the audio thread said they were playing the mode exclusively over normal PUBG. The PAYDAY gun sounds were singled out as better than PUBG's own. Complaints, in frequency order: the music was too loud with no separate mute (2 threads, 13 replies on one), team saboteurs exploiting the all-four-must-escape rule, the stealth completion bonus being too small, a final-objective bug (Russian-language thread), and cynicism about the 100k USD streamer challenge (Russian-language thread). Nobody in the sample called it a skin-deep event. Source: steamcommunity.com PUBG forums, collected directly, anecdote, small n.
4. A funnel check in the same forum: a thread asking "Will any of you buy PAYDAY 3 because of the event?" drew six repliers, effectively all no, including one who had not known PAYDAY 3 existed and one PAYDAY 2 player who declined "based on this mode's quality". Anecdote, n=6, but it matches the measured ~260 CCU halo in the section above.
5. Creator attention as a proxy for community engagement: the official mode trailer sits at 1.3M views, but PUBG-creator coverage of the mode was thin (largest found: 5.2K and 4.9K views), while the largest PAYDAY-side commentary video has 42K views. The crossover generated more creator conversation inside the PAYDAY community, mostly negative, than inside the much larger PUBG community. Source: YouTube view counts collected directly, measured but a proxy, medium confidence.
6. Stealth versus loud: no public data exists on what share of HR1 sessions were played stealthy versus loud, and mode telemetry remains a Krafton-only asset. What we did find: first-hand evidence that a stealth-playing minority existed and that it felt under-rewarded. A Steam forum player who completed heists in stealth called the mechanics "poorly copied" and the 10% completion bonus "terrible even on hard mode" (May 24, zero replies). A challenge-runner (Glitchilix) uploaded Hard Solo Stealth 100% completions of Diamond District and Nightclub, proving full-stealth was completable and pursued as a challenge, at 163 to 378 views each, an audience of almost nobody. The 80.lv developer interview establishes full-stealth completion was added during development at significant AI and scripting cost ("building two games at once"). Source: 80.lv (high confidence on design facts), Steam forums and YouTube (anecdote, tiny n) for usage.
7. Retention: we could not find mode-level retention data anywhere public. The honest statement is that nobody outside Krafton knows whether HR1 retained players across its five-week window.
8. Console versus PC sentiment: not sampled. Console players do not post on Steam forums, and the console-focused subreddits sat behind the same Reddit block described in section 8. We have no read on console sentiment and will not guess at one.

## 5. Tobias's bets, one by one

1. Bigger and more complex heists (Turbid Station, 99 Boxes or Touch the Sky, Syntax Error, Under the Surphaze). Verdict: contradicted on complexity, supported on new content. The evidence for HR1's positive reception, such as it is, attaches to exactly the qualities Tobias himself named in the 80.lv interview: arcade-like, digestible whether you play once or twenty times, minimum hurdles. Krafton's strongest measured PvE result (a third of the BR base trying their arcade PvE mode) is a breadth result, not a depth result. HR1 deliberately simplified every heist it adapted, and the HR2 feature list itself hedges by proposing to remove high-complexity side objectives. More heists: clearly right, content volume is the engine of a re-release. Bigger and more complex as a goal in itself: the evidence leans against, and the plan's own hedges suggest Tobias half-knows it.
2. Stealth expansion via ingredients (lockpick and safecracker minigames, WIFI hacking, QR codes, motion and floor sensors, drones, lasers) while avoiding PAYDAY systems (security rooms, lures, detection bar, guard search states, pagers, special-order animations). Verdict: the avoid-list is supported, the volume of ingredients is not. The avoid-list is the best-evidenced call in the whole plan: RankOne shows the reachable audience is exactly neutral on stealth (1.00x, with solo-stealth under-indexing), so heavyweight simulation stealth would serve players who are not in the building, and HR1's suspicion-counter stealth already cost "two games at once" to build. But the same neutrality argues against shipping seven or eight new stealth ingredient types at once: each needs teaching (HR1's tutorial was images in a menu), each fragments QA across the stealth and loud paths of eight stages, and the players most likely to appreciate the depth, PAYDAY 2 veterans, are the group publicly calling ingredient-stealth shallow. There is a coherent middle: three or four ingredients, introduced per-heist, gated on HR1 stealth-usage telemetry. Evidence for stealth as a retention driver in this mode: silent, nobody has the data except Krafton. One refinement from the PUBG-side sample (section 4, anecdote, tiny n): the only first-hand stealth feedback we found attacks the reward, not the mechanics count. A player who completed heists in stealth called the 10% completion bonus "terrible even on hard mode". If that generalizes, HR1's stealth deficiency was that playing well felt unpaid, and the cheapest stealth investment in HR2 is reward tuning through the new currency economy, before any new sensor type is built.
3. Progression rework (EXP to currency, free skill and weapon selection, loadout customization, replacing five fixed classes). Verdict: supported. The RankOne PUBG fingerprint over-indexes on competitive structure, testing participation and progression-adjacent systems, HR2 targets returning players rather than one-time tourists, and loadout freedom is the most authentically PAYDAY feature on the list, serving both audiences at once. One caveat with evidence behind it: the five classes were HR1's onboarding device (Remmers, 80.lv: avoid "overloading the player with choice"), so removing them without shipping default loadout presets re-creates the choice-overload problem for the walk-up player.
4. One additional difficulty level. Verdict: no direct evidence, low risk. PAYDAY culture runs on difficulty ladders and the PUBG fingerprint skews competitive, so it is plausible, cheap and unlikely to hurt. We found no player data asking for it.
5. Partial escape and restart system. Verdict: supported, now with direct player evidence. HR1 ending every heist by dumping players to the main menu with no restart and requiring all four to escape is plain session friction, and the PUBG Steam forum sample contains a first-hand complaint that maps exactly onto this feature: team saboteurs who "won't leave when all the objs are done and intentionally fail the mission", exploiting the all-four-must-escape rule to hold the other three hostage (June 6 thread, anecdote, confirmed by one replier). Partial escape removes that griefing lever entirely. This is the one HR2 feature where we found a player asking for the fix before the feature was announced. Tobias's note that Krafton guidance is needed remains right, since partial escape touches PUBG's spectate, leave and reward flows.
6. Five new weapons (rotary gun, grenade launcher, akimbo, Thanatos .50, flamethrower). Verdict: no evidence, low risk, with one observation. These are loud-play power fantasy items, which matches where the audience data says the appetite is. They also further tilt the mode toward loud play at the same time the stealth budget expands, which sharpens the question in item 2.
7. Shield AI enemy. Verdict: no evidence, low risk, standard escalation content.
8. 3C and movement modifications to the PUBG engine. Verdict: his risk flag is supported and we would go further, see pitfall 1 below.
9. UI/UX overhaul, tutorials, cosmetics import. Verdict: supported. Cosmetics and masks imported from PAYDAY 3 are the cheapest authenticity carrier for the guest audience, and tutorial investment is a precondition for every other bet on this list.

## 6. Pitfalls, most severe first

1. Engine and 3C dependency on Krafton. The one item that can slip the whole schedule, because it runs through another company's engine team and codebase. HR1 already had to rebuild AI systems from scratch inside the PUBG engine rather than modify existing ones (80.lv, high confidence). Tobias flags the risk himself; the pitch-relevant addition is containment: define the fallback mode ("HR2 ships on HR1 movement if the mods miss Alpha"), get the Krafton engineering commitment in writing at milestone level, and keep every other feature decoupled from the 3C work so it cannot hold the release hostage.
2. Aggregate scope against a five-week live window and a re-release business model. Individually defensible features (four bigger heists, four upgraded heists, stealth ingredients across all eight, progression economy, new difficulty, partial escape, UI overhaul, five weapons, new enemy) sum to a near-sequel built for a mode with no publicly demonstrated acquisition power and an unknown retention profile. The staffing plan peaks at 30 to 40 people over roughly a year. If HR1's telemetry does not show strong repeat play, HR2's scope is priced for evidence that does not exist yet. Severity high because it is the compounding of every other risk.
3. Building depth for an audience that is measurably neutral on it. RankOne: stealth affinity 1.00x, co-op PvE 1.63x, SWAT 2.53x (medium confidence). The stealth ingredient expansion plus bigger heists is the part of the plan most exposed to this, and the PAYDAY 2 veterans who do want depth are small in reachable numbers (20% of the PUBG panel, but that is panel-inflated) and publicly sour on the crossover. The mitigation is sequencing and telemetry gating, not cutting stealth.
4. Onboarding debt. HR1 taught its mechanics through menu images, and HR2 adds minigames, sensors, drones, an economy, loadouts and a new difficulty on top. The plan does include tutorial updates, but as a line item, not as a pillar. For a walk-up F2P audience the tutorial is load-bearing for every other investment; if players do not understand the stealth ingredients they will play loud and the stealth budget becomes invisible. (Inference from design facts; direct HR1 usage data unavailable.)
5. Progression economy risk. Converting EXP to currency with free selection creates a real economy needing sinks, pricing and exploit review inside a five-week window, and it deletes HR1's onboarding device (fixed classes). Solvable with default loadouts and conservative pricing, but it is systems work masquerading as a feature toggle.
6. Misreading the halo as the goal. The measured HR1 halo moved PAYDAY 3 by about 260 average concurrents. If part of HR2's internal justification is reviving PAYDAY 3, the measured ceiling on that effect is now known and it is low. The defensible framing is Starbreeze revenue plus franchise visibility plus PUBG engagement, in that order.
7. Partial escape edge cases. Reward splits, leaver behavior, spectate flow and griefing (three players extract, one hostage-holds the timer). Needs Krafton ruling early, exactly as Tobias's note says. Low severity if addressed early, expensive if discovered in Beta.

## 7. What we would say to Tobias

1. Your avoid-list on stealth is right and we can show why with data: the reachable audience inside PUBG is exactly neutral on stealth and strong on loud co-op, so pagers and detection bars would have been budget spent on players who are not there. We would go one step further in the same direction: treat the ingredient list as a menu, not a manifest, ship three or four ingredients across the new heists first, and let usage data pull the rest in.
2. The progression rework is the right call and the most PAYDAY-authentic thing in the plan. The one thing we would preserve from HR1 is its onboarding function: keep default loadout presets so the walk-up player still has a one-click start, and let the currency economy be conservative at launch since a five-week window gives no room to patch an exploited economy.
3. On the four new heists, our question is not which, it is how much of each. HR1 worked, by your own description, because it was digestible. Turbid Station and Under the Surphaze are the two most systems-heavy heists in PAYDAY 3, and the feature list already hedges toward removing complex side objectives. We would define a complexity budget per heist up front, against HR1's stages as the baseline, rather than trimming at Beta when the cuts are expensive.
4. Before we lock any of this, one request to Krafton is worth more than everything in this memo: HR1's mode telemetry. Adoption share of the PC base, stealth-versus-loud split, completion and retry rates, week-over-week fall-off, event pass attach. We have been open about what public data cannot show, and that dataset resolves the stealth bet, the difficulty bet and the scope level in one meeting.
5. On 3C and movement, we agree with your risk flag and would formalize it: a fallback definition where HR2 ships on HR1 movement, decoupling of all other features from the engine work, and a milestone-level commitment from the Krafton side. That is the one dependency neither of our companies controls.
6. One framing note for the business case: on Steam PC, HR1 did not grow PUBG's user numbers, and the PAYDAY 3 halo was real but small. That is not a criticism, it is what limited-time modes do. It just means HR2's case rests on engaging and monetizing the existing PUBG base, and the scope earns its budget through repeat play, which is also the strongest argument for your progression rework and for the re-release cadence Krafton's CEO has already endorsed publicly.

## 8. Open questions and where the evidence is thin

1. HR1 mode-level telemetry (adoption, retention, stealth usage, pass attach) exists only inside Krafton. Everything in section 5 marked silent resolves there. Highest-value ask.
2. PUBG-side player sentiment is now partially sampled but still the thinnest area. What we collected: the full set of PUBG Steam forum threads on the mode (about 10 threads, about 30 participants) and creator-side view counts. What stays closed: Reddit returned HTTP 403 at the network level for every route we tried, including old.reddit.com, the JSON endpoints, console subreddits, and a full browser session with a realistic user agent and viewport; the block is against the server's datacenter IP, not against a tool. YouTube comment sections similarly sat behind a sign-in bot check for both browser automation and yt-dlp, and escalated to a full captcha wall during collection. Closing the remainder needs a residential connection: a manual Reddit and YouTube-comments pass from Robert's own browser (r/PUBATTLEGROUNDS and r/PUBGConsole, search "payday", May to July 2026) would take under an hour and is the only way to sample console sentiment, which we have zero read on.
3. Starbreeze's Q2 2026 interim report, the first covering post-launch economics, was not yet published at research time. Worth checking before the pitch meeting, since it may contain the first public live-performance statement.
4. RankOne caveats: panel n is strong for PUBG (3,109) and PAYDAY 2 (2,137) but weak for PAYDAY 3 (382) and PUBG Mobile (183). Absolute percentages are panel shares, not population shares, and likely overstate multi-game overlap; the over-index and affinity ratios are the robust part. RankOne's comparables answer on crossover-mode benchmarks blurred measured and modeled claims (including an unverifiable "Fortnite FERCI" metric) and we have excluded it from this memo's conclusions.
5. Krafton's own PvE mode is reported as both "XenoPoint" and "Genopoint" across outlets translating the same Korean sources. We treat them as one mode; worth confirming with Starbreeze.
6. We found no public statement on whether HR2 will be limited-time or persistent, only the Krafton CEO's general preference for seasonal re-release of PvE modes. The monetization and revenue split between Starbreeze and Krafton for HR2 is unknown to us.
7. Console-side HR1 data (population, sentiment, CCU) is entirely non-public. All retention reasoning above is Steam PC.
