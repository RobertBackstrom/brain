When a project has small scope + limited time + proven tech (e.g. K2C: 8 months, team of 6, on top of a shipped engine and base game), default the team to **WYSIWYG work — on-screen / in-engine implementation** rather than estimate-driven or paper-design work. Avoid the estimate/paper-design rabbit hole.

**Why:** Robert flagged this on K2C 2026-05-05 standup follow-up. Small-scope/limited-time/proven-tech projects don't earn back the cost of detailed estimates and exhaustive paper specs — the time spent on them is time NOT spent making the thing visible and playable. Paper design is also less useful when the underlying tech is proven (the team already knows it works), and detailed estimates are highly tweakable so the precision is illusory. Slight-vagueness on promises to the publisher is preferred over over-committing on paper.

**How to apply:**
- Default planning shape for any K2C-like project (small scope + limited time + proven tech): in-engine prototypes and visible artifacts first, paper design and estimates only when explicitly load-bearing.
- When a designer/artist is choosing between "estimate asset times" and "make the thing in engine", prefer the second. Explicitly say so in standups and tickets if the team is drifting toward the first.
- When sprint-shape decisions surface (per pm_learnings.md "Present shape options explicitly"), if the project meets the small/limited/proven criteria, the WYSIWYG shape is the default — no need to surface paper-design as a serious option.
- Doesn't apply to large/long projects where contracted milestones depend on detailed estimates, OR to projects on unproven tech where paper design is risk reduction.
- Source project: K2C.
