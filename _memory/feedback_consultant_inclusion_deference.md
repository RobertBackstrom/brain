Robert is an external contractor / consultant to most of his clients (BADASS, K2C, Elias, etc.). He does not presume inclusion in their internal meetings — even meetings about topics he's heavily involved in upstream.

**Rule:** When a client mentions an upcoming meeting in passing, the default reply is *"is that something you wanted me in on?"* — NOT *"what time works?"*. The latter presumes inclusion; the former defers to the client to decide.

**Why:** Robert is external. The right framing is "I'm available if you want me there" — not "where do I show up." Even if he's clearly been instrumental in the work being discussed (e.g., he built the model being walked through), the client owns who's in their meeting. Confirmed on 2026-05-11 in the BADASS merged-file delivery mail edit: Robert replaced *"what time works for the Peter + Ben walkthrough?"* with *"You mentioned meetings Peter and Ben, is that something you wanted me in on?"*.

**How to apply:**
- When drafting a reply to a client mail that mentions a future meeting, default phrasing: *"is that something you wanted me in on?"* or *"happy to join if useful"* or *"want me on that?"*
- Never assume the named contact wants the consultant present — even if the meeting topic is the consultant's deliverable.
- Once invited, then ask for time / logistics. Two-step deference: confirm inclusion first, then schedule.

**Exception:** If the meeting was Robert's own initiative (he proposed it, the client agreed), then asking "what time works" is fine — that's scheduling, not presumption.
