Long mails need a TL;DR section at the top — covering: (1) what was delivered, (2) 3-5 bullets of what changed/key points, (3) what needs their decision, (4) what's coming separately.

**Why:** Robert's correspondents (CEOs, clients, partners) skim before reading deep. Without a TL;DR they may miss the action items or the headline ask. He flagged this on 2026-05-11 reviewing the BADASS merged-file delivery mail (8+ sections, dense detail).

**How to apply:** For any drafted mail with more than ~5 paragraphs, more than 3 distinct topics, or where action items / decisions are buried below context, add a TL;DR block at the top. Format: short header (e.g., "TL;DR" or "Headline"), 4-6 single-line bullets ending with one explicit "needs your decision" line if applicable. The rest of the mail then expands each bullet. Don't duplicate content — TL;DR is index, not summary.

**TL;DR-specific rules (UNIVERSAL):**
- **Strip all internal jargon.** TL;DR audience may include people downstream of the named recipient (forwards to investors, board, partners). Even on technical mails between specialists — say "the more complex setup with a support team" instead of "the v3 modular spec". Confirmed universal rule (Robert 2026-05-11).
- **Don't bury action items in TL;DR.** TL;DR bullets are action / decision / headline — not open questions or status detail. If a bullet starts to expand into multiple sub-points, push it to the body.
- **Front-load the deliverable link BEFORE TL;DR**, not after. The link to the file/doc should be the first clickable element in the mail. TL;DR is the second thing the reader sees.

Source project: BADASS (2026-05-11).
