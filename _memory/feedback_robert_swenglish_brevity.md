How Robert tightens a Swenglish WhatsApp/DM outreach draft. Learned 2026-06-29 from his edits to the Magnus Lysell (LUG) Curveball pre-warm - keep this pattern for short personal messages.

**The edits, draft -> sent:**
- Opener: "Tjena Magnus! Hoppas allt rullar på er sida sedan sist." -> **"Tja! Hoppas allt rullar på bra!"** - drop the name, drop "sedan sist / på er sida". Short and warm.
- Don't over-name people: "ihop med Joel och gänget på The Gang" -> **"ihop med The Gang"**. Name the company, not every person.
- Make the ask direct and name the counterparty company: "er roll skulle vara funding + marketing" -> **"vi skulle behöva LUGs hjälp med fundingen + marketing"**.
- Close question short, company-named: "Är det här något ni skulle vilja titta närmare på?" -> **"Är det här något för LUG?"**
- Cut sign-off filler: drop "om du nappar" and "Hör av dig!". End on the offer: **"Har en kort pitch jag kan skicka över."**

**The pattern in one line:** shorter and less hedged on *structure*, but MORE tentative in *stance*. No name-stacking in the opener, don't list people, make asks short and direct, name the partner company, cut sign-offs. Applies to WhatsApp/DM/Slack AND email.

**Email example (Curveball -> The Gang thread, 2026-06-29, my draft -> his sent):**
- Opener "Tjena gänget!" -> **"Tjena!"** (drop the collective address).
- First person + tentative stance: my "Vi har gått igenom Curveball ordentligt nu" -> his **"Jag har funderat kring Curveball nu"**; my definitive "hur vi på AP ser att vi får ut det" -> his hedged **"hur man skulle kunna angripa det hela"**. He writes as "jag" thinking out loud, not "vi" declaring.
- Lead with the ONE core idea, drop the feature list: he cut "byggd mot den stora Blade Ball-publiken. AP kör co-dev och lite publishing" and kept just **"det centrala är få till en betald Early Access på PC under 10 dollar med P2P"**.
- Cut the sell: my "Vi tror LUG passar bäst, vi har jobbat ihop med dem förut och de kan både pengarna och marknaden" -> his **"Vi tror LUG skulle kunna passa perfa"** (hedged, no justification stack).
- Add a low-ego flexibility offer: **"men den kan vi ändra på om det känns off"** - invites input, signals he is not attached to it. A Robert signature move.
- Short close: my "Vad säger ni om att involvera LUG? Ville höra er input innan vi går vidare." -> **"Vad tror ni?"**
- He CUT the excitement flourish he had asked me to add ("Vi är väldigt taggade..."). Lesson: a draft may carry genuine enthusiasm when he asks for it, but his own instinct on send is matter-of-fact - do not lean on emotional flourishes.
- Casual spelling stays even in a business mail: "perfa" (perfekt), "jämntemot".

**Email example 2 (Curveball -> Joel Edström, 2026-08-04, operational ask; my draft -> his sent).** Two edits, both instructive:

- **He hedged a fact I had verified from the code.** My "Kort svar på din fråga: nej, det går inte att spela nu utan att AWS dras igång igen" -> his **"Antar att inte det går att spela nu utan att AWS dras igång igen"**. I had confirmed it from the project (GameLift plugins, the backend URL, LootLocker calls in the logs), and he still softened it to an assumption. Read: he does not assert a technical claim to a client as certain unless he has seen it run himself. Give him the evidence and the confident version in chat; write the client-facing line hedged unless he says otherwise.
- **He deleted the entire "and here is why that is good for you" paragraph.** I had written a paragraph tying the answer to the ask ("Det är precis det P2P löser... så blast.tv är snarare ett argument för att dra igång"). He cut all of it and went straight from the fact to the request. This is "cut the sell" at paragraph scale, not just phrase scale: in an operational mail to a warm contact, state the fact, make the ask, stop. The reasoning is for the conversation, not the mail.
- Kept verbatim: both numbered sub-asks, the credentials heads-up, and the closing Steam app-id question. Concrete asks and factual notes survive his edits intact. It is framing and persuasion that gets cut.

**Email example 3 (Wavedash -> Kyler Blue, 2026-08-05, asking a partner for code; my draft -> his sent).**
Same cutting pattern, plus one move I had not seen before:

- **He removed the contractual lever.** My "Our agreement says your team commits back to the original
  location of code once porting is done, but our repo has had no pushes since January 7" became just
  **"Our repo has had no pushes since January 7, so I assume it lives somewhere on your side."** The
  clause was accurate and it was his strongest argument, and he still cut it. Read: with a partner he
  is on good terms with, he asks on the facts and keeps the contract in his back pocket.
- **He cut the whole "to be straight about why" paragraph**, so the mail does not disclose that we are
  looking at other browser platforms at all. (Third time in one session he deleted my
  reason-you-should-care paragraph. Stop writing them.)
- **New: he does not only subtract, he trades.** He *added* "Also would be great to have a chat about
  any promotion we could assist you with for the game." An offer of help, unprompted, replacing the
  explanation he removed. The mail ends up shorter **and** warmer, not just shorter.

**How to apply:** draft outward operational mail as fact -> ask -> stop. Leave out the bridging paragraph that explains why the recipient should care, unless Robert asks for a persuasive register; where the draft wants one, consider offering the recipient something instead. Hedge technical assertions in client-facing copy even when the underlying finding is solid, and keep contractual leverage out of a friendly partner ask unless it is needed.
