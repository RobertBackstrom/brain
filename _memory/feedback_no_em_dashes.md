Em-dashes (—) and en-dashes (–) are the single most reliable signal of LLM authorship to a human reader. Robert flagged "AI sloppiness (emdashes etc)" after rewriting a Teams post I drafted, manually replacing every em-dash with a regular hyphen-space-hyphen ( - ).

**Why:** This is now well-known in Robert's circles as the AI tell. A single em-dash undermines the warmth/personal voice he's cultivated and signals "this was AI-drafted" even when the rest of the writing is on point. Same applies to en-dashes (commonly used in date ranges like "12–13 June" — should be "12-13 June" or "12 to 13 June").

**How to apply:** Across ALL drafted text — emails, Teams/Slack messages, GDocs, MD files, tickets, comments, even internal-only memos. Use a regular hyphen with spaces ( - ) for parenthetical breaks, mid-sentence pauses, or appositives. Never em-dash (—). Never en-dash (–). For ranges, use plain hyphen ("12-13 June") or a word ("12 to 13 June"). Same for any document the user might paste/forward verbatim. This is one of those "if in doubt, default to hyphen" rules — the rare case where being slightly less typographically correct is the right call.
