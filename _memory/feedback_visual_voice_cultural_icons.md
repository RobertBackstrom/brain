For visual-first presentations (Pecha Kucha, Pixar pitch, demo-day, conference talk), default the visual stack to: (1) real photo/screenshot/artifact if it exists in Robert's life, (2) iconic film/TV/game still everyone in the room recognizes, (3) published concept art from a known title. **AI-generated original imagery is for atmospheric/transitional slides only — never for "who am I" or "what we are" character beats.**

**Why:** Behold Summit 2026-05-25 Pecha Kucha rebuild. We delivered a 20-slide deck + 13 fal.ai Flux.dev images (Blanche/Dalí gothic aesthetic). Robert called the AI images "too generic" and used zero of them in the final deck. His final deck used: Drakar och Demoner childhood RPG covers (slide 1 + 20 bookend), real hardcore-scene photos (slide 2), Karate Kid + a photo of his actual Unity workstation (slide 3), Tom Hanks + Boris Johnson for "likeable + loose cannon" (slide 4), the real GamesBeat 2021 AP press article (slide 5), actual John Blanche art (slide 6), 40K Adeptus Mechanicus + LOTR + Pulp Fiction stills throughout, real 2022 church-demolition photo for the pivot (slide 10), and a Frodo→Isildur two-slide callback joke (16+19). Pecha Kucha's 20-second clock rewards INSTANT recognition; a Tyrion Lannister still communicates "wise advisor with hard-won lessons" in <1 second. An AI-generated gothic figure requires the speaker to build the metaphor in real-time, which burns precious seconds.

**How to apply:** When briefing visuals for any time-constrained presentation:

1. **First pass — inventory real artifacts.** Photos from Robert's life, screenshots of real press, real product UI, real industry artifacts. Search his Gmail/Drive/photo archive before generating anything. The 2021 GamesBeat article slide was sitting in his email the whole time.

2. **Second pass — cast cultural icons.** For each "who I am / what we are / what we want" beat, ask: which fictional character or cultural moment IS this? Tom Hanks = likeable. Boris Johnson = unfocused loose cannon. Mr. Wolf = fixer. Frodo at Mount Doom = "kill your darling." Tyrion = wise advisor. Pop culture deep cuts also prove insider/audience-shared taste, which earns trust faster than original art.

3. **Third pass — AI ONLY for atmospheric transitions.** Concept-art-style slides between character beats (the "exploring a brave new world" / "scaffolds upon scaffolds" type) where no specific cultural reference fits. Even here, *published* concept art (e.g. The Last of Us, Diablo, Interstellar stills) beats AI when available.

4. **Copyright posture for closed-room talks.** Pop-culture stills + game concept art are fine for closed-door audiences (peer events, internal pitches). Don't generate AI art "to be safe" — the recognition penalty is worse than the negligible IP risk.

5. **When AI art is the right call:** Generic mood/texture plates, brand-art briefs where you need original mark assets, product mockups that don't yet exist, anywhere the goal is "make new visual" rather than "communicate via shared reference." See [[artdirector_learnings]] for the prompt-engineering side.

**Edge case:** If Robert specifically asks for original AI art ("brief a logo concept", "generate mood references for an unbuilt world"), that's still the right tool. The rule is about *presentation visuals for personal/character communication*, not all visual work.
