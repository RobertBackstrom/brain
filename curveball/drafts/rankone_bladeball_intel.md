## 3. PC comparables + pricing read
Source: RankOne comparable analysis, 282 profiles.

| Game | Price | Peak CCU | Rating | Owners (est.) |
|------|-------|----------|--------|---------------|
| Lethal League Blaze | $19.99 | 2,673 | 95% Overwhelmingly | 200k – 500k |
| Knockout City | $19.99 → F2P | 6,027 | 90% Very Positive | 1M+ (later shut down) |
| Omega Strikers | F2P | 19,257 | 88% Very Positive | 500k – 1M |
| Laser League | $14.99 | <500 | 85% Very Positive | 50k – 100k |
| Major League Curveball (ours) | F2P (listed) | <100 | N/A (EA) | <20k |

**RankOne's strategic read (note: this pushes back on the sub-$10 brief):**
- Optimal price for a small premium launch: **$14.99 – $19.99**. A lower price "often fails to increase conversion significantly while devaluing the perceived depth."
- Position as a **reaction-brawler / physics duelist**, NOT a "sport" or "party" game.
- Lethal League Blaze = the premium success model (stylized fighting-game framing, skill expression, durable niche). Knockout City = cautionary (social-sports framing could not survive a $20 wall, went F2P then shut down). Omega Strikers = F2P content-treadmill trap for small teams.
- Affinity for the skill-first niche: Spellbreak 12.7x, Absolver 10.1x.
- Moat vs Roblox Blade Ball: make the deflection/curve logic feel more sophisticated than the original to capture pro-tier players.

**How this was reconciled in the pitch:** kept sub-$10 per Robert's brief, justified via the Knockout City lesson (a high wall kills the viral, community-scale adoption Blade Ball's mobile-native base needs), and surfaced the $14.99-19.99 trade-off openly as a lever to set with the partner. Open decision for Robert.
