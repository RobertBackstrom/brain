## 2. The comparator numbers: narrow the criticism

Verified review counts today, against what the deck claims:

| Comparator | App | Price | Released | Reviews (live) | Deck claim | Implied ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scritchy Scratchy | 3948120 | $6.99 | 18 Mar 2026 | 15,217 | 1,000,000 | 66:1 |
| Gamblers Table | 3618390 | $4.54 | 11 Jan 2026 | 2,953 | 230,000 | 78:1 |
| This Ain't Even Poker, Ya Joker | 4038320 | $5.99 | 11 Dec 2025 | 1,781 | 130,000 | 73:1 |

**All three land in a 66:1 to 78:1 band.** That internal consistency changes the diagnosis. These
are not cherry-picked inflated numbers; they are almost certainly one estimator's output, or
cross-platform totals, applied uniformly across the slide. Yesterday's framing ("two of his three
comparators are inflated", "about 3x the Steam figure") **overstates it, and Robert should not say
it that way.** If he tells Vincent the comps are inflated 3x and Vincent answers that 750k+ is the
developer's own announced cross-platform number, Robert looks like he did ten minutes of homework
instead of thirty.

The defensible version, which is also the more useful one:

1. **The numbers carry no source in the deck.** That is the actual problem. Any publisher asks.
2. **66:1 to 78:1 is the aggressive end of the review-to-sales range.** A publisher running its
   own model at 30:1 to 50:1 lands materially lower on the same three games and will ask him to
   explain the gap. Better he pre-empts it than gets caught by it.
3. **At least one number is cross-platform sitting in Steam-only context.** Scritchy Scratchy's
   750k+ is PC and mobile combined per the developer's own announcement. On Steam alone it is
   lower. Mixing the two in one column is what makes the slide look loose.

**The fix is labelling, not cutting.** Cite the source and the multiplier, split Steam from mobile,
and show a band instead of a point number. Smaller numbers he can defend beat bigger ones he
cannot, and that is the same conclusion as yesterday by a cleaner route.

Gamblers Table is no longer unverified: it exists, shipped 11 Jan 2026, 2,953 reviews at $4.54.
The 230,000 itself stays an unsourced estimate.

## 3. The best thing on that slide is not the unit numbers

argues *for* his ask rather than inviting an audit of it. Worth telling him, and it costs us
nothing.
