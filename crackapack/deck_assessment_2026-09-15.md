# Crack a Pack, publisher deck: assessment

**Date:** 2026-09-15 · **Deck:** `CrackAPack_Aug 2026.pdf`, 11 slides, dated 10 Aug 2026 ·
**Source:** Vincent (simsum) on Discord, 2026-08-10 · **Audience:** internal, Robert only ·
**Position:** advisory plus introductions, no offer

## 1. What he is asking for

1. **A publishing partner:** global distribution, marketing and ads, community, bundles, Steam
   first and third party events, trailers and creative, streamer outreach.
2. **USD 50,000 advance**, split 30K production (feature complete, card art, environment) and
   20K marketing.
3. **Production support:** QA, localization, controller support, platform ports.

The game: Steam, USD 5.49 (sub-5 at a 15 % launch discount), 10 to 15 hours, target release
**12 Dec 2026**, stated budget USD 50,000, expected **200,000 units**. Studio assets quoted:
32,000 mailing list members, 1,200 Discord members.

## 2. The short verdict

The concept is the strongest thing in the deck and the ask is genuinely small. What does not hold
up is the evidence he is using to support the number: two of his three comparators are inflated,
his own shipped title sold roughly two orders of magnitude below the projection, and a nearly
identical game beats him to market by two and a half months. None of that makes it a bad project.
It makes the deck the wrong deck to send to publishers in its current form, which is useful to
tell him, because that is what he asked for.

## 3. What checks out

1. **The genre read is right.** Incremental is growing on Steam (his own chart: 28k reviews in
   2022 to 103k in 2025), pack opening is a real content format on YouTube and TikTok, and no
   incremental has owned it. That is a fair gap.
2. **The loop is clear and cheap to build.** Crack, place, earn, automate, with card burning for
   prestige instead of a run reset. One sentence explains it, which matters in this genre.
3. **The price and length are correctly matched to the comparator set.** Sub-6 dollars, 10 to 15
   hours, no multiplayer. He is not overbuilding.
4. **He markets.** Manual Twitch outreach, Reddit organic and paid, Keymailer, a mailing list he
   actually grew. Most developers at this size do none of it.
5. **The Steam page is live** (app 4678500) and the art is finished and distinctive.

## 4. What does not check out

Verified against Steam, the developers' own announcements and third party estimates on
2026-09-15.

| Deck claim | What is actually there |
|---|---|
| Scritchy Scratchy: 1,000,000 units | Roughly 335k on Steam (Gamalytic estimate), 750k+ across PC and mobile per the developer's own announcement. The deck's number is about 3x the Steam figure. |
| This Ain't Even Poker Ya Joker: 130,000 units | The developer announced 85,000+ (Steam news, app 4038320). |
| Gamblers Table: 230,000 units | Not verified this pass. Treat as unconfirmed until it is. |
| "Expected unit sales 200,000" | No basis is given in the deck. It sits above every verified comparator except the Scritchy outlier. |

Three more things the deck does not mention:

1. **A near identical game ships first.** *Crack the Pack* (developer Doigt, app 4614210) is
   "coming soon" with a planned date of **29 Sep 2026**: booster packs, 1,000+ card variants, an
   upgrade tree, employees. That is two and a half months ahead of Crack a Pack, in the same loop,
   under a name one word apart. Whoever funds this is funding the second mover into a lane that is
   about to have a reference point, and the search and store algorithm confusion is real.
2. **simsum's shipped title did not sell at this scale.** *Gamestonk Simulator: Gone Rogue*
   (app 3349970, EA Oct 2025, 1.0 May 2026, EUR 13.99) sits at 83 % of 106 reviews with an
   estimated 2.3k copies and roughly USD 20.6k gross (Raijin, medium confidence), all time peak of
   112 concurrent players. The 200,000 projection is somewhere between 50x and 80x what the same
   team, doing the same marketing, achieved on a higher priced game. That gap needs an explanation
   in the deck, and there is not one.
3. **He already has a publisher.** Steam lists **Vsoo Games** as publisher on both Gamestonk
   Simulator and Crack a Pack. The deck asks for a publishing partner and says nothing about Vsoo.
   Until that is explained, nobody can tell what is actually being offered.

## 5. The numbers, run honestly

1. At USD 5.49, after Steam's 30 %, and allowing for VAT, regional pricing, refunds and the
   launch discount, net to the developer lands near **USD 3.00 to 3.20 per unit**.
2. A 50,000 dollar advance therefore recoups at roughly **16,000 units**. That is about 5x what
   Gamestonk Simulator sold, and about 8 % of the deck's own projection.
3. So the interesting question is not whether 200,000 is real. It is that the downside is capped
   at 50k and the breakeven is a number this team could plausibly hit. That is what makes the ask
   fundable, and it is a much better argument than the one the deck is making.
4. **He should rewrite the deck around point 3.** Cheap to fund, low breakeven, second mover with
   better art in a proven loop. That is a true story. "200,000 units because Scritchy Scratchy did
   a million" is not, and any publisher who spends ten minutes checking will find the same thing
   this memo found.

## 6. Schedule risk

The deck's roadmap says announcement in August, demo in September, full release 12 Dec 2026, with
"our genre doesn't clash with GTA" as the reasoning for the date. As of today the Steam page shows
**no demo** and a release date of **"To be announced"**. September is half gone. Either the demo
slipped or the date did, and a 12 December release with a 30k production budget still unspent
looks tight from here. Worth asking him directly rather than assuming.

## 7. Where we are actually useful

Robert's position is advisory plus introductions, so the value we can add without taking a deal:

1. **Tell him about Crack the Pack.** If he does not know, this is the most valuable thing in this
   memo. It changes his naming, his announcement beat and possibly his date.
2. **Fix the comparator slide before it goes to publishers.** Verified numbers with sources, and
   the breakeven framing from section 5. He will get further with smaller, true numbers.
3. **Explain the Vsoo situation in the deck.** Any publisher reads the Steam page. Leaving it
   unaddressed reads as either an oversight or something hidden.
4. **Introductions:** the right target is a publisher who does sub-6 dollar Steam volume titles
   and can run paid UA against a proven loop, not a premium indie publisher. Shortlist to be built.
5. **Festivals and Steam events:** a Next Fest slot and the themed Steam sale calendar are
   straightforward wins for a demo driven genre. He already knows SNF, so this is scheduling, not
   education.

## 8. What we need from him before anything else

1. What is Vsoo Games' role on Crack a Pack, and what rights do they hold on it and on Gamestonk?
2. What does he mean by publishing partner given Vsoo is already on the page?
3. Actual current wishlist count on app 4678500.
4. Is 12 December still the date, and when does the demo go up?
5. What does the 30k of production actually buy if the game has to be feature complete by then?
