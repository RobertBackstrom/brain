# RankOne as an AI data asset - the licensing thesis

Internal strategy one-pager. The artifact to walk Peter and Johan through. Sourced from the Jun 17 sync, the reconciliation note, and the State of RankOne / Future of Creation deck.

## The thesis in one line
RankOne's real asset is not the app - it is **the most authentic, human-curated taste graph in gaming**, and the buyer for that in 2026 is AI. Licensing the corpus (not the consumer product) is the play that turns RankOne's genuine strength into recurring revenue without first having to win the consumer-growth race it has been losing for five years.

## The asset - what is actually sellable
Eight years of manual curation has produced a dataset that is hard to replicate:
1. **2.6M+ human-curated game relations** (Played / Playing / Want-To-Play), growing +51% YoY - explicit, intentional taste signals, not inferred from purchase logs or playtime.
2. **413k written reviews** (+60% YoY) - natural-language opinion tied to identity and history.
3. **101k cross-platform user profiles** - longitudinal preference data spanning a user's whole gaming life, across storefronts, not locked to one platform's telemetry.

Why this is differentiated for an AI buyer:
- **Authentic and human, not scraped or synthetic.** In a market drowning in synthetic and bot-polluted data, provenance is the scarce thing. This is consented, account-tied, deliberate human curation.
- **Intent-labelled.** Want-To-Play vs Played is a clean preference/intent label - exactly the kind of structured signal a recommendation or preference model wants and rarely gets cleanly.
- **Longitudinal + cross-platform.** Most game data lives inside one walled garden (Steam, PSN, Xbox). RankOne's spans them, per identity, over years.

## Who buys it
Tiered by fit, not by logo:
1. **Games recommendation / discovery AI** (storefronts, publishers building discovery, games-data players like Newzoo / Sensor Tower / Video Game Insights). Closest fit - they already understand and price games-preference data. Peter's home turf.
2. **AI products in the gaming-adjacent space** - companion/agent products, personalization layers, anyone building "what should I play next" or in-game preference modelling.
3. **AI labs / model companies** wanting clean, rights-cleared human preference data as a training or fine-tuning/preference source. Higher ceiling, but games-taste is a narrow vertical for a general lab - treat as upside, not the base case.
4. **Data marketplaces / licensing aggregators** - a distribution channel rather than an end buyer, useful to reach #3.

Reality check: #1 and #2 are the realistic near-term revenue. #3 is the headline-grabbing version but the most likely to "underpay for a niche dataset" - which is exactly the question only Peter can answer.

## The model
- **Data licence + API/feed**, recurring (annual licence or usage-metered), not a one-off data dump - a dump destroys the asset's value.
- Sold as a **clean, anonymised / aggregated product** by default (this is also what makes the rights path workable - see gate below), with richer tiers where consent supports it.
- This is a **B2B asset line**, complementary to - not a replacement for - the consumer product, because the corpus only compounds while users keep curating. The consumer app becomes the data engine; the licence is the revenue.

## Why it is defensible
- Replicating 8 years of manual, intentional curation is slow and expensive - you cannot scrape intent.
- Retention is improving (7-day 36.3%, up from 32.8% YoY) while the base grows, so the corpus deepens, not just widens.
- Cross-platform, identity-linked, longitudinal coverage is structurally hard for any single-platform incumbent to match.

## The honest risks (name them before Peter does)
1. **Is games-taste data scarce-and-valuable, or niche-and-underpaid?** The whole thesis rests on this. It is the single highest-value question to put to Peter (ex-Newzoo) - he can price it and name the first real buyer.
2. **Data rights / consent is a hard gate.** Licensing EU users' preference data for AI use needs a lawful basis and ToS/privacy coverage that almost certainly was not written for this (GDPR purpose-limitation). Assessment in progress with the Lawyer agent - `rankone_data_rights_assessment.md`. Nothing concrete with a buyer until that clears.
3. **User-trust collision.** "We sell your taste data to AI" can spook the engaged ~20% who are the entire corpus engine. The anonymised-aggregate default and clear consent framing are how you de-risk this; the KM Troedsson-era "didn't deliver on the identity promise" trust gap is the relevant precedent.

## What we validate next
1. **Peter's lens (his unique value):** is this data scarce or underpaid, who is the realistic first buyer, and what is a credible ACV? Get this before building anything.
2. **The rights gate (Lawyer, in flight):** lawful basis + ToS/anonymisation path. Gates every buyer conversation.
3. **Proof discipline:** hold the pivot to **one paid pilot or signed LOI within ~6 months** so "reduces near-term revenue pressure" does not become "defers revenue indefinitely."
