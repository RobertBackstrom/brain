## English version - for the meeting and as a follow-up

*All figures in USD at 9,50 SEK per dollar. The share figures are the legal ones and register in SEK.*

### The shape, in one line

You get to 30 % through two legs: you buy the early minority holders' shares directly from them, and you subscribe for new shares in the company with the money you were going to put in anyway.

### Leg 1 - buying existing shares

1. The whole tail below Alexander is available: six to eight holders, **55 999 shares, 14,14 % of the company**.
2. They came in between 2020 and 2022 and have had no exit path in five years. They are sellers.
3. Roughly **$470K**. Priced per seller, not as a single company price.
4. The money goes straight from you to them. The company is not a party and the balance sheet is untouched.

### Leg 2 - a new issue to you

5. **89 790 new shares at $16.00 each** (152 SEK, the registered figure), which is **about $1.44M** paid into Aurora Punks.
6. $16.00 is the subscription price from the last registered round in August 2024. A registered fact, not a negotiating position.
7. It needs a shareholder resolution at two thirds majority, which is the formal process we have to run for any new issue regardless.

### Where you land

8. **Exactly 30,0 % of the company.**
9. **About $1.9M in total**, which sits inside the range you started from.
10. A blended entry of **$13.10 per share against the $16.00 subscription price, an 18 % discount**.
11. That puts the company at **$6.3M pre-money and $7.8M post-money**.

### Why this is cheaper for you than buying shares alone

12. Your plan: about **$1.07M** for the shares, plus the **$1.22M** you put into the company afterwards. **$2.3M in total, and the $1.22M buys you nothing.**
13. This structure: **$1.9M for the same 30 %. You save roughly $385K.**
14. It is the same money doing the same work. The difference is that it counts towards your 30 % instead of being spent on top of the purchase price.
15. It also means a **smaller total facility**, which makes your own financing easier to get approved rather than harder.
16. And you are not dependent on any single large holder deciding to sell. The tail gets you to 14 %, Alexander on top of that gets you to 26 %. The rest only exists in a new issue.

### What the $1.44M does inside the company

17. Clears about **$125K** of shareholder loans and cleans up the balance sheet, which is exactly what a lender wants to see going in.
18. Funds the Unyverse co-development.
19. Fills the contingency reserve and the prototyping fund. That maps line for line onto the use of funds in your own memo.

### Unyverse, as its own track

21. The co-development contract is priced separately on our standard economics, and it gets locked **before** signing rather than left open.
22. Straight about the size: for eighteen months with a meaningful team on our side the contract needs to sit **north of $1.5M**. Below that our recurring floor goes down rather than up, and the stability stops being stability for either of us.
23. On scope: $1-2M does not finish the game as it is described publicly. What I can build for that money is a **combat-first premium release**, character creator as the front door, six to ten hours plus replay, PC, fifteen to eighteen months. That way the two things you already have are the whole product instead of a tenth of it. Run the vertical slice as milestone one so nothing is wasted if the facility takes longer to close.

### One structural point worth taking early

24. Swedish company law does not allow a company to lend money towards an acquisition of its own shares, so the seller's note version in the memo needs replacing. This structure solves that on its own, because your equity contribution becomes a subscription rather than a loan.

### What I need from you

25. Your total cost-to-complete number for Unyverse, and what the $1-2M buys inside it.
26. Who the technical lead on production is.
27. The links to the combat test area and the character creator.
