# TCG Grading — Market & Legal Scan

**Date:** 2026-06-16
**Author:** BizDev agent
**Brief:** Robert's thesis — there's room for an EU-based card-grading service because sending cards to the US for grading is expensive and slow for Europeans. Scoped as a Pokémon-first **bolt-on to the planned TCG webshop**, with a hard look at whether card manufacturers (The Pokémon Company / Nintendo) require a licence to grade their cards.

---

## TL;DR

1. The pain is real - VAT/customs on a US round-trip can add 20%+ on the graded value, and landed cost for a single PSA Value card has been hitting €70-90.
2. But the gap is closing fast. PSA opens a full grading facility in **Frankfurt summer 2026**, killing the shipping/customs problem for the dominant brand. Several EU/UK graders already exist (Ace Grading UK, PCA France, CCC, Cardmarket Grading).
3. **No manufacturer licence is required to grade Pokémon cards.** The entire industry grades unlicensed. The only legal guardrails are trademark: don't imply Pokémon affiliation/endorsement, don't put "Pokémon" in your brand name/logo. No precedent of Pokémon suing a grader.
4. The moat in grading is **brand trust** - a PSA 10 resells for multiples of an unknown-brand 10. A de-novo EU grading brand would slab cards that carry little resale premium. That is the central risk to "start our own grader".
5. **Recommended bolt-on shape: become a submission hub / group-submitter, not a new grading brand.** Low capital, immediate value-add to the shop, rides the PSA Frankfurt tailwind instead of fighting it.

---

## 1. Market at a glance

1. Card grading services market: **~$4.1B in 2025**, projected ~$10.8B by 2034 (~11% CAGR). North America ~48% of revenue.
2. **26.6M cards graded in 2025** (record). PSA alone graded 19.26M (+26% YoY); has graded 500M cards lifetime.
3. **Pokémon is the single biggest category - 16.1M cards graded in 2025**, more than half of all volume. 75B+ Pokémon cards printed globally. This validates the Pokémon-first scope.

## 2. Competitive landscape

### US majors (the brands that carry resale value)
1. **PSA** - gold standard since 1991. ~$40+/card, 2-6 week turnaround (longer in 2025). Raised prices again in 2025.
2. **BGS / Beckett** - base from $12/card. **Acquired by Collectors (PSA's parent) in Dec 2025.**
3. **SGC** - ~$24/card, fastest (1-3 wks). Also under the Collectors roof now.
4. **CGC** - ~$18/card, 2-4 wks. The only major still independent.
5. **Consolidation flag:** three of four majors (PSA, BGS, SGC) are now one company. A NY congressman called for a monopoly investigation in Jan 2026. Relevant because it signals pricing power and an industry ripe for a credible alternative - but also that scale/trust is everything.

### EU / UK entrants (the "gap" is already partly filled)
1. **Ace Grading (UK)** - founded 2021 by creator "Randolph", Pokémon + sports focus. Sleek slabs, good dashboard, accepts UK/EU/US/Japan/AU/NZ. The closest existing analogue to Robert's idea, and proof a European Pokémon-first grader can launch.
2. **PCA (France)** - established European grader.
3. **CCC Grading** - EU, publishes international shipping guidance.
4. **Cardmarket Grading** - **DEFUNCT (corrected 2026-06-16, see addendum 2).** Was the grading arm of Cardmarket, the dominant EU marketplace. Shut down in 2024. Do NOT treat as a live competitor - the fact that it *failed* is the single most important data point in this whole scan.
5. **Beckett (BGS) Europe** - **already grading in NW Germany since Jan 28 2025**, full services, ~€20 Base / €36 Standard, grades Pokémon + all major TCG. Plus **Dave & Adam's Europe** (PSA submission centre, Netherlands) - existing EU intake points.

## 3. The EU-gap thesis — verdict

1. **The cost/pain is genuine.** Cards sent to the US and returned can be hit with import VAT (often 20%+) assessed on the *graded* value - a €1,000 Charizard can trigger a ~€200 surprise bill on delivery. Total landed cost for one PSA Value card frequently reached €70-90 before the grading fee.
2. **Workarounds already exist.** European intermediaries use the temporary-admission customs regime to avoid double VAT; UK/EU consolidators run group submissions that cut per-card cost 30-50% via bulk shipping + higher-tier pricing.
3. **The decisive change: PSA Frankfurt, summer 2026.** PSA's first full-scale Continental Europe facility (GM Matthias Peuckert). Grading inside the EU eliminates the transatlantic shipping + customs problem for the #1 brand. Expected Value tier ~$33/card (~€45-60 effective with handling). Note the catch: **Bulk Value turnaround is expected ~95 business days** - still slow.
4. **Implication:** "be the first EU grader" is no longer the wedge - that window is basically gone. The live wedges are (a) **speed** (PSA bulk is ~95 days), (b) **service/integration** (grading attached to a buy/sell shop), and (c) **trust/locality** for a specific community (Pokémon-first, EU).

## 4. Legal / licensing — the question Robert asked

**Do you need a licence from The Pokémon Company / Nintendo to grade their cards? No.**

1. **The whole industry operates without a licence.** PSA, CGC, Beckett, Ace Grading and every EU grader grade Pokémon cards with no manufacturer agreement. That is the strongest evidence the activity is legal as-is.
2. **Why it's legal:** Grading is a service performed on a physical card the owner already owns (first-sale / exhaustion principle). You're not copying or reproducing the artwork - you encapsulate the original card, so there's no copyright reproduction. Referring to "Pokémon" to say what the card is falls under nominative/descriptive fair use of the trademark.
3. **The real guardrails (trademark, not licensing):**
   - Don't use Pokémon branding (name, logo, characters) in your business/service name or marketing in a way that implies affiliation, sponsorship, or endorsement by The Pokémon Company. Their stated policy is to decline trademark/copyright use.
   - Don't put Pokémon marks on the slab/label or your storefront in a way a customer could read as "official Pokémon grading".
   - Keep your brand name and slab design clearly your own.
4. **Litigation check:** Pokémon is litigious (Palworld patent suit, $15M China copyright win, etc.) but **all of it is against counterfeiters, knock-off games, and unlicensed reproduction - none against graders.** Grading authentic cards isn't in their enemy set; graders arguably *help* them by fighting counterfeits.
5. **One genuine legal exposure to design around:** authentication liability. If you slab a fake as genuine, or mis-grade, that's a consumer/contract risk on *you*, and counterfeit Pokémon cards are a real and growing problem. This is an operational/insurance issue, not a licensing one - but it's the thing that actually bites graders.

**Bottom line for the legal question:** No licence needed. Pick a clearly-independent brand name, keep Pokémon marks off your slab and storefront chrome, and the licensing risk is effectively nil. The real legal/operational risk is authentication accuracy, not IP.

## 5. The strategic fork for a bolt-on

The "bolt-on grading" idea splits into two very different businesses:

### Option A — Grade in-house (launch our own EU grading brand)
1. **Against:** Grading's entire value is brand trust + slab liquidity. A PSA/CGC 10 resells far above an unknown-label 10. A brand-new slab carries almost no resale premium for years. Capital-heavy (equipment, trained graders, consistent standards, anti-counterfeit tooling). You'd be fighting PSA Frankfurt + Cardmarket Grading + Ace head-on.
2. **For:** Highest margin and full control *if* the brand ever earns trust. Ace Grading shows it's possible from a standing start with a creator/community engine behind it. Robert does not have that audience today.

### Option B — Submission hub / group-submitter (recommended)
1. Shop becomes an **authorized dealer / group-submitter** for PSA (and/or CGC/Ace), aggregating customer cards into bulk submissions.
2. **Why it fits the webshop:** customers buy raw cards from you, hand you cards to grade, and buy/sell graded cards - grading becomes a sticky service loop inside the shop, not a separate company.
3. **Economics:** dealers get bulk pricing + priority tiers; you take a per-card service margin + shipping/handling, with near-zero capital outlay. PSA's group-submission program and signed dealer agreements are the existing, documented path.
4. **Tailwind not headwind:** PSA Frankfurt makes being a *PSA submission hub* better (cheaper, faster, no customs), where it makes being a *competing grader* harder.
5. **Speed angle:** because PSA bulk is ~95 days, a hub that offers transparent status tracking, insured handling, and a faster tier bundled with the shop has a real service edge.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Don't start a de-novo EU grading lab.** The gap that made it attractive is being filled by PSA Frankfurt, and you'd be slabbing low-liquidity labels against trust-rich incumbents.
2. **Bolt grading onto the webshop as a submission + graded-card-trading service.** Become a PSA (and possibly CGC/Ace) authorized group-submitter; offer "drop your cards, we grade + slab + can buy them back" as a loop. Low capital, immediate differentiation vs a plain card shop.
3. **Lean on the two durable wedges:** (a) Pokémon-community trust + EU locality, (b) speed/transparency over PSA's 95-day bulk lane.
4. **If the in-house ambition stays alive,** treat it as a phase-2 only after the shop has an audience and submission volume - i.e. earn the customers first, then consider a house slab. Ace Grading is the template, but it was built on an existing creator audience.
5. **Open questions to resolve before any build:** (a) is PSA Frankfurt taking on dealer/group-submitter partners at launch, and on what terms; (b) Cardmarket Grading's pricing/positioning (closest competitor that also owns the EU marketplace); (c) authentication-liability insurance for counterfeit exposure.

## Addendum — PSA Frankfurt dealer path & batch-submission economics (2026-06-16)

Deep-dive on the recommended Option B (submission hub), prompted by Robert.

### PSA Europe launch sequence — the dealer door opens FIRST
1. **Dealer/partner submissions open July 2026**, grading starts **August 2026**, full public launch later in the year. PSA is deliberately starting with **authorized dealers and partners** "to build the right foundation". So the dealer channel is the earliest way in, and being an EU dealer at launch is a genuine first-mover slot.
2. Coverage: continental Europe + Ireland. Facility is PSA's first full-scale EU operation, part of a $200M global capex push, 100 new hires.
3. **Capacity warning:** in May 2026 PSA *paused its Value (cheapest) tiers* in the US due to a demand spike and launched a public backlog tracker. So the low-cost bulk lane is supply-constrained - you can't reliably promise customers the cheapest/fastest tier as a dealer.

### The PSA Authorized Dealer gate — this is the catch for a brand-new shop
To become a PSA Authorized Dealer you must:
1. Have been a **full-time dealer for at least 2 years**.
2. Hold **capitalization of $10,000+** including inventory.
3. Provide **3 credit references + 3 PSA Authorized Dealer references**.
4. Have **previously endorsed PSA** (graded cards with them, bought/sold PSA-graded cards).
5. No felony convictions (key employees) in 5 years; sign the PSA Dealer Application and be appointed.

**Implication:** a webshop that doesn't exist yet cannot meet the 2-year-trading + 3-dealer-references bar on day one. The authorized-dealer status is a 12-24 month *destination*, not a launch move. Three realistic sequencing options:
- **(a) Reseller/concierge first.** Launch grading concierge on the shop using an *existing* EU authorized dealer's account behind the scenes (white-label / sub-submit). Slim margin, zero gate, starts building the PSA trading history that qualifies you later.
- **(b) Partner with an existing authorized dealer** (e.g. Black Label Grading is already an official PSA UK & EU dealer) for a revenue split, co-branded.
- **(c) Build toward direct authorized-dealer status** over ~2 years of trading, then bring grading fully in-house on the shop.

### The model already exists in the UK — and that's the opening for an EU/Sweden play
Two UK businesses run *exactly* the batch-submission play Robert described:
1. **Black Label Grading** - Official PSA UK & EU dealer since 2024. Pure intermediary: managed logistics, customs handling, consolidated shipping, real-time tracking, "same grading standards as direct PSA". Pricing (GBP, per card): Value Bulk £23, Reholder £30, Booster £35, Regular £70, Express £135, Super Express £395. Cheap tiers ship **once a month** to batch for cost. Value Bulk + Value Plus currently **CLOSED** (mirrors PSA's paused Value tiers). No minimum order.
2. **The Sub Center** (Herne Bay, Kent) - multi-grader middleman (PSA, TAG, Beckett, CGC), physical drop-off store, £1M goods-in-trust insurance, Euro-Grade-6 safe, CCTV, API order tracking. Positions as "UK's leading middleman service".

**The fresh wedge:** both are **UK** businesses. Post-Brexit, a UK middleman shipping to PSA still crosses a customs border, and so does serving EU customers. A **Sweden/EU-based** sub center feeding **PSA Frankfurt is fully intra-EU - no customs border either direction.** That is a structural cost/speed advantage no UK incumbent can match, and it's the actual gap (not "no EU grader exists", but "no EU-native, EU-Frankfurt-fed batch hub serving the Nordic/EU Pokémon market").

### How the spread works (the revenue model to copy)
A batch-submission hub makes money on five layers, all visible in Black Label's structure:
1. **Per-card markup** over PSA dealer pricing (dealer rates are below public rates).
2. **Batch consolidation** - one bulk shipment + higher-tier volume pricing, charged to customers at fixed per-card fees.
3. **FX spread** (charge in SEK/EUR, pay PSA in USD).
4. **Value-charge handling** when a card grades above its tier cap.
5. **Attach revenue** - the shop sells the raw card, grades it, and can buy back / resell the slab. This loop is the real prize and the bit a pure grader can't replicate.

### Recommended next moves
1. **Email PSA Europe / dealer relations now** (dealer applications open July) to get the actual EU dealer terms, fee schedule, and partner criteria in writing - confirm the 2-year/references gate applies in the EU and ask what "partner" (vs full authorized dealer) status involves at launch.
2. **Price a pilot on the reseller/concierge model** (Option a) so grading can go live on the shop without waiting on dealer status.
3. **Get the Cardmarket Grading pricing/partner terms** - they're the one EU competitor that also owns the marketplace; need to know if they're beatable on Pokémon-Nordic focus + speed.
4. **Insurance quote** for goods-in-trust + counterfeit/mis-grade liability (the one real legal exposure from the main scan).

## Addendum 2 — Cardmarket Grading comp: the cautionary tale (2026-06-16)

Dug into Cardmarket Grading as "the closest competitor". **It turns out to be the closest *precedent for failure*, not a live competitor** - and it's the most useful single data point in this scan.

### What it was
1. Launched **late 2021** as the grading arm of **Cardmarket** - the dominant EU TCG marketplace (German-based, EU-wide, the natural buyer/seller network any EU card shop wants).
2. Partner/operator: **Guard and Grading Solutions (GGS)**, a DGuSV-certified German appraisal company with experienced graders. So it had a credentialed grading operation behind it.
3. Model: 1-10 scale, clean German-made slabs with a **QR code linking to a population report**, **integrated directly into the Cardmarket marketplace**. This is almost exactly the bolt-on Robert sketched - marketplace + in-house EU grading, no transatlantic shipping.

### What happened
1. **It died.** By 2024 Cardmarket ended the partnership; grading could no longer be bought through the platform. Branding reverted to GGS, which now does mostly **recasing / protective cases**, not new grading.
2. The **facility it ran in is now Beckett's European grading operation** - the trusted US brand moved into the building the EU-native experiment vacated. That's the whole thesis in one image.

### Why this is the most important finding in the scan
1. **The strongest-possible version of "marketplace-integrated EU grading" already tried and failed.** Cardmarket had the audience, the brand, the EU marketplace, AND a certified German grading partner - every advantage a new entrant lacks - and its own slabs still didn't stick. The reason is the moat from the main scan: **liquidity/trust.** Even on Cardmarket's own marketplace, buyers wanted PSA, not a Cardmarket/GGS slab. A house slab with no resale premium is a product nobody asks for twice.
2. **This kills Option A (start our own EU grading brand) about as conclusively as a market can.** If Cardmarket couldn't, a Nordic webshop won't.
3. **Cardmarket itself pivoted to exactly the model we recommend** - it now stays a neutral marketplace and lists **third-party grading via partner apps/services** (TCG PowerTools, TCG Home, GGS) rather than grading itself. So Cardmarket is **not a competitor to a submission hub - it's a potential channel/integration partner.**

### The corrected EU landscape (this changes the picture)
The EU is filling up fast, and specifically with the **trusted brands**, not new EU labels:
1. **Beckett (BGS)** - grading in NW Germany since **Jan 2025**, full services, ~€20 Base / €36 Standard, Pokémon + all TCG. Live today.
2. **PSA** - Frankfurt, dealer-first July 2026, grading Aug 2026.
3. So by late 2026 the two most trusted slabs are both produced **inside Germany**. The "expensive/slow to ship to the US" pain - the original thesis - is being solved by the incumbents themselves, intra-EU.

### Net read after the comp
1. The opportunity is **not a grading brand** (Cardmarket proved it dies) and increasingly **not even "EU grading access"** (PSA + Beckett both in Germany). Those wedges are closing.
2. What's left is durable and unsexy: the **service + retail-attach layer** - be the trusted **Nordic/EU Pokémon shop** that handles submission/logistics to PSA-Frankfurt (and/or Beckett) for customers, batches Nordic volume to Germany intra-EU, and runs buy/sell around the slab. The grading is a commodity you broker; the **shop + trust + convenience** is the business.
3. Robert is Sweden-based: even intra-EU, a Nordic customer still ships to Germany. A **Nordic-consolidating hub** (drop-off/mail-in in SE/Nordics → one batch to Frankfurt → return) is the concrete, defensible version of the play. No UK incumbent and no German grader is serving that specific seam directly.
