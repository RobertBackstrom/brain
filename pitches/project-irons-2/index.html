Starting point

How we read the brief

Two bodies of work with different risk profiles. The four new heists are content production with a known shape. The systems work carries the unknowns, because several items depend on the PUBG engine and on decisions still open on your side.

Content

RankOne Insights behavioural panel, 3 109 measured PUBG: Battlegrounds profiles, August 2026. Over-index against the average player: 1.00x is baseline, 2.53x is two and a half times average. The ratios measure what pulls this audience toward a mode, not what they play once inside it. Absolute panel shares run higher than population shares, so we do not lean on them.

What we would raise early

  • The crossover affinity runs through PAYDAY 2. It is strong in both directions, roughly 4x either way, but it is PAYDAY 2 players who overlap with PUBG, not PAYDAY 3 players. That matters for which community the cosmetics, masks and marketing are aimed at
  • The reachable audience over-indexes on loud cooperative play and sits at baseline on stealth. Our proposal is to introduce the stealth ingredients per heist and measure them, not to cut them
  • The stealth feedback we could find was about the reward, not the mechanics. One first-hand account, from a player who completed heists in stealth in the first mode, called the 10% completion bonus too small to be worth playing for. Single voice. If your telemetry points the same way, tuning the payout through the new currency economy is cheaper than building any new sensor type
  • A complexity budget per heist, set against the first project as the baseline, defined up front rather than trimmed at Beta when the cuts are expensive

What would settle it

  • Krafton telemetry: adoption share of the PC base, the stealth versus loud split, completion and retry rates, week-over-week fall-off, event pass attach
  • That dataset resolves several of the open scope questions in one meeting
  • Everything above is measured behaviour, not reported sentiment
  • Full analysis, sources and confidence levels, we bring to the meeting
Approach

How we want to run it

Capacity arrives when the work for it exists. A content team that lands before the technical picture is locked spends its first months producing work that gets revised. Seniority is front-loaded, the picture is locked in September, production follows it.

Senior core first

Seven people in September, all leads or senior specialists. They land the technical evaluation and a locked First Playable plan before a single content role is added.

Leads on-site

The lead group sits at your office, on your build, in your standups and reviews. Production roles work between your office and ours, with the split set per phase.

Ramp on the content

Level design and art scale up as heists move into production and scale down as they close. Specialist disciplines come in for the phases that need them and step off after.

One accountable team

An executive producer accountable to you for the whole delivery, not a set of individually placed contractors. Single point of contact, single point of escalation.

QA outside the core

QA runs as a dedicated external function that scales per milestone, so verification capacity is not competing with production capacity for the same headcount.

Engine work on us

We carry the engine and 3C work rather than routing it through the PUBG team. The code and tech art leads open that thread in week one, so the scope is known before content production starts.

Headcount

Why the team is this size

The team is roughly half the size of the plan you outlined. What keeps it down on the left, where it would grow on the right.

What keeps it down

  • The evaluation month - most oversizing comes from producing before the technical picture is settled and then paying for the revisions. September settles it first
  • Heists move through a pipeline - level design is staffed to the number of heists actually in concurrent production, which is what sets the Alpha 1 peak of four rather than a flat allocation across the project
  • Continuous leads - every lead role runs from September through Gold. Ramping happens in the production layer and never in the leadership layer, which is where hand-over cost usually shows up
  • Sequenced specialists - UI, lighting and systems design each get the phase where they are the focus, instead of a seat held for thirteen months
  • QA outside the team - verification scales on its own and does not take production seats

Where it would go up

  • More heists in parallel than four, or a wider HR1 upgrade than the one scoped here
  • Engine and 3C scope landing heavier than the September evaluation indicates
  • A shorter schedule. This plan trades headcount for calendar, and that trade can be reversed
  • Live or seasonal content after Gold, which is outside this plan
On team size

This is the lean version and it is not a constraint we are attached to. If your agreement with Krafton is priced per head, we can staff deeper and run more heists in parallel. If it is a fixed price, a lean team protects your margin. Tell us which one you are working against.

Quality assurance

QA as an external function

QA as a dedicated external contractor function rather than fixed seats inside the development team, priced as a separate line at 500 SEK per hour and billed on hours used. A full-time QA month is 80 000 SEK. It scales to the milestone instead of sitting flat across the project, and it does not consume core team capacity.

How it works

  • Scaled per milestone - light during production phases, heavy into each milestone build and through certification
  • Functional and certification QA both covered, including console compliance passes
  • Reporting into the project daily, with the producer owning triage and the bug budget
  • 500 SEK per hour, billed on actual hours, separate from the development retainer

Why outside the team

  • Verification capacity flexes independently of production capacity
  • You are not paying for peak QA during phases that do not need it
  • Verification stays independent from the people producing the work
  • Volume agreed per milestone once the build cadence is set, so the cost follows the actual need

The four decisions behind this ordering

  • The HR1 upgrades sit late, at Alpha 2. The four existing stages are being brought up to a systems baseline that does not exist until the new systems are locked. Upgrading them earlier means upgrading them twice
  • The heists are staggered, not parallel. One proves the pipeline at First Playable, the second follows it, and three and four run together once the pipeline is known. That is what lets level design peak at three people instead of five
  • Stealth ships in two sets. The first set goes in early enough to be measured. The second is gated on what the first set and your telemetry show
  • Engine work is front-loaded and fenced. It starts in September and finishes at Pre-Alpha, with a go or no-go against a movement fallback at Alpha 1. Nothing downstream is allowed to depend on it past that gate

Constraints in the schedule

  • September produces decisions, not content. Seven and a half people cover it
  • February to May is the peak at 23. Heist production, systems, art and UI overlap through those months
  • Nothing new starts after May. June and July are convergence only: the HR1 upgrades finishing, balance, tutorial, cosmetics and stabilization
  • August is certification and nothing else
  • QA runs as a band underneath, scaling to each milestone build and widening through certification, outside the developer count

Before we start

Open items and due diligence

The first group blocks the start and needs resolving before September. The second is the due diligence our lead team runs during the month, pitched at pipeline and structure rather than at individual features. The feature-level decisions still on your TBD list get costed as a by-product of it.

Needed to start

  • Build and trunk access, plus the design documentation behind the first project
  • Desk space for the on-site group, ramping from a handful in September to a peak in spring
  • The 3C and movement scope, written down. We need to know what you intend to change before we can cost it. Our number here is an assumption, not a calculation, and it is the largest single variable in the plan
  • A named technical counterpart on the PUBG side and an agreed way of working on engine changes
  • Krafton guidance on the restart and escape system, which you flagged as likely needed

What our leads assess in September

  • Build and tools - build times, pipeline health, and how long it takes to get from a change to something playable. This number sets the pace of everything after it
  • Art pipeline - what the PAYDAY 3 asset path actually gives us inside the PUBG engine, and the real cost per asset class rather than the assumed one
  • Tech debt - what the first project left behind, how much of it we inherit, and the state of the engine fork we would be working in
  • Console baseline - current performance against target budget, and the certification risks that follow from it
  • Design challenges - the structural ones rather than the feature list: how far stealth can go for this particular audience, whether the progression rework holds up against how the mode is actually played, and where the difficulty curve lands
Questions

What we would want to talk through

Questions for a first working session. Some move the plan and the cost. Some are player-experience calls that are cheaper to settle now than after something is built.

Scope, and what it costs

  • 3C and movement, in detail. This is the single biggest scope factor in the plan and the one we can estimate least well from outside. We would want it broken down: which specific changes to movement and feel are in, who signs off on each, what the acceptance test looks like, and how much of it runs on the PUBG team's calendar rather than ours. Until that list exists, our number for this workstream is an estimate
  • Game mechanics and side objectives. Your own list says scope and complexity here still needs defining, and floats removing the high-complexity side objectives. We would like that conversation early and with player expectations on the table, because it costs a fraction in September of what it costs at Beta

Player experience, and what we are aiming at

  • EXP into virtual currency. Worth deciding whether this is a genuinely spendable currency or a renamed unlock counter. A real one needs sinks, pricing and exploit review, which is systems work rather than a feature toggle. There is also the question of whether it connects to PUBG's own economy or lives only inside the mode. That reads as a franchise decision more than a design one, and it probably needs Krafton in the room
  • Stealth mechanics against stealth systems. Your avoid-list tells us clearly what you do not want to build. What we would like to hear is the player experience you are aiming at, because that decides how many ingredient types are worth building and how they should be introduced. Our own view is that how well stealth pays through the new currency matters at least as much as how many tools it offers
  • Extraction and escape. Partial escape is the feature we found the clearest player demand for, but the edge cases decide whether it lands: reward splits, leaver behaviour, spectate flow, and what stops three players extracting while the fourth holds the timer. What is the expectation, and how much of it is Krafton's ruling rather than a design call we can make?
  • Menus and UI. If you have telemetry on where players stall in the menus, or a list of the friction you already know about from the first mode, that is what we would design against

None of these block a start. They are the things to decide in September rather than assume and correct later.
