The input layer is not part of your game today

On Luna the phone-as-controller pairing, the input relay and the speech recognition are Amazon platform services, running in Amazon's data centre alongside the game instance. The game receives clean events and never handles audio. On a console none of those services exist. Room-code join, companion client, relay and speech recognition all become part of the product.

The engine is Unity, confirmed at the meeting, which is the stack our porting lead ships on. What is still unknown is how separable the Luna input layer is from game logic. Section 08 states what moves if it is woven through.

Speech recognition: three options

A. On the relay Recommended

Phone captures audio and sends it to the relay. Relay returns text. The console receives words and button events, never audio, and loads no speech model.

Effect: no speech model in any console memory budget, which is what makes Switch 1 tractable. One identical code path on all six SKUs.

Cost: voice play requires a network connection, and a per-session ASR cost we size at Gate 1 once a vendor is chosen. Audio leaving the device is a data-protection question on a family title: the publisher is data controller, not us, and we host the relay only during Gates 2 to 6. Long-term hosting and controllership need an owner before launch.

B. On the console

No network dependency, no running cost, nothing leaves the room. Viable because this is not open dictation: at buzz-in the game already knows the correct response, so the task is scoring audio against a small candidate set. Tens of megabytes, not gigabytes.

Cost: memory and CPU on the weakest SKU. Proposed as a switchable second backend, not the primary.

C. No voice

Gamepad only. Multiple choice or on-screen keyboard, buzzer on a face button. The entire input layer above disappears.

Cost: roughly a third of the price, and a different product. Priced in section 06 so the floor is on the table.

The decision is made at Gate 1 in December, before the main port starts. The deliverable of that gate is a written architecture decision, not a build.

Submissions are not the same count as SKUs

Sony runs SIEA, SIEE and SIEJA as separate companies. Certification, age ratings, pricing and store pages are handled per territory. A primary build normally clears global FQA once and is then submitted to each regional facility, but each territory is still its own submission with its own pass and its own failure mode. Microsoft and Nintendo do not work this way. In one territory the six SKUs are six submission packages. Across all three they are ten. This estimate assumes one territory; the variation is priced in section 06 and it is question 1 in section 08.

Consequence

For four players answering aloud in one room across all six SKUs, the phone microphone is the only one reliably present. The relay therefore has to carry audio, not button events alone.

Old-gen

PS4 and Xbox One are included and ride on the next-gen work. Removing both takes roughly two months off the porting engineer and is the one line in this estimate that separates cleanly.

07Assessment

SWOT

SStrengths

  • Rendering load is low. A quiz title is not GPU or frame-budget constrained on any SKU, including Switch 1. The usual reason ports get expensive does not apply.
  • The port half is routine and predictably priced. Platform bring-up, save data, trophies, store hooks and cert checklists are work we do continuously.
  • Relay-side speech gives one code path across six SKUs. No per-platform microphone handling, and no speech model in any console's memory budget.
  • Room code plus phone browser is a proven console pattern. Over a decade of shipped precedent, so platform-holder acceptance is not an open question.
  • The pre-phase does not need your source. Gate 1 runs on your design and the current build; Gate 2 proves the companion path against a stand-in console harness we build. Source is first required at Gate 3 in March, which buys roughly three months of slack against the Amazon approvals.
  • The expensive layer is paid for once. Second title reuses relay, companion client and all platform integration.

WWeaknesses

  • No source access yet. An estimate made without code typically moves by around twenty per cent in either direction once it is read. This is the weakest part of the number, and there is no contingency line in it.
  • Engine and coupling unconfirmed. If Luna input handling is woven through game logic rather than isolated behind an interface, the port side grows.
  • The relay is a service, not a deliverable that ends. Hosting, uptime and running cost need an owner after launch. Currently unassigned.
  • Voice play requires a network connection. A living-room party game that needs the internet to work fully is a support surface and a review-score surface.
  • Switch 1 still needs a memory pass. Moving speech off-device removes the largest risk, not the work.
  • Four delivery roles, no redundancy. One porting engineer across six SKUs is a single point of failure for twelve months.
  • The title is not finished. The pre-phase works against a build that is still changing, so some of what we design in December and prototype in February gets revisited in March. Priced in, but it is why Gate 2 is a decision gate and not a deliverable we bank.

OOpportunities

  • Old-gen is cheap on top. PS4 and Xbox One ride the next-gen work, and the installed base for a family quiz title is still there.
  • Switch 2 is cheap once Switch 1 is done, and it has a system microphone. Not free: a native SKU needs its own devkit, SDK and lotcheck submission. Switch 1 back-compat already runs there, so a native build is a choice rather than a requirement.
  • The companion stack is reusable. Beyond the second quiz title, it fits any future party title from the studio.
  • Amazon covering QA and release management removes a line that is normally significant on a six-SKU submission.
  • Nov 2027 puts release-ready in the holiday quarter. See the matching threat: the submissions that get it there land in the busiest certification window of the year.
  • The companion layer is the defensible part. Once built, phone-join plus voice is a capability a straight port vendor does not have, and it carries to any future party title.

TThreats

  • Source access is gated by Amazon with no committed date. Everything after Gate 1 moves with it.
  • Jeopardy! console rights may be scoped to Luna. Clearance is outside our control and sits on the critical path. Cheapest to start in parallel with Gate 1.
  • Three platform holders, six SKUs, independent failure. A failed submission costs weeks, not days, and they do not fail together.
  • Publishing ownership unsettled. Amazon versus Bandit Island changes who submits, who holds accounts and who is liable at cert. Must be settled before Gate 5.
  • If Amazon does not cover QA and release management, roughly 600 000 SEK appears late in the project rather than at the start.
  • Feature and content changes before the February launch land as rework on the companion layer, which is the part we build first. A shipped title would not carry this; an unreleased one does.
  • A slip in your February target moves our March start. The pre-phase absorbs a delay to source access; it does not absorb a delay to the game being finished.
  • Privacy review on audio leaving the device is a per-platform question on a family title, with GDPR art. 8 and, for a US release, COPPA behind it. Answerable, but we do not control that calendar and the publisher, not us, is data controller.
  • Licensor approval cycles, not just rights. A licensed Jeopardy! already shipped on PS4 and Xbox One in November 2017 under Ubisoft, so a prior console licence exists and may still carry exclusivity. Beyond clearance, a licensor typically approves builds and store assets at each milestone, which sits directly on the Gate 5 to 6 calendar.
  • September to October is the most congested certification window of the year. Six submissions across three platform holders all land in it.
  • ASR running cost and vendor lock-in. Per-session cost is small at launch volumes and unquantified at scale. Vendor is chosen at Gate 1; switching later is not free.

Questions for you

Answering these turns assumptions into scope. The first one moves the certification effort more than anything else on the page.

1

Which PlayStation territories are in scope: SIEA, SIEE, SIEJA, or a subset?

Each is a separate submission, certification pass, age rating and store page. One territory means six submission packages; all three means ten. Priced in section 06 at 60 000 to 120 000 SEK per additional territory.

2

Are PS4 and Xbox One in or out?

They ride on the next-gen work and are currently in. Out removes roughly two months of the porting engineer, 240 000 SEK. It is the one line that separates cleanly.

3

Do you want a native Switch 2 SKU, or is Switch back-compat enough at launch?

Back-compat runs the Switch 1 build on Switch 2 with no extra submission. A native SKU needs its own devkit, SDK and lotcheck, and it is the only one of the six with a system microphone.

4

Who publishes on console, and who holds the platform accounts?

Amazon or Bandit Island. It does not change the engineering, but it decides who submits, who owns the store pages and who is liable at certification. Needs settling before Gate 5, not during it.

5

Is Amazon covering QA and release management, confirmed rather than assumed?

The estimate excludes both on that basis. If it turns out otherwise, add roughly 600 000 SEK for a release manager across the certification window and a QA pass per platform.

6

Does the Jeopardy! licence already extend to console distribution?

Not our work, but it is on our critical path. A licensed Jeopardy! shipped on PS4 and Xbox One in 2017, so a prior console licence exists and may carry terms that matter here. Worth starting in parallel with Gate 1 rather than discovering at submission.

7

Source and build access, and half an hour with whoever built the Luna input and voice layer.

The access is gated by Amazon and we are not pushing. The half hour is not, and it would sharpen Gate 1 considerably.
