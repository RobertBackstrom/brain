The opportunity

The Blade Ball audience, and the gap on PC

Blade Ball launched on Roblox in 2023 and quietly became one of the platform's most-played PvP experiences. The loop is simple to read and hard to master: a homing ball accelerates around an arena, you deflect it with a timed swing, last player standing wins. It has spawned an entire category - "Blade Ball games" - that now reaches tens of millions of players every month. Every successful version so far lives inside Roblox. The players who want a deeper, better-running, cosmetics-driven version on PC do not have one.

The game

Major League Curveball: the existing build

The Gang built Curveball in Unreal Engine 5.3. It is content-complete rather than a prototype: a polished take on the Blade Ball loop with a live Steam page, including 4 arenas, 38 weapons, 11 character skins, 14 abilities, online PvP and co-op. The core is finished. It was never taken to market.

Hit the ball

The homing ball targets a player and speeds up with every deflect. Send it at an opponent and avoid getting tagged.

Time your swing

Deflection is pure timing. Block at arm's length to redirect the ball toward the player nearest your cursor.

Three hits and out

Take three strikes and you are eliminated. Last player standing wins the round. Matches are short.

The core loop

4

5

Abilities add the depth

Defensive

Invulnerability, invisibility, slow-mo. Short windows of safety that can decide a round.

Offensive

Extra damage, faster ball, decoy balls. Tools to apply pressure and create openings.

Movement

Increased speed, higher jumps, teleportation. Reposition, escape, or close the gap on command.

Arenas and expression

4 arenas, each a different test

  • MLC Arena - the baseline, no distractions, the true test of skill
  • Japanese Garden - pillars block sightlines and ball paths
  • Himalayan Peak - environmental hazard zones to avoid
  • Rafts - the floor breaks apart on impact, fall in and you are out

Cosmetics, never pay-to-win

  • Skins - full-body avatars, with FX and animations at the high end
  • Weapons - 38 blades, from a classic bat to balloon swords and rackets
  • Emotes - a quick-chat wheel to celebrate or flame in the heat of a round
  • Zero gameplay impact - cosmetics are pure self-expression
Positioning

Why a premium PC Early Access

The Blade Ball audience has only ever been served free-to-play inside Roblox, and the attempts to take the genre to PC and console have not landed. That leaves an opening: a paid, polished, standalone PC version for the dedicated players who over-index on PC and console and want depth, better performance, and cosmetics worth owning. A sub-$10 paid Early Access turns the existing community's interest into day-one revenue, keeps the audience committed, and gives a paying base to build live content on, without the cost and risk of a full free-to-play live-ops launch up front.

On price

A sub-$10 entry is a deliberate choice. Knockout City is the cautionary case: a polished dodgeball PvP that launched at $19.99, where the premium price limited the play-with-friends adoption a community-scale game depends on, and it moved to free-to-play before shutting down. Blade Ball's core audience is mobile-native and used to free play, so a low entry price keeps adoption accessible while still filtering for committed players and generating revenue from day one. The counter-case: comparable skill-first premium titles such as Lethal League Blaze did well at $14.99-$19.99 by positioning as a reaction-brawler. Final price is a decision to make with the partner.

Proposed structure

Funding, recoup and revenue split

A starting point for the conversation, built on the structure Aurora Punks and Light Up Games have used before. Final terms to be agreed between the partners.

Funding & recoup

  • Low development cost - the game is content-complete, so the finishing work (P2P + PC polish) to reach Early Access comes in at under 100K SEK (~$9K). Most of the budget goes to marketing, not development risk
  • Co-funded - Aurora Punks and Light Up Games jointly fund the finishing pass and the Early Access launch
  • Recoupable spend - development funding plus publishing spend (marketing, QA, localization) recouped 100% before developer royalties

Revenue split

  • After recoup - developer receives the majority of net receipts, publishers retain the balance (e.g. 70 / 30 net, per our prior structure)
  • Marketing budget - a capped, jointly agreed launch and live budget
  • Publisher roles - Aurora Punks on co-dev and lite publishing, Light Up Games on funding, marketing and global reach

Indicative only. Specific MG amount, split percentages, marketing cap and milestone schedule to be set with Light Up Games and The Gang.
