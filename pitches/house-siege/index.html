04Core loop

Salvage

Your material asks whether building and repairing is as much fun as destroying. We have not found a game where it is. Teardown, Brick Rigs, Besiege and Instruments of Destruction are all pure destruction.

So we propose that the defender's verb becomes destructive too.

Defenders have no build resources and no menu to place things from. Everything they board a gap with has to be ripped out of the house first. You need to block the front door, there is no pile of planks, there is a staircase. You tear out the staircase, drag it over and slam it against the door.

We call it salvage. Furniture is free salvage: sofas, doors, bathtubs, fridges, the piano. Once the loose furniture is gone, the only thing left to salvage is load bearing structure, and taking that lowers your own Integrity.

Defending is as physical as attacking; nobody stands still holding a button. Every barricade costs something, so late in a round you are trading structure for time. And the map is reshaped by the defence as well as the attack: you tore out the staircase, so now you cannot get upstairs either.

The loop, fifteen to twenty five seconds

GRAB
Rip something loose. A door, a bathtub, a fridge, a load bearing beam.
HAUL
Carry it. Heavy, awkward, you are slow and you look stupid.
SLAM
Put it down hard. Defenders board a gap. Attackers use the same object as a ram or a ramp.
BREAK
Someone destroys what you just placed. The loop starts again.

Both teams use the same four verbs. One interaction model instead of two is cheaper to prototype and easier to balance.

05Combat

Physics combat, no weapons

Your material does not say what happens when two players meet in a corridor. Our proposal: nobody carries a weapon and nobody dies.

Players get staggered, knocked down or buried for ten to fifteen seconds, then dug out by a teammate or they recover on their own. No health bars, no respawn screens, no kill feed, no scoreboard of kills. The only number on screen is House Integrity.

Shove

You have hands. Push someone off a ledge, through the hole you just made in the floor, or into the path of something heavy that is already falling.

Swing what you carry

You are already hauling a bathtub. Swinging it needs no separate combat animation set, weapon art or balance table.

Drop your load

Release the fridge on whoever is underneath you. The object is already simulated, so this costs nothing extra to build.

Cut the support

Bring the floor down while three of them are standing on it. Attackers do this to break in. Defenders do this to bury the people who just broke in.

Rig it

Defenders can rig a barricade so it collapses when touched.

Bait the collapse

The house telegraphs what is about to fall. Stand where the other team will follow you under it.

Weapons

Your material lists demolition tools, explosives, rockets and fighting room by room. That is a shooter. With weapons the budget roughly doubles, because weapons mean gunfeel, ballistics, hit registration, weapon art, animation sets, a damage model and a permanent balance job. The comparison set stops being the games on the previous page and becomes Rainbow Six Siege, which has had a decade and a AAA team. And the audience changes from people who want to laugh with their friends to people who want to win.

Physics only combat costs little extra, because every object is already simulated for the destruction system.

06Skill

How good can a player get?

This decides the controls, the pace, the animation budget and the live balancing load, so it has to be settled before step two. Two options.

Execution skill
The Fortnite model

Building is fast and precise. Top players place structures in fractions of a second, and the ceiling is in hand speed and muscle memory. Mastery looks spectacular and creates a competitive scene.

What it costs: a very high ceiling only stays fun with constant balancing, and it needs a large population to matchmake fairly. In a 3v3 game one practised player beats everyone, and a friend who joins later cannot keep up.

Requires a permanent balance and matchmaking team, which is not in this budget.
Knowledge skill
Recommended

Carrying is slow and awkward on purpose. One object at a time. No build menu, no hotkeys, no inventory. You physically cannot build fast, so nobody can out click you.

The ceiling is in reading the house. Knowing which wall is load bearing. Knowing that pulling the staircase cuts the attackers' route but also your own. Knowing when a collapse is two hits away and standing so the other team follows you under it.

Needs no live balancing team.

We recommend the second. Friends of mixed ability have to be able to play together. Slow and heavy is funnier than fast and precise. A knowledge ceiling deepens through new houses rather than through patches.

This is a reversible decision only until step two starts. After that it is in the controls, the animation and the netcode, and changing it is a rebuild.

07The risks

What the plan has to answer

Five risks, and how the plan answers each.

Defending has to be fun
Salvage and physics combat are our answer. Step zero tests it for 180,000 kronor before anything else is committed.
Destruction plus multiplayer
Teardown has no multiplayer. Our approach is to synchronise damage events rather than physics state: only which pieces broke is sent, and the debris flies slightly differently for each player. This is standard practice.
PvP needs people
A game for six players needs six players online at the same time. Bots cover that and have their own milestone in step two.
Balance is a running cost
Asymmetric games need tuning for years. Rainbow Six Siege has been at it for a decade. That argues for fewer tools and clearer roles, for switching sides every round, and for the knowledge ceiling in the previous section rather than the execution one.
Price point
Your material describes a premium title. The genre House Siege fits sits at six to eight dollars. We recommend the lower price. It needs deciding before step one.
08Art direction

Flatpack

The house should visibly be built from parts that click together, like flat pack furniture. That makes destruction readable, because you can see exactly which piece came loose, and it makes production cheap, because one kit covers six houses.

This audience reads over polished marketing material as a sign that there is no game behind it. Rough but coherent reads as somebody building something. The titles in the tables above all look deliberately simple.

References, and what we take from each

Colour instead of detail

A piece goes from intact to cracked to gone through colour, not texture detail. Readable at small sizes and cheaper to produce.

One costume slot per character

Blank figures with one swappable layer. That opens a route to cosmetics later without building character art now.

Fixed wide camera

You need to see the whole house fall, and the clip needs to work at feed size.

Images above are the reference titles on Steam, shown to fix the target. They are not House Siege artwork. Concept art in this direction is the first deliverable of step one, and we would produce it as real renders from the actual build rather than as generated images.

09Step zero

One month to find out if it is fun

Step zero builds the loop in grey boxes and plays it. One month, one programmer, a designer at half time.

What gets built

One room made of grey cubes. Salvage, carry, slam, break. Shove and drop. One wall that can be broken into pieces and one support that can be cut. Local co-op on one machine or two on a LAN. Nothing else at all.

What does not get built

No art, no characters, no menus, no netcode, no bots, no matchmaking, no progression, no sound beyond placeholders.

Month 0Grey box prototype1.5 person months
Programmer at full time, design and direction at half time. Ends with a build on your machine and an hour of us playing it together.
180,000SEK

Cash portion 126,000. This is the entire commitment required to start.

The decision at the end is yours. If the loop is not fun, we say so and you stop at 180,000. If it is fun, the build is the base for the step one trailer.

10Step one

The store page, before the game

The goal is a published Steam page, a trailer that looks like gameplay, and around thirty vertical clips for TikTok, Reels and Shorts. Without building the full game.

The method is a trailer build. Collapses are authored as baked simulations and played back as animation, so we need no destruction engine in this step. The house is built on a purchased modular kit that we give its own identity through a custom shader, a locked palette and an original character silhouette. That is what stops the kit looking bought.

11The gate

The wishlist gate

The store page produces a wishlist number after sixty days. We suggest you set the threshold now, before the outcome is known.

Polden Publishing, a publisher in this genre, runs a marketing test before signing and publishes 30,000 wishlists from that test as its signing threshold. They state 150,000 dollars as their average investment per title and report more than a million wishlists generated before production decisions across their portfolio.

12Step two

Build to Steam Beta

Six months answering three questions in order: does the loop survive contact with a whole house, can the destruction be synchronised over a network, and can bots carry the game when six people are not online. The end state is a Steam beta you can build a community around.
