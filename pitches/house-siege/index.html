02The market

The most capital efficient genre on Steam right now is called friendslop. Short rounds, low price, built to be clipped and shared. House Siege has the one property that actually decides outcomes there: a hook that works on a muted autoplaying video with no explanation.

03The scale

One decision determines whether this is buildable

House Siege as your material describes it, with 4v4 or 5v5, full structural physics, six house archetypes, progression, matchmaking, backend, cosmetics and a console roadmap, is a 15 to 25 million krona project over roughly three years. That is not a criticism of the idea. It is what that level of ambition costs, whoever builds it.

The same game scaled to 3v3, one house, chunk based destruction and six minute rounds reaches a playable beta in ten months for under three million.

Scaling down is not lowering the ambition. It is putting the evidence in front of the money.

04Core loop

The house is its own building material

Your material asks exactly the right question: is building and repairing as much fun as destroying? We went looking for a game that answers yes, and we cannot find one. Teardown, Brick Rigs, Besiege and Instruments of Destruction are all pure destruction. Nobody has managed to make repair the fun half.

So we propose that the defender's verb becomes destructive too.

Defenders have no build resources and no menu to place things from. Everything they board a gap with has to be ripped out of the house first. You need to block the front door, there is no pile of planks, there is a staircase. You tear out the staircase, drag it over and slam it against the door.

We call it salvage. Furniture is free salvage: sofas, doors, bathtubs, fridges, the piano. Once the loose furniture is gone, the only thing left to salvage is load bearing structure, and taking that lowers your own Integrity. So the house you are defending is also the stockpile you are defending it with.

You tear the house down to hold it up.

That does three things at once. Defending becomes as physical and as funny as attacking, with nobody standing still holding a button. Every barricade costs something, so late in a round you are trading structure for time. And the map is reshaped by the defence as well as the attack: you tore out the staircase, so now you cannot get upstairs either.

The loop, fifteen to twenty five seconds

GRAB
Rip something loose. A door, a bathtub, a fridge, a load bearing beam.
HAUL
Carry it. Heavy, awkward, you are slow and you look stupid.
SLAM
Put it down hard. Defenders board a gap. Attackers use the same object as a ram or a ramp.
BREAK
Someone destroys what you just placed. The loop starts again.

Both teams use the same four verbs. That is a design choice, but it is also a budget choice: one interaction model instead of two halves the cost of the prototype and makes balance easier to hold over time.

05Combat

You fight with the house, not with guns

Neither your material nor our first draft was clear about what happens when two players meet in a corridor, and it is the most important unanswered question in the design. Here is our answer, and it is also the single biggest cost decision in the project.

Nobody carries a weapon. Nobody dies. You never kill anyone, you inconvenience them spectacularly.

Players get staggered, knocked down or buried for ten to fifteen seconds, then dug out by a teammate or they recover on their own. No health bars, no respawn screens, no kill feed, no scoreboard of kills. The only number on screen is House Integrity.

Shove

You have hands. Push someone off a ledge, through the hole you just made in the floor, or into the path of something heavy that is already falling.

Swing what you carry

You are already hauling a bathtub. It is a weapon by physics, not by design. No separate combat animation set, no weapon art, no balance table.

Drop your load

Release the fridge on whoever is underneath you. The most satisfying thing in the game costs us nothing to build because the object is already simulated.

Cut the support

Bring the floor down while three of them are standing on it. Attackers do this to break in. Defenders do this to bury the people who just broke in.

Rig it

Defenders can rig a barricade so it collapses when touched. A wall that looks solid and is not is the best trap in a game about structures.

Bait the collapse

The whole house is telegraphing what is about to fall. Standing somewhere so the other team follows you under it is the highest skill play available.

Why not weapons

Your material lists demolition tools, explosives, rockets and fighting room by room. Read plainly, that describes a shooter. If House Siege is a shooter, three things follow immediately. The budget roughly doubles, because weapons mean gunfeel, ballistics, hit registration, weapon art, animation sets, a damage model and a permanent balance job. The comparison set stops being the games on the previous page and becomes Rainbow Six Siege, which has had a decade and a AAA team. And the audience changes from people who want to laugh with their friends to people who want to win, which is a harder audience to reach and a much less forgiving one.

Physics only combat costs close to nothing extra, because every object is already simulated for the destruction system. It is funnier, it clips better, and as far as we can tell nobody else is doing it.

06Skill

How good can a player get?

This needs answering before anyone writes a line of code, because it decides the controls, the pace, the animation budget and how much live balancing the game will need forever. There are two honest options and they lead to different companies.

Execution skill
The Fortnite model

Building is fast and precise. Top players place structures in fractions of a second, and the ceiling is in hand speed and muscle memory. Mastery looks spectacular and creates a competitive scene.

What it costs: a very high ceiling only stays fun with constant balancing, and it needs a large population to matchmake fairly. In a 3v3 game one practised player simply beats everyone. A friend who joins in month six has a miserable evening.

Requires a permanent balance and matchmaking effort we would be selling you a fantasy about at this budget.
Knowledge skill
Recommended

Carrying is slow and awkward on purpose. One object at a time. No build menu, no hotkeys, no inventory. You physically cannot build fast, so nobody can out click you.

The ceiling is in reading the house. Knowing which wall is load bearing. Knowing that pulling the staircase cuts the attackers' route but also your own. Knowing when a collapse is two hits away and standing so the other team follows you under it.

Mastery is in where and when, not in how fast. That ceiling needs no live balancing team.
The best player in the room should win because they knew the house, not because they clicked faster.

We recommend the second one for three reasons. Friends of mixed ability have to be able to play together, and that is the entire premise of this genre rather than a nice extra. Slow and heavy is funnier than fast and precise, and funny is the marketing. And a knowledge ceiling deepens through content, meaning new houses, rather than through patches, which means the game gets better as you add to it instead of needing a team to keep it alive.

This is a reversible decision only until step two starts. After that it is in the controls, the animation and the netcode, and changing it is a rebuild.

07The risks

What the plan has to answer

These five things decide whether House Siege becomes a game. We put them up front, because the whole plan below is built to answer them in the right order and at the lowest possible cost.

Defending has to be fun
The biggest risk, and the one salvage and physics combat are our answer to. Step zero exists purely to test it, for 180,000 kronor, before anything else is committed.
Destruction plus multiplayer
The most expensive combination there is at this project size. Teardown has no multiplayer and that is no accident. The answer is to synchronise damage events rather than physics state, meaning we only send which pieces broke and let the debris fly slightly differently for each player. That is standard practice and it makes the problem tractable.
PvP needs people
A game for six players needs six players. That cold start problem is what sinks most smaller PvP titles. This is why bots are not an extra feature in this plan. They are part of the product and they get their own budgeted milestone.
Balance is a running cost
Asymmetric games need tuning for years. Rainbow Six Siege has been at it for a decade. That argues for fewer tools and clearer roles, for switching sides every round, and for the knowledge ceiling in the previous section rather than the execution one.
Price decides the game
Your material describes a premium title. The genre House Siege fits sits at six to eight dollars. Those are two different products with two different budgets, and the choice needs making early because everything else follows from it. We recommend the lower price and the larger audience.
08Art direction

Flatpack

The house should visibly be built from parts that click together, like flat pack furniture. That makes destruction readable, because you can see exactly which piece came loose, and it makes production cheap, because one kit covers six houses.

The art should look like a person made it quickly, not like a machine made it perfectly.

That is a commercial position rather than an aesthetic one. This audience recognises over polished marketing material instantly and reads it as a sign that there is no game behind it. Rough but coherent reads the opposite way: somebody is actually building something and is in on the joke. Every one of the best selling titles in the tables above looks deliberately simple.

References, and what we take from each

Colour instead of detail

A piece goes from intact to cracked to gone through colour, not texture detail. Readable at small sizes and dramatically cheaper to produce.

One costume slot per character

Blank figures with one swappable layer. That opens a route to cosmetics later without building character art now.

Fixed wide camera

You need to see the whole house fall. Clippability beats immersion, because the clip is the marketing.

Images above are the reference titles on Steam, shown to fix the target. They are not House Siege artwork. Concept art in this direction is the first deliverable of step one, and we would produce it as real renders from the actual build rather than as generated images, for the reason in the pull quote.

09Step zero

One month to find out if it is fun

Before the store page, before the art, before anything else, we want to build the loop in grey boxes and play it. One month, one programmer, a designer at half time, and nothing else.

What gets built

One room made of grey cubes. Salvage, carry, slam, break. Shove and drop. One wall that can be broken into pieces and one support that can be cut. Local co-op on one machine or two on a LAN. Nothing else at all.

What does not get built

No art, no characters, no menus, no netcode, no bots, no matchmaking, no progression, no sound beyond placeholders. Anything that is not the question gets cut.

If carrying a bathtub across a room and slamming it into a doorway is not funny in grey boxes, no amount of art will save it.

This is the cheapest honest answer to the only question that matters, and it is a question no market data and no pitch deck can answer for you. It also gives us something you can sit down and play rather than a document you have to take on trust.

10Step one

The store page, before the game

The goal is a published Steam page, a trailer that looks like gameplay, and around thirty vertical clips for TikTok, Reels and Shorts. Without building the full game.

The method is a trailer build. Collapses are authored as baked simulations and played back as animation, so we need no destruction engine in this step. The house is built on a purchased modular kit that we give its own identity through a custom shader, a locked palette and an original character silhouette. That is the part that stops the material looking bought, and it is the one part we do not cut.

11The gate

The number that decides step two

The entire point of building the store page before the game is that it produces a number which makes the next decision easy. We suggest you commit to the number in advance, before you know the outcome.

The benchmark comes from outside. Polden Publishing is a publisher specialising in exactly this genre and working from data: they run a marketing test before signing anything, and their published threshold for signing a game is 30,000 wishlists from that test. They state 150,000 dollars as their average investment per title and report more than a million wishlists generated before production decisions across their portfolio.

One important nuance. 30,000 wishlists is a threshold for continuing to invest, not for making money. At normal thirty day conversion it corresponds to roughly 11,400 copies sold, which pays for nothing. That number means the concept carries. For the whole plan to work we want to see around 150,000 wishlists at launch, which is entirely normal in this genre if step two delivers a beta people want to clip.

12Step two

The build, and the road to Steam Beta

Six months answering three questions in order: does the loop survive contact with a whole house, can the destruction be synchronised over a network, and can bots carry the game when six people are not online. The end state is a Steam beta you can build a community around.

14Next steps

Three things, then we can start

01

One game at a time

Your material runs three concepts in parallel. We suggest House Siege runs alone until step zero has been played. The other two lose nothing by waiting a month.

02

An entity and a clear IP owner

Because part of our fee sits on the back end, the agreement needs a clear counterparty and clear ownership. It is a formality, but it should be in place before we start.

03

One creative decision maker

You want to be actively involved creatively, which is good and something we work with often. It works when one person has the final word and the agreement says who.

Proposed meeting

An hour where we walk the core loop and the combat together, settle the skill question in section 06, and look at the reference games in practice. If it lands, we can set a start date for step zero and have something playable in your hands a month later.
