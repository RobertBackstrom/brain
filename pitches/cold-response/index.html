The flight model

The part nobody else got right

Flight dynamics are where a training simulator either transfers to the real aircraft or does not. This one was written by an FPV pilot who builds his own airframes, which is why experienced pilots recognise the feel within seconds.

  • Each motor is simulated on its own. Thrust is resolved per motor against air density and wind, so asymmetric thrust, prop wash and the behaviour of a damaged or unbalanced airframe fall out of the physics instead of being faked.
  • Real tuning, not a difficulty slider. Transmitter input passes through the pilot's chosen rate curve to produce degrees per second, which an advanced PID controller modelled on Betaflight and Cleanflight then matches. All common tuning rate curves are supported and PID values are adjustable.
  • Your own radio. Full transmitter support through the standard OS APIs, alongside gamepad and mouse and keyboard, including the menus. A pilot brings the controller they already fly and the configuration they already trust.
  • Wind, air density and foliage. The aircraft is flown through a simulated medium in a simulated forest, not through empty space with a wind value applied.
  • Radio signal disturbance. Video and control degradation modelled, which is the failure mode that actually ends real flights.
Modularity

Build the airframe, then fly it

Drones are composed from parts rather than picked from a list. Motors, frames, upgrades and payloads combine into the aircraft you want, and the physics responds to what you built: mass, thrust and balance change the way it flies.

For a training customer this is the whole argument. A configuration that exists in the real world can be rebuilt in the engine and flown against the same conditions, whether that is the airframe your own unit operates or the one being flown at you. A simulator with a fixed aircraft teaches one aircraft. This teaches the pilot.

Payloads already run from shotguns and grenades to recoilless weapons, including the Swedish Pansarskott m/86. Roles already exist for reconnaissance, one-way attack and heavier assault, each with its own loadout.

Terminal effects

Ballistics that know what they hit

Ballistics are multithreaded and resolved properly. Every physical surface carries metadata describing the material and its properties, so when a projectile begins penetration a headless simulation calculates the interaction.

A hard material at a steep angle is likely to ricochet. A softer one may spall, fragment, or let the projectile through while shedding a realistic amount of kinetic energy. Damage to vehicles, personnel and equipment is calculated against VPAM, the international armour rating standard. Calibre and distance matter, which means an effects assessment in the simulator means something outside it.

The world

400 square kilometres of Sweden, and a full year of it

The environment is not a backdrop. It is the variable that decides whether a flight succeeds, and it is the thing existing simulators reproduce worst.

  • Real terrain. The landscape is derived from Swedish GIS data. The first world is a 400 000 000 square metre playable area, populated by a bespoke procedural generation system that uses a grammar-based approach so that artistic control is retained rather than surrendered to noise.
  • A simulated year. The world runs a full year at an accelerated rate. Seasons, weather and the day and night cycle are continuous, and plant life and wildlife change with them. Flying the same route in February and in July are two different problems.
  • Materially destructible. Almost every object is either destructible or affected by the physics simulation. The environment changes as the exercise proceeds, so the second run through a position is not the first one repeated.
  • Ground vehicles too. A wheeled and tracked physics framework with multi-role crewed vehicles, fuel and maintenance, and a persistent global landscape deformer that leaves tracks which then affect handling.
Systems

What is running underneath

Audio

Proprietary audio middleware

Written in house to simulate the speed of sound, air absorption and frequency shift. Distance and direction are audible, which matters for a pilot learning to interpret what they are hearing as well as what they are seeing.

Engines

Combustion engine simulation

An open-source combustion engine simulator implemented as a multithreaded actor component, driving both audio and vehicle physics from the same source of truth rather than from a sound file.

Opposition

Goal-oriented action planning

Non-player behaviour runs on a proprietary GOAP architecture, so opposition adapts and works around problems. Behaviour shifts with equipment, weather, season, fatigue, physical and mental state, and time of day. Threat models, not target sets.

Networking

Iris replication, spatial filters

Custom spatial filters minimise network churn in both directions. Peer to peer for small groups, or hosted dedicated servers. Every system was built for networked play rather than retrofitted for it.

Code

C++ where it is hot, AngelScript where it is not

Performance-critical systems in C++, the majority of simulation logic in AngelScript, which ships uncompiled. That is what makes scenario and systems work possible for a customer or an integrator without handing over the engine.

Server stack

Telemetry, forensics and analytics

The dedicated server stack ships with full telemetry, forensics, administration and moderation tooling and an interactive world map, plus behaviour tracking using statistical modelling and machine learning. Most of an after-action review capability already exists here.

Already pointed this way

A training mode is being designed, with input from people who train on this

A dedicated Swedish military simulation mode is in design. Six roles can be assumed, so a session runs with a virtual instructor and trainees learning to operate drones on real radio transmitters, and support for DIS, the protocol that lets separate simulators share one exercise, is on the technical roadmap.

To be precise about its status: the direction of that mode is an ongoing design discussion with individuals connected to Hemvärnet, the Home Guard. It is not a formal collaboration, a procurement, or an endorsement by the Swedish Armed Forces, and we would not present it as one. What it does mean is that the design is being shaped by people who actually train drone operators, rather than by developers guessing at requirements.

The developer is also explicit about where the gap is. On the military revenue model their own material says, in as many words, that they are seeking investors with proven expertise in defence-tech monetisation and growth, because that experience is currently limited internally. That is precisely the hole this conversation fills.

The structure

How it would be put together

01

A new company licenses the technology

A purpose-built Swedish company takes a licence from Eternal Minds AB for the defence, security and professional training field. The consumer game stays with Eternal Minds, so the two roadmaps do not fight each other.

02

Part of the price is a co-production

Cash, plus a co-production of the consumer game. That keeps the engineer who built this engaged and building rather than cashed out, and keeps the flight model under the most unforgiving testers there are.

03

Investment funds the product

Into the operator layer, the compliance work and the first deployments. Not into proving the simulation, which is playable today and can be demonstrated live on request.

Three things that are open, stated plainly

  • It is not certified for military training. Certification, where it applies, is a workstream with a cost and a calendar.
  • The export-control classification is being established in writing rather than assumed. It affects the structure, so it is being handled before anything is sold.
  • The procurement timeline is the real risk, not the technology. Which door we go through changes the answer by quarters, and your side may well know more about that than ours.
