01The Mission

What the job actually is

The game mode targets the PUBG audience, so anything that requires prior PAYDAY knowledge is a cost, not a feature. Everything below follows from that.

The job is three new heists, the four existing Irons 1 stages brought up to the same standard, and one consistent experience across all seven.

Three leading thoughts

01

Accessible PAYDAY for the PUBG audience

These are PUBG players first, and they want a mode they can read immediately. Recreating PAYDAY 3's complexity inside PUBG does not pay for itself, so we simplify heist structures, objectives and stealth mechanics while keeping the core PAYDAY identity. Intuitive systems, clear progression, and gameplay that works for players who have never played PAYDAY.

02

Fast-paced, varied and re-playable heists

Shorter, action-packed experiences with strong visual and gameplay variation across environments, weapons, combat, stealth, enemies and objectives. Re-playability through progression, loadouts, weapon tuning and meaningful power growth, and polish where it is felt most: gunplay, weapon feel and moment-to-moment feedback.

03

Evolve the whole experience, not just the new heists

New stealth mechanics, progression, weapon balancing, difficulty and the Shield enemy work across both the new and the existing heists. One consistent experience rather than separate pieces of content, and one that sits inside PUBG rather than beside it: design language, progression and UX that PUBG players already recognise.

How we hold that direction: iterative level design. Prototypes early and often, put in front of target-audience players and played inside PUBG rather than in isolation, so we can verify both the flow and how the mode feels next to the rest of the game while there is still time to act on the answer.

Level design

Variation

The heists offer visual variation, multiple vistas and environments. We keep that when we port them. Where we cut gameplay or size, we keep at least part of each distinct environment intact.

Simplify

More linear structure and heist objectives. Remove complex optional objectives where needed and adjust levels accordingly, including degrading loot and loot bag scanning. Reduce map size where needed. Add new stealth mechanics but keep them simple, and keep the levels enjoyable for players who do not want to play stealth.

Culturalisation and content compliance

Culturalisation is part of the level work, not a compliance pass at the end. The categories that actually move a blockout are civilian presence, police and law enforcement as targets, how theft and real-world currency are depicted, and blood and gore, and they differ by market and by platform. The cheapest answer is structural: we shape the space so the restricted content is never needed, rather than stripping it out late and leaving a level that reads as missing something. The definitive list is confirmed with Krafton's market approvals early, and the blockouts are planned against it from day one rather than discovered at cert. It is one of the reasons the three heists are restructured rather than ported, and it is priced into the level design and scripting load.

Fast pace

We keep the heists short and action packed, cut or simplify the slower segments, and hold the gameplay loop tight so the tempo between heists and progression rewards carries and long loss states are avoided. New and existing weapons are tuned closer to the PAYDAY experience, with solid power spikes on unlock.

Stealth mechanics

Stealth grows through simple stand-alone features that add level variation, rather than complex general systems that are hard to read: lockpicking and safe cracking in the PD3 style, hacking to temporarily disable individual cameras, motion and floor sensors used sparingly, QR codes simplified so no randomly spawned phones are needed, security lasers with fuse boxes or wall terminals, silencer mods (default on side arms, unlocked on primaries), and stealth skills and tools such as throwing knives and stealth variables on armour and skills.

Difficulty and challenge

The new difficulty tier is what keeps the mode worth returning to once players have learned the heists. Replay value falls away as soon as the routes are known, and the progression rework makes players measurably stronger over the run, so the challenge has to scale with them or the later hours flatten out. We build that increase out of encounter and behaviour design: tighter guard and camera coverage, the Shield enemy used as mobile cover, less forgiving stealth detection and fewer second chances. Not out of stat inflation that turns enemies into bullet sponges, which reads as unfair rather than hard. It is designed alongside the progression and weapon tuning rather than bolted on afterwards, so the tier is balanced against what players have actually unlocked by the time they reach it.

Enemy design

One new enemy type, the Shield enemy. The work is less the enemy itself and more implementing it well across both new and existing levels, and updating existing enemy behaviour to use the shield as mobile cover in combat.

The existing four levels

Revisited and updated with the new enemy and AI behaviour, the new stealth mechanics, the new difficulty, rebalancing against the new progression and weapon tuning, and tutorial and HUD elements as needed.

The new heists

Three new heists, alongside the four existing Irons 1 stages upgraded.

99 Boxes (or Touch the Sky). A large map blending outdoor and indoor environments around shipping containers and warehouse storage, patrolling guards and cameras with civilians spread out. We keep the smooth indoor/outdoor transition, rebalance for PUBG players, focus on avoiding cameras and guards, and remove civilians.

Syntax Error. Large, with multiple visual styles and vistas: busy streets, parks, server rooms, futuristic interiors. The street section is built around a civilian crowd, and the mode cannot let players kill civilians, so we cut that section rather than ship a street that is empty for no reason a player can read. The remaining variation is kept while we shorten the heist and reduce the overall size.

Under the Surphaze. Compact, stacked on itself across multiple floors with several entry options, heavy on stealth and on gathering information to locate objectives while bypassing motion detectors and lasers. We keep the stealth but make the flow more linear and clearer, simplify the security puzzles, group the information so there is less back and forth, and replace civilians with cameras, patrolling guards and level mechanics. Mechanics: QR code scans, motion scanners, laser traps.

02The team

One delivery team

One team with a single line of accountability, not a set of individually placed contractors. Named seniors in every key position, the large majority of them on site in Stockholm.

Senior leadership

Proven experience leading and driving complex projects from start to finish, with people who have shipped and operated live titles at this scale.

PAYDAY and PUBG experience in the room

Several of the people named here worked on the first PAYDAY mode in PUBG. The code base, the pipeline and the constraints are known going in, not discovered in month three.

AAA depth, small-team discipline

The team spans AAA production depth and the cost discipline of small teams, which is what a mode of this size needs: senior judgement without a AAA overhead.

On site in Stockholm

More than twenty of the twenty-five at peak sit inside the Starbreeze organisation in Stockholm, so design questions are answered in the room and the mode is reviewed against the franchise every week. The exceptions are named and few: two artists in the UK and India, one programmer in Umeå, and part of QA, all in roles where daily presence adds nothing.

Test driven development

Builds in front of real PUBG players from Pre-Alpha and monthly after that, backed by frequent in-house testing and embedded QA. A QA lead and three testers sit inside the team and the budget.

04Project & delivery plan

Twelve months, nine milestones

The start month is open. Krafton indicated a later start is workable as long as the 12-month development window and the support period after it stay unchanged. Month 0 is signature, and the twelve development months run from there.

The team is Swedish, so a twelve month plan contains a Swedish summer, and this plan is costed with that already in it. The answer is not to move the milestones around July. It is that the lead positions overlap rather than sit single-threaded, so a lead taking their holiday does not stall the workstream behind them, and the milestone dates hold.

The dependency that sets this order. The new heists cannot be blocked out to their final structure until the stealth mechanics are defined, because the mechanics decide the routes, the sightlines and the objective placement. That is why stealth sits in the Prototype milestone in month 2 rather than in Pre-Alpha, and why level design and stealth design run as one workstream from month 2 onwards. Retrofitting stealth into finished blockouts is the rework we are designing this plan to avoid.

06Ways of working

How the project runs

Embedded, not outsourced

The team works inside the Starbreeze organisation in Stockholm rather than as a separate studio delivering against a spec. That is the whole point of the setup: collaboration, communication and franchise review stay close, and the mode is built where the PAYDAY knowledge already sits.

One accountable team

A senior core accountable for the whole delivery, not a set of individually placed contractors. Single point of contact, single point of escalation and risk management.

Full ownership

We take end-to-end ownership of the work required to deliver against the project's creative and technical goals, and that includes validating the design rather than assuming it. Playtests with target-audience PUBG players from Pre-Alpha, findings written into the milestone deliverables, plus frequent in-house testing and embedded QA.

Risk and mitigation
Ramp to 25 inside four months
The team is already in place and ready to go. If production needs change, we have quick access to senior specialists who can be brought in as needed.
Manual scripting load from the port
Irons 1 showed that recreating levels in the PUBG engine is scripting-heavy. The level design and tech design roles are staffed early and held through Beta.
Two IP holders in the approval chain
A milestone is the wrong place to discover a dependency, because the decisions we need from Krafton land weeks before the milestone that exposes them. So: a standing weekly technical contact on the PUBG side from month 1, a named decision owner at Krafton and at Starbreeze for anything touching engine, certification or the reward flows, and a written decision log carrying the date each answer is needed by. The milestone calendar is then set against Krafton's submission date and worked backwards.
Communication and infrastructure across time zones
The mode is built inside a live codebase owned by a team in Seoul, seven to eight hours ahead of Stockholm and working primarily in Korean. We plan for that rather than around it. The daily overlap, Stockholm mornings against Seoul afternoons, is held as a standing slot for engine and integration questions, everything else moves asynchronously against the written decision log, and technical sessions of any depth are run with interpretation arranged in advance rather than improvised in the room. Source, build and sync infrastructure across both sites is stood up in month 1, because transfer and iteration times on a codebase this size are a schedule item and not an IT detail.
Scope growth
Every milestone carries a named deliverable list. Additions are re-planned in the open, with a number attached.
