01The game

Game description

Disposable Corps is a low poly first and third person shooter set in the First World War, with steampunk mechs, tanks, artillery, destructible terrain and digging. Rounds cycle Prepare, Defense and Attack, with the two teams on opposite phases. Players earn money and spend it on gear, on fortifications, and on AI soldiers they recruit and then command, so a 5v5 match already puts far more than ten bodies on the field.

The demo is free and live. Start with the tutorial. That is where the UX problems are.

What is in the build today

The round

Phases cycle Prepare, Defense and Attack, the two teams always on opposite phases. Flags are capture points. Each flag has a white ground circle that becomes your team's defense zone once captured. Waypoints are marked with coloured smoke.

The economy

Money from kills and objectives, deducted for team kills. Three sinks: gear, fortifications (sandbags, gun nests, spawn points, digging, and since August 2025 a buildable factory that produces tanks), and AI soldiers bought at spawn icons on the map.

The squad layer

The player recruits AI soldiers and commands them from a menu. NPCs also assist with building since the December refactor, and the AI was reworked to handle more units and more complex tasks. This exists already, and it is on the fault list because it works badly.

Combat and hosting

First and third person, WW1 arsenal, tanks with armour deformation, track damage and ammo racks, heavy artillery with gunner seats, destructible terrain. A server browser exists and January 2026 added a host region filter. Steam lists LAN PvP and LAN Co-op.

03The plan

The problems to solve

The build today is four games stacked: a shooter, a squad RTS, a base builder and a vehicle sim, spread over a map larger than any of them needs. Below is what we think has to be solved in the first phase. How it gets solved is the evaluation pass's call.

Scope
Four systems, one session

The shooting, the squad layer, the building and the vehicles all compete for the same twenty minutes. Nothing in the match tells a player which one it is about.

Readability
No bounded objective

The map is larger than ten players fill. No front line, no objective marker, no clock. The publisher's fault list says players do not understand what they are meant to do.

The squad layer
Bought, then unread

Players buy AI soldiers and command them, and the HUD does not show how many they have, where those soldiers are or what they are doing. Command is on single keys with no on screen affordance.

What we would park until after launch

Money economy and tank factories Nation roster beyond two factions Deep armour simulation LAN as a selling point The tutorial as a separate level
One shape we have sketched, not a decision

Written before anyone on our side has had the current build, the repository or any telemetry. It is here to show the size of rescope we have costed, not to fix the design. The evaluation pass can replace all of it.

90 seconds
Dig in

The defender fortifies one bounded sector: fixed dig points, barricades, gun nests. The attacker picks an approach and support.

5 to 7 minutes
Over the top

One objective, a single trench line, clearly marked, on a visible clock. Each player commands four to six AI soldiers, so 5v5 fills a front.

60 seconds
The line moves

If the line falls the front shifts to the next sector and the loop restarts with sides swapped. Three sectors is a match.

When more content gets added, and what is not in this budget

Raised here because the publisher will ask it, and the answer changes the launch date rather than the monthly number.

What the 1 860 000 covers on the content side is three maps, two factions and light progression. That is the Early Access set. Nothing beyond it is budgeted, priced or scheduled.

Our position: after Early Access, not before

  • Early Access exists to sell an unfinished game and fund the rest of it. Content built before month 10 is spend with no revenue behind it, and it moves the release date.
  • The month 8 playtest says what players actually want more of. Building it before that reading is guessing.
  • If retention at months 4 and 8 does not hold, more content does not fix it. Content on a loop players leave is spend without a return.

So: the decision point is month 8, on playtest data, in time to plan the first drop for months 11 and 12, and it gets funded from Early Access revenue rather than from this budget.

If the publisher wants content before launch instead, it is a separate number and a later release date. We would size it after the review month, when the real cost of a map on this pipeline is known rather than estimated.

04The seat

Product owner and UX, in one person

The first step, in the review month, is a UX evaluation pass on the current build, to assess what needs to be done. The seat is product owner and designer in one person. What we expect it to own: the order set, the recruit model, the cut list, the onboarding path, and the interface work listed below. Robert stays executive producer and carries the publisher and developer relationship, the schedule and the commercial.

What we have seen in the public build

  • Tutorial: at least five instruction blocks render on top of each other in the same text area, and two voice over tracks play at the same time.
  • HUD names the enemy's phase, not the player's own.
  • HUD shows a money total with no indication of what it buys.
  • No objective marker, no front line, no phase clock.
  • Buy, build, map and recruit, and squad command sit on single keys with no on screen affordance.

Sources: the public demo, the developer's playtest notes, the store screenshots and Robert's own sessions. Nobody on our side has had the current build, the repository or any telemetry.

A suggested way forward, and the designer's first task

The list below is what we would do and in what order. It is a suggestion, not a brief. The first task on the seat is to play the demo and come back on it: what is missing, what is in the wrong order, what we have sized wrong, and what we have called a fix that is really a redesign. The day figures are our own, made from the outside without the build, and they are the part we most want argued with.

1
The first session

Starts with a ten second diagnostic. Open the tutorial and read the overlapping lines. Same sentence in several languages means language selection is not applied to the tutorial layer, a settings level fix. Different instructions from different steps means the step machinery never tears down, a broken sequencer. That answer sets the scope of everything after it. Then design the replacement: the plan cuts the tutorial as a separate level and teaches inside the first round of a real match.

Which phase am I in, what is the objective, where is the front, how long is left. Own phase named rather than the enemy's, one objective marker, a sector boundary read, a phase clock, and team identification on soldiers. One sector at a time means one objective and one clock to show.

Opens only in the phase where it makes sense, shows what the money buys then, closes. The December patch notes dropped the separate buy key, so part of this may already exist.

Map and recruit

The map screen doubles as the place you buy AI soldiers, mid match. Recruitment is where the squad layer starts. Scoped as a fix in phase 1, redesign deferred.

4 to 5 days

Sizing. The list is roughly 45 days at the optimistic end and 55 at the pessimistic, against about 40 days available in phase 1 at 60 % of full time. It assumes the designer is in the build during the review month, that UI implementation sits with the development team, and that map and recruit stays scoped as a fix. Those three assumptions and the gap between 55 and 40 are what the first pass has to settle. Front loaded fits the curve better than flat.

Icon and HUD asset production is not designer work, and until now it had no home in this budget. Two months of UI artist are now reserved inside the design seat, months 6 to 9. The split is in the commercial, slide 06.

Fix versus redesign, and what the evaluation pass needs

Fixes, closable from a written spec

The tutorial root cause if the diagnostic lands the cheap way. Phase text naming your own phase. Labelling or removing the unlabeled blue and orange bar. Team identification. Hiding money outside spendable phases. Debug overlays and cursor lock out of release builds. The placeholder lobby name and the blank player portraits. Roughly a fifth of the effort and most of the visible improvement in month one.

Redesigns, a designer owns the pass

In round guidance, the round state HUD, the three order radial and squad readout, the phase gated panel, and the map and recruit model if a fix is not enough. The closed playtest at the end of phase 1 is the go or no go, and it runs on whatever HUD is in the build by then.

What we cannot answer yet, and would get you in week one

  • The current playtest build. The public demo is the August 2025 one.
  • Fifteen minutes of screen capture of the command menu, the map and recruit screen, the build panel and the lobby. No public image shows any of them.
  • Engine and UI framework, and whether UI lives in prefabs, data or code.
  • Any telemetry from the two public playtests: session length, tutorial completion, quit points.
  • What the blue and orange bar on the HUD means.

Seven of the eight store screenshots have the HUD hidden. The one that does not shows a ping readout, an FPS counter, an unlabeled bar, the words "Enemy prepare phase", a money total, ammo and a health bar. No clock, no objective, no compass, no squad readout.

Real cost is each side's own internal cost for the person, at the rates the two studios already use between them. Over twelve months the team costs 2 250 000 SEK, the project pays 1 860 000, and 390 000 is carried.

Two months of UI artist sit inside the design seat. The seat runs at 60 % of full time through month 5, then drops to 30 % once the loop is settled and the role is mostly product ownership. That frees 2,1 months of the seat, and two of them go to a UI artist at half time across months 6 to 9, producing HUD assets and icons against the designs from phase 1. The seat's monthly cost to the project does not move, so the total above is unchanged. An artist's rate is not a designer's, so the exact number of weeks gets set in the review month. If it comes out short it is fewer artist weeks, not a larger number.

The network work is merged into the developer seat rather than sitting on its own line. Moving the game off dedicated servers and onto player hosting is the one technical promise the plan makes, a server browser and host region filter already exist, and it is the same person's job as the gameplay work.

The developer's engineer has asked for that direction at the level of naming the change rather than describing it. Nobody on our side can answer at that level without the repository, so the technical direction is a written deliverable of the review month, in those terms, and the code assessment is the first thing the developer seat does.

Risk and mitigation
Nobody has read the code
No repository access, engine and version unconfirmed, netcode state unknown. Every estimate here is a range until the review month closes.
The population problem
A 5v5 game needs matches to fill. Bots first is the mitigation and the systems already exist.
Seven months of silence
Wishlists decay. Part of the marketing spend in months six to nine recovers ground rather than gaining it, and the launch is effectively a relaunch of a page carrying a mixed demo score. A new demo app under the rescope resets that.
The working model has no sanction
We own the product decisions and the schedule, their coder owns the implementation. It holds as long as he delivers against agreed deadlines. The review month tests that at small scale.
A China weighted audience
Two thirds of the demo's reviews are in Chinese, which sets the price assumption and takes a real bite out of net per unit. A Western channel is a condition.
No artist in the budget
The earlier shape carried a half time artist. It does not any more, so UI asset and icon production has to come off the developer side or out of the design seat's own hours. Worth pricing in the review month.
