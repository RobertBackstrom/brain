Not a rebrand. The game keeps its name, its key art, and the 94% Very Positive history it already earned. A Director's Cut is a new product and a fresh marketing moment - new store beat, new trailer, new wishlists - without throwing away the brand and community already in place.

Same name, same art, same review legacy. New buyers see a proven game, not an unknown. No equity is left on the table.

The Game

You start with one hero. Soon you have a dragon problem.

Tears of Adria is a real-time strategy adventure with autobattles. You begin with a single hero. Soon you have a party, a few questionable artifacts, and far too much confidence. Somehow this is the correct way to resurrect a dead goddess.

Old-school RPG bones - orcs, dwarves, elves, undead, all with their own rivalries - wrapped around a 20×20 grid where positioning, gear, and skill interactions decide every fight. The game doesn't bother explaining most of it.

"Tears of Adria doesn't care where you go, or why. What it can't stand is weaklings."

Your main character is locked in. The rest are replaceable. Scale a party up to 80 henchmen and bring chaos to the land. Or play it small and stealthy with six Rangers. Or be a lone Necromancer commanding an undead legion. Same map, different runs.

Hidden paths. Strange encounters. Rewards with no introduction. Some things only exist if you notice them.

Gameplay

Real-time autobattle. Combat resolves in seconds, not turns.
